The proposed function will improve the quality and speed of strategic technology and investment decisions. It will provide a repeatable way to connect business strategy, investment choices, delivery initiatives, technology standards, data, security, and operational change.

4. Approve the Minimum Viable EA (MVEA) operating model and 12-month evolution roadmap at the end of the Foundation Programme.

## Why this matters now

[Client / Enterprise Name] is managing change across [business transformation, cloud, data/AI, customer experience, regulatory, technology-modernisation, M&A, cost-optimisation] priorities. These initiatives create value only when decisions are coherent across business capabilities, processes, applications, integration, data, platforms, security, and delivery.

Without an enterprise-wide architecture capability, organisations commonly experience:

- Investment decisions made with incomplete visibility of dependencies, duplicated capabilities, and lifecycle cost.
- Programmes optimised locally but misaligned across business units, product domains, or technology platforms.
- Delivery delays caused by late-stage design decisions, unclear standards, unmanaged exceptions, and integration surprises.
- Growing application, data, integration, and infrastructure complexity that increases operating cost and change risk.
- Limited traceability from strategic objectives to funded initiatives, measurable benefits, architecture decisions, and delivered outcomes.

The proposal is not to create an additional approval layer. It is to establish a lean enterprise capability that makes change **more investable, more deliverable, and easier to govern**.

---

## Our proposition

We propose an Enterprise Architecture function that acts as the organisation’s **strategic change and decision-enablement capability**.

It will:

- Translate strategy and business outcomes into a coherent portfolio of capabilities, value streams, roadmaps, and investment choices.
- Provide early, proportionate architecture engagement for major initiatives—before irreversible funding and design decisions are made.
- Establish reusable principles, standards, reference architectures, and guardrails that accelerate rather than constrain delivery.
- Make enterprise dependencies, risks, technical debt, and rationalisation opportunities visible to executives and portfolio leaders.
- Govern architectural decisions and exceptions transparently, with clear accountabilities and service-level expectations.
- Build architectural knowledge as an enterprise asset rather than recreate it programme by programme.

### Target business outcomes

| Outcome | What will change | Indicative measure |
|---|---|---|
| Better investment choices | Business cases expose capability, dependency, risk, cost, and target-state impacts before approval | % of strategic investments architecture-assessed before funding |
| Faster, safer delivery | Teams use agreed guardrails and reusable patterns early in solution shaping | Design-review lead time; rework or late-stage design issues |
| Reduced complexity and cost | Duplicated, ageing, or low-value applications and integrations are identified and prioritised | Applications/integrations retired or rationalised; run-cost avoided |
| Improved strategic alignment | Portfolio initiatives can be traced to strategic outcomes and target capabilities | % of priority initiatives mapped to capabilities and roadmaps |
| Stronger risk management | Architecture, security, resilience, data, and regulatory risks are exposed earlier | Exceptions, risk acceptance trend, and remediation closure rate |

*Final targets will be baselined during mobilisation; no benefit figures are assumed in this proposal.*

## Minimum Viable EA operating model

The function will begin lean and scale only where it demonstrates value.

### Mandate

The EA function will enable enterprise decisions and assure material change against an agreed target state. It will set direction and guardrails; it will not replace product, delivery, engineering, security, data, or operational accountability.

### Initial scope

**In scope**

- Enterprise strategy, business capabilities, major change initiatives, technology and application portfolios, data and integration dependencies, architecture principles, standards, architecture decisions, and exceptions.
- Priority initiatives above agreed thresholds for investment, risk, regulatory impact, cross-domain dependency, or strategic importance.

**Out of scope during the Foundation Programme**

- Detailed solution design owned by delivery teams.
- Comprehensive documentation of every legacy asset.
- Retrospective governance of all in-flight projects unless specifically prioritised.
- Tool-led repository deployment before the information model and working practices are proven.

### Roles and accountabilities

| Role | Accountable for |
|---|---|
| Executive sponsor | Mandate, executive alignment, barriers, and value realisation |
| Head of Enterprise Architecture | EA strategy, operating model, capability roadmap, service performance, and architecture governance |
| Enterprise architects | Cross-domain architecture, strategic roadmaps, enterprise decisions, and portfolio insight |
| Domain architects | Business, data, application, integration, technology, security, or cloud domain direction and standards |
| Solution architects | Initiative-level solution architecture and alignment with enterprise guardrails |
| Portfolio / Transformation Office | Demand intake, investment process integration, benefits tracking, and roadmap coordination |
| Architecture Steering Group | Decision making for enterprise priorities, significant architecture choices, and exceptions |

### Governance principles

- **Engage early:** Architecture engagement begins during discovery and business-case shaping, not only at design approval.
- **Be proportionate:** Review depth reflects investment, risk, complexity, and enterprise impact.
- **Decide transparently:** Decisions, assumptions, conditions, and exceptions are recorded and visible.
- **Reuse by default:** Proven standards and patterns are used unless a justified exception is approved.
- **Measure outcomes:** The function reports business and delivery outcomes, not documentation volume.

## 90-day Foundation Programme

### Phase 1 — Align and diagnose (Weeks 1–3)

**Purpose:** Establish sponsor alignment, understand the strategic context, and focus the function on the most valuable problems.

**Activities**

- Confirm mandate, sponsorship, strategic drivers, and success criteria.
- Interview key executives and leaders across strategy, portfolio, finance, security, data, technology, delivery, and operations.
- Assess current architecture practices, decision mechanisms, artefacts, tools, skills, and demand.
- Identify priority initiatives, decisions, risks, and rationalisation opportunities.
- Define the initial stakeholder map and engagement plan.

**Deliverables**

- EA current-state and capability assessment.
- Executive problem statement and value case.
- Stakeholder map and communications plan.
- Initial demand backlog and prioritised use cases.
- Draft EA principles.

### Phase 2 — Design and prove the operating model (Weeks 4–7)

**Purpose:** Co-design the Minimum Viable EA operating model and prove it on live enterprise priorities.

**Activities**

- Define mandate, services, roles, decision rights, governance forums, architecture-review triggers, and exception process.
- Create the initial business capability map and strategic alignment view.
- Establish a small set of standards and reference patterns for immediate use.
- Support [two to four] live strategic initiatives with architecture visioning and option analysis.
- Establish an architecture-decision record and minimum information model.

**Deliverables**

- EA operating model and governance charter.
- Architecture principles, review process, and exception process.
- Initial capability map and strategy-to-initiative traceability.
- Architecture Vision and decision packs for selected initiatives.
- Minimum repository / knowledge-management structure.

### Phase 3 — Mobilise and embed (Weeks 8–12)

**Purpose:** Secure adoption, create the roadmap, and transition to sustainable operation.

**Activities**

- Operate the governance cadence and refine it from feedback.
- Finalise an initial architecture roadmap, transition states, and work packages.
- Define measures, reporting, and benefits-realisation ownership.
- Confirm target team shape, skills, sourcing, training, and repository/tooling needs.
- Develop the 12-month EA evolution plan and executive decision pack.

**Deliverables**

- 12-month EA capability roadmap and implementation plan.
- Architecture governance calendar and service catalogue.
- Initial standards, reference architectures, and decision register.
- KPI dashboard and benefits measurement baseline.
- Mobilisation close-out report and recommendations for scale-up.

## Indicative 12-month roadmap

| Horizon | Focus | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|
| Months 0–3 | Establish, align, and prove value | Mandate agreed; governance operating; priority initiatives supported; initial capability map and principles in use |
| Months 3–6 | Embed in investment and delivery processes | Architecture engagement integrated into portfolio gates; standards and patterns adopted; decision/exception workflow established |
| Months 6–9 | Extend portfolio and domain coverage | Roadmaps for priority domains; application and technology lifecycle views; rationalisation opportunities prioritised |
| Months 9–12 | Optimise and scale | Benefits evidenced; maturity reassessed; service catalogue and team expanded only where justified |

## Measures of success

Success will be measured against a baseline agreed in the first three weeks. Suggested measures include:

- Percentage of strategic initiatives receiving architecture engagement before investment approval.
- Time taken from intake to an architecture decision or review outcome.
- Percentage of material architecture decisions recorded with accountable owners and review dates.
- Adoption of approved standards and reusable patterns.
- Number and value of identified application, integration, platform, or supplier rationalisation opportunities.
- Number, age, and resolution rate of architecture exceptions.
- Stakeholder satisfaction with EA usefulness, responsiveness, and clarity.
- Delivery rework, dependency issues, or risk exposures identified early enough to alter a decision.

## Investment and delivery options

The final commercial proposal will be based on confirmed scope, stakeholder availability, and priority initiatives. The following illustrates three possible models.

| Option | Description | When to select |
|---|---|---|
| A. Foundation | 90-day assessment, operating-model design, and proof on limited priority initiatives | The enterprise needs alignment and a practical starting point |
| B. Foundation plus embed | Foundation Programme plus [6–9] months of hands-on operation, coaching, and roadmap execution | The enterprise wants to build internal capability while reducing mobilisation risk |
| C. Managed EA capability | An outcome-based EA service with agreed leadership, domain expertise, governance, and reporting | The enterprise needs immediate capacity while recruiting or restructuring |

**Indicative team for the Foundation Programme:** EA lead, enterprise/business architect, technology/application/integration architect, portfolio or transformation analyst, and specialist input from security, data, finance, and delivery as required.

## Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| EA is perceived as a governance bottleneck | Use clear intake thresholds, time-boxed reviews, measurable service levels, and early delivery value |
| Mandate is unclear or sponsorship is fragmented | Confirm executive sponsor, decision rights, scope, and escalation path in Week 1 |
| Too much focus on documentation or tooling | Prioritise live decisions, roadmaps, reusable patterns, and outcome measures; introduce tooling incrementally |
| Delivery teams see EA as external to delivery | Include delivery, product, engineering, security, data, and operations leaders in design and governance |
| Benefits are difficult to evidence | Baseline a small number of agreed metrics and track decisions, avoided risk, and rationalisation opportunities |
