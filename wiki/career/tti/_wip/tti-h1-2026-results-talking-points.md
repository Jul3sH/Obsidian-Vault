# TTI H1 2026 Results — Talking Points

## What this is, and why it exists

**What it is:** the financial facts from TTI's H1 2026 results announcement that Julian can use in conversation with Ty Staviski or Kari, ranked by how much they help the enterprise-architecture pitch — plus the things that look useful but are traps.

**Why it was created (8 Aug 2026):** TTI announced record half-year results on 30 June 2026, and the people Julian is talking to spent the preceding weeks producing them. Knowing the numbers cold is the cheapest available credibility, and one of them points directly at the cost line his proposal targets.

**How it is used:** Julian only, before the Ty call on Mon 10 Aug. **Everything here is PUBLIC**, so unlike [[tti-ea-requirements]]'s INTERNAL material, all of it is safe to reference without explaining how he knows it. Pick two or three; do not recite. **⚠ Two different sources, added 9 Aug — don't conflate them:** the H1 2026 numbers (items 1-6) are from `TTI IR announcement 2026-06-30`; talking point 7 (Steve's quote) is from the earlier, separate [[tti-2025-annual-results-script]] (AR25 annual call). Different events, months apart.

## ⭐ The one finding that matters: SG&A is eating the margin gains

This is the sharpest thing in the announcement for Julian's pitch, and it is stated in TTI's own words.

| | H1 2026 | Movement |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 42.9% (record) | **+258 bps** |
| SG&A as % of sales | 33.0% | **+173 bps** |
| EBIT margin | 9.9% (record) | **+86 bps** |

**Roughly two-thirds of a record gross-margin gain was consumed by SG&A growth.** The operational teams delivered 258 basis points; 86 reached EBIT.

TTI attributes the SG&A rise to *"continued investment in new product development, field resources, increased commercialization activities and write-offs of intangible assets related to the rationalization of underperforming categories"* — i.e. mostly deliberate growth investment, not waste. **Do not imply it is waste.** But the arithmetic still stands, and it is exactly the line Ty's structural-cost agenda targets.

**Why this is the strongest hook available:** Ty's stated lever is taking out *structural corporate admin and G&A cost*. That line is currently rising as a share of sales. Julian's proposal targets structural cost that comes out once and stays out. The pitch lands on the live pressure point rather than a generic one.

> **Say it like this:** "The thing that struck me in the half-year numbers was the gross margin — 258 basis points is a serious operational result. What's interesting is that 86 of it reached EBIT, because SG&A moved 173. Most of that looks like deliberate investment. But it does mean the structural side of that cost base is where the next margin point has to come from, and that's the part architecture actually touches."

## Talking points, ranked

**1. The EBIT target is nearly met — so what's the next lever?**
*"After delivering a 9.9% EBIT margin in the first six months of 2026, we have an increased level of confidence in our ability to meet or exceed our internal target of 10.0% EBIT margin by 2027, with further upside in 2028 and beyond."*
The 10% is essentially in hand. The interesting question is what produces the upside *after* it, and structural cost is the honest answer. **This also publicly confirms the 2027 date**, which until now was only in a summary note.

**2. The free cash flow target was raised — from over US$1.0bn to over US$1.3bn for 2026.**
A 30% increase in a target, mid-year. Cash generation is the priority, and it says capital discipline is high right now. **The direct architecture link:** technology capex avoided through governance supports this target too, not only EBIT. Two mechanisms, not one vague "architectural lever": a region that reuses a group standard instead of building its own never spends the capital, and a design checked against the standard before it's built doesn't need rebuilding after. Both are capex that comes out once and stays out. Full argument: [[tti-ea-governance-value]] §0b.

**7. Steve's own framing points straight at the pitch — but it's from the AR25 annual call, ⚠ not H1 2026, do not conflate the two events:**
*"How can we leverage our negotiating costs on IT? How can we do the things globally to be able to free up more cash?"* (full quote also names "leverage our back office"; drop that clause, it adds nothing). **Cost management frameworks like FinOps and TBM enable informed trade-off conversations between IT and finance. Architecture frameworks like TOGAF ensure adoption of global standards.** Two distinct framework types, each answering one of Steve's two questions. This is the strongest available bridge from TTI's own words to the pitch, and the pattern to reuse generally: state the goal in their words, then name the architecture response. Logged as P3 in [[tti-ea-requirements]]. Now built into [[tti-ty-engagement-proposal]] and [[tti-ty-call-script]].

**3. A US$500m buyback started in June** (US$42m done by end July).
Relevant in a way worth thinking about before saying: when a company is returning capital, every dollar of new spend competes with the buyback. **This is an argument for the fee being small and the hurdle being low, not for the value being big.** US$320,644 against a US$500m repurchase programme is a rounding error — which is exactly the framing the breakeven hurdle in the proposal already uses.

**4. Capex includes "automation and productivity initiatives around the globe"**, and capacity expansions in **Vietnam and the Americas over the next 12-18 months**.
New sites mean new technology footprint, integration decisions, and standards to conform to — a genuine, concrete architecture hook that is forward-looking rather than remedial.

**5. Net interest expense down 29.4%**, credited to *"efficient management of financial resources."*
Ty's own domain, and a fair thing to acknowledge. Low value as an argument, high value as evidence Julian read the announcement properly.

**6. Effective tax rate 8.0%**, with the Group continuing to *"leverage its global operations and align its strategy to manage various tax policy changes globally."*
**This is Kari's territory** (ex-KPMG transfer pricing / TESCM). Worth having for the Kari channel, not the Ty one.

## ⚠ The ERP line — read it carefully before using it

> *"Our global MILWAUKEE business grew 10.5% on an underlying basis in local currency, **after adjusting for the planned 2025 timing impact related to the MILWAUKEE Americas ERP system conversion**."*

**What it establishes:** an ERP conversion had a material enough financial effect that TTI is still adjusting reported growth for it a year later, in a public announcement.

**What it does NOT establish:** an overrun. The word is **"planned"**. This is TTI describing a deliberate, managed timing effect — consistent with the CEO's public position that the conversion *"executed flawlessly."*

**So do not reach for it as evidence of anything going wrong.** Doing so would repeat the over-reach caught on 8 Aug with the ERP-tier attribution. What it *is* good for is a neutral, informed observation that large ERP conversions have financial consequences that ripple across reporting periods — which supports the assurance argument in [[tti-ea-governance-value]] §5b without naming a failure. Background: [[tti-ea-requirements]] § The overrun question.

## What NOT to say

- **Don't call SG&A growth "bloat" or imply waste.** TTI attributes it to deliberate growth investment, and Ty signed off on it. The observation is arithmetic, not criticism.
- **Don't congratulate at length.** One acknowledgement, then move to what it implies. A candidate reciting a competitor-standard results summary reads as homework, not insight.
- **Don't use the ERP line as evidence of an overrun.** See above.
- **Don't offer views on the buyback or capital allocation.** Not Julian's remit, and it invites a "what would you know about it" reaction.
