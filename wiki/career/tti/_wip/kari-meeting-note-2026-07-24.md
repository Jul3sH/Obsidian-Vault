**Date:** Fri 24 Jul 2026

## Kari - Background and Style

- Background: law school, then MBA, then KPMG (partner, based in Asia, built out significant TESCM/transfer-pricing functionality), then left KPMG to join TTI approximately 10 years ago
- Self-described as non-linear/unorthodox career path
- Works closely with IT; exact internal remit not fully clear from the conversation

---

## Julian's Opening

- Led with the Stephan relationship going back to the mid-80s
- Mentioned knowing Horst in the early days of setting up TTI (used as a credibility anchor)
- Delivered elevator pitch covering:
  - EA diagnosis: lots of steering committees and silos are often a symptom of no architectural authority - Ty may not have called it EA, but Julian's read is that is what is needed
  - AI: moving from unstructured pilots with no clear ROI to sponsored, governed programmes
  - Service design lens: playing with what still needs human judgment, what is a standard operating model, what can be delegated
  - AI governance point: companies that do not govern AI gains will see people pocket productivity as personal gain - it will not show in EBIT because there is no velocity baseline and no governance structure
  - Generalists point: many of the right people were let go in the AI wave; generalists who can leverage AI and outsource to operating models are the new premium skill
  - Processes needing to be engineered properly before AI can govern them
- Kari's response to the AI section: "I think that's great. You've been getting into that."
- Julian disclosed he had been made redundant and had not landed a global role; the right people who would have pulled him in had retired or moved countries; had found it very difficult the last six months through conventional channels; re-engaged via Stephan

---

## Kari's Description of the Need

- Described the need unprompted in EA terms: somebody to come in and look holistically at the business, the processes, how things are structured, and then the technology to support it
- Julian confirmed: "That is very much aligned to Enterprise Architecture"
- No pushback on framing or scope

## Geographic Scope

- Kari's view: initial conversations were about US/HK alignment, but if we are doing this it should be done everywhere - across the whole enterprise
- Julian agreed
- Context provided: Australia is okay, Europe is okay - the alignment gap is elsewhere
