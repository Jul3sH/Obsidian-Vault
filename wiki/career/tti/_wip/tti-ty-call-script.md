# TTI - Ty Call Script

## What this is, and why it exists

**What it is:** what Julian *says* on the Ty call - the opening line, five talking points, and prepared holding lines for the objections most likely to come. The written proposal Ty *reads* is a separate document, [[tti-ty-engagement-proposal]].

**Why it was created (9 Aug 2026):** it lived inside the proposal file until now, two-thirds of the way down, behind the document Ty reads. That meant scrolling past the wrong content to reach the right content under time pressure, on the morning of a call that decides the engagement. Split out so the pre-call surface is one clean open.

**How it is used:** open this and nothing else before the call. `send: NEVER` - several lines here would be actively damaging if they reached Ty, Stephan or Tony Chung.

**Why this line and not the others (Julian, 8 Aug):** he works best with something real to deliver against within days; a month of analysis is where he loses direction. So execution-first is not a sales device here, it is how the engagement has to be built to succeed. It also resolved the 7 Aug Codex FATAL - Ty cannot defend a no-break 12-month commitment where the value case only appears after signature. **The frame underneath it, in Ty's head (from the Codex review):**
> *"I'm not buying a theory of enterprise architecture. I'm buying a mechanism that takes one stuck cross-region technology decision and gives it an owner, a standard, a ratifier and a date."*

**⚠ NEVER say "an extra pair of hands", "help out", or "where people are too busy".** The instinct behind those words is right; the words are a contractor framing. Stephan repeats Julian's phrasing verbatim, and it reaches Tony Chung as "we've got someone helping with the backlog" - which kills the director-level argument the whole engagement rests on. Say **"land it"**, **"get it moving"**, **"make it resolve"**. The early delivery is the *proof of the mechanism*, never a substitute for it.

Hard project, done right, because one team rallied around it. Ty's quote lists G&A cost reduction and leveraging synergies/AI/assets as separate drivers, not AI pointed specifically at G&A.

2. **"The reason that layer pays is that cross-region decisions currently have no owner, so they go to a committee.** Every one that waits defers whatever was behind it. The scope is three capabilities in one engagement: the architecture capability itself, cost transparency across the estate - so you can actually read what technology costs - and Gen AI skills distribution: skills evaluated, endorsed, and published where every team can find them, with TTI's own teams operating it from there." **If he probes the cost discipline** *(reframed 9 Aug - supersedes the old "TBM, not FinOps / FinOps is the cloud corner" line, do NOT say that)*: *"TBM is the strategic, top-down version; FinOps is the real-time, operational one. Fluency in how the two disciplines relate lands as senior; promising a full TBM programme is a rod for your own back. **On Gen AI, stay bounded** - the first skill through the pipeline, then their teams run it. Never sound like an AI transformation programme.

3. **"I'm not going to quote you a benefit number I haven't measured. Let him do that arithmetic himself.

4. **"Month one is execution, not a study.** Three things on your desk in thirty days - one for each capability. THE LINE still opens with it - that is the energy and the invitation - but the written month-1 list no longer commits to it. If Ty picks it up and wants it as a deliverable: enthusiasm, picked together in week one, the machinery built around it - and **never promise it RESOLVES inside 30 days**; the decision is made by the owner Ty names. Speakable: *"I don't make the decision - your named owner and ratifier do. I build the machinery that gets it made."* **Every month-1 item is an artefact Julian ships alone** - nothing waits on anyone else moving first.

5. **"It's twelve months, not six - six buys tactical fixes that revert when I leave, and it's actually dearer per month."** Then the number, stated plainly:

   > *"It's **US$299,563 all-in for the twelve months**, full-time with normal leave. That's based on my previous **Global Head of Enterprise Solution Architecture** salary of HK$155,773 a month, with 25% added as there are no benefits, bonus or employer costs on top."*

- **Tony Chung sequencing.** He must hear about this before it reaches a sign-off desk, framed as pooling authority rather than confiscating it. The "What this is not" paragraph in the proposal exists for him, not for Ty.
- **The NPV arithmetic is checkable:** US$299,563 ÷ 2.2607 = US$132,505. The 2.2607 is the 3-year annuity factor at 10% with benefits falling in years 2, 3 and 4 (0.8264 + 0.7513 + 0.6830). If Ty supplies TTI's WACC the whole table re-runs.
- **The 10% discount rate, explained (added 9 Aug, for if Ty asks "why ten percent?").** A saving that arrives in two years is worth less than the same saving today, because the money tied up meanwhile has a cost - the business could be earning a return on it elsewhere. The discount rate is the annual rate used to shrink future savings back to today's value. At 10%: a saving landing in year 2 counts at 82.6 cents on the dollar (1 ÷ 1.10²), year 3 at 75.1 (1 ÷ 1.10³), year 4 at 68.3 (1 ÷ 1.10⁴). Those three factors are what sum to the 2.2607 annuity factor in the arithmetic note above - benefits are assumed to start only in year 2, because year 1 builds the structure. **Why 10% specifically:** it is the conventional, conservative round-number stand-in for a company's cost of capital (WACC) when you don't know their actual figure. Direction to hold under questioning: a **higher** rate shrinks future savings more, so the breakeven saving **rises**; a lower rate does the opposite - so 10% being roughly right or slightly high is the conservative position. **The proposal deliberately makes no offer to re-run at TTI's WACC** (the "give me your WACC" line was removed 9 Aug - if Ty wants it at his rate he can ask, or run it himself; it's his prerogative, not a service to volunteer). If he does ask, the table re-runs in a minute from the arithmetic note above. **Speakable version:** *"Ten percent is a conservative stand-in for your cost of capital - money later is worth less than money now, so I discount the future savings back to today's value before comparing them to the fee."*
- **TTI IT spend is deliberately not asserted.** It is not in the vault and guessing it in front of a Deputy CFO loses the room. The proposal hands him the hurdle and asks him to supply his own denominator - the same device as letting him set the multiple ([[tti-ty-call-simulation-2026-07-19]]).
- **The procurement line IS safe to say.** Steve's *"how can we leverage our negotiating costs on IT"* is from the **public** annual results call ([[tti-2025-annual-results-script]] line 435), logged as P3 in [[tti-ea-requirements]]. **Do not confuse it with T12** - a similar-sounding IT-cost-leverage line about Ty's organisation that came from the employee Q&A and is internal/not quotable. A Codex review conflated the two on 8 Aug; the distinction is real and worth holding.
- **How month 1 "settles the hurdle question", mechanically (added 9 Aug - so the claim can be defended step by step).** Month 1 measures the **addressable pool**, not savings. (1) Julian defines what counts: the coordination tax (committee and escalation time on cross-region decisions, from calendar data, hours × loaded rates - the definition rule is why a renamed "alignment sync" can't leave the baseline) and the duplication cost (run cost of overlapping applications, from portfolio and contract data). (2) TTI's own finance ops pulls the data: the baseline comes from Ty's numbers, counted by his people, against rules Julian sets - neither side marks its own homework. (3) The output is an annual recurring figure, the pool, and the hurdle question becomes "is capturing this fraction of the measured pool credible?" - a division Ty does himself, the same device as fee-versus-spend. (4) The months 2-12 planning (target state, gap analysis, roadmap - the ADM cycle in the proposal's "Months 2-12" section) is then priced against that baseline as it lands: pool converted to capture, lever by lever. **The plan is no longer a month-1 deliverable** (cut 9 Aug - no plan before target state and gap analysis exist). (5) If the pool measures small, Julian says so in writing - the no-unmeasured-numbers spine applied to his own case. "Settled" means the benefit case has been converted from Julian's assumption into TTI's own measured numbers, either way. **Timing honesty (revised 9 Aug): month 1 commits to the measurement being defined and underway, not complete** - calendar and portfolio pulls in a big org can run past day 30 through nobody's fault, so the pool number lands "as the first numbers land," possibly early month 2. The written promise is the floor; finishing inside 30 days is the overdelivery case.
- **The cost-transparency closing passage, decoded (added 9 Aug - what each sentence is doing, so it can be defended fluently).** (1) *"Run-rate spend you are already paying, so payback is fast"* - run-rate is the recurring money that leaves automatically every year (licences, support, cloud, renewals). Savings found there mean stopping a payment already flowing, so they start immediately and repeat - no benefit lag, no dependency on behaviour change, unlike a transformation benefit. (2) *"The share of technology spend categorised and visible"* - the metric Ty holds Julian to: what percentage of TTI's technology spend is mapped into the taxonomy. Near zero today (one lump in the P&L), rises as mapping progresses, and entirely architect-controlled - it measures whether the view got built, not whether anyone acted on it. (3) *"Wherever the group is standardising how it reads cost and margin..."* - the deliberate conditional. Julian knows internally (employee Q&A, NOT quotable) that a group cost/margin standardisation initiative exists; the "wherever" phrasing asserts no knowledge, and invites Ty to make the connection himself - repositioning the capability as the missing technology layer of something already underway. Spoken version of the same device: *"Is there work underway to standardise how the business reads its cost and margin?"*
- **Taxonomy-risk guardrails (9 Aug, from the TBM evidence review).** Julian's two stated fears - a taxonomy that never gets agreed, and a taxonomy nobody uses - are the two failure modes the published evidence documents. The success pattern is to attach the taxonomy to an allocation model, a named decision-owner, a savings target and a review cadence; without those it becomes "an artifact of governance aspiration" (the GAO's federal TBM audit found transparency but no identified savings for exactly this reason). The four-step design in the proposal already encodes the mitigations, and month 1's measured baseline is the decision-attachment. **Never quote the case-study numbers to Ty** (Bank of Ireland, MassMutual, National Grid, SBA, Washington State) - vendor-published, non-TTI analogues, same ruling as the ISG and Thai Union figures. The "15-30% of governed budget" governance-cost claim is unverified - do not repeat it anywhere.
- **FinOps payback speed is stated qualitatively, not with a benchmark percentage.** Deliberate: the proposal rests on "I won't quote what I haven't measured", and a vendor statistic would break that discipline at the one point a CFO would check it.
