## Key Takeaways

- DDG is NOT hiring a conventional Chief AI Officer through interviews. They will engage 3-5 exceptional candidates as **paid consultants for 6-10 weeks**, on real DDG problems, and appoint a permanent Group Chief AI / Agentic Transformation Officer only if someone earns it in execution.
- Hire #1 (the CAIO-track role) starts as an **8-12 week high-level consulting engagement**, reporting to Owner / Group CEO. Mission: redesign DDG as an AI-native, agentic, continuously learning enterprise - not roll out tools or chatbots.
- Three separated leadership roles: #1 Agentic Transformation Lead, #2 AI Implementation / Agent Engineering Lead (recruited in parallel, much more technical), #3 Group Data Leader (seniority decided later).
- Chief scorecard weights systems-level integration over any single technical skill: agentic enterprise design 20%, AI depth / execution / decision science 15% each.
- The domain is sports/betting-adjacent: football pricing workflows, trading, exchange, sports data, rights valuation. Microsoft environment, seven development teams.
- The consulting process must create value even if nobody is appointed - so a consulting-shaped contribution is legitimate by design, not a consolation prize.

**DDG - AI Leadership & Head-Hunting Brief**
*Building an AI-native, agentic and continuously learning enterprise*
Discussion draft for Oli Slipper, September 2026. Confidential.

### 1. Executive Summary

We should not assume that the best data leader is also the best person to redesign DDG as an agentic enterprise. The capabilities overlap, but they are not the same. Our immediate objective is therefore not to fill a conventional "Chief Data & AI Officer" role. It is to identify exceptional people who can help us determine what an AI-native DDG should become, while simultaneously building the technical and data capabilities required to make that vision real.

The proposed approach is to recruit several top-end AI transformation candidates initially as paid consultants, give them real DDG problems, and assess them through execution. The strongest individual may earn the Group Chief AI / Agentic Transformation role. There is no requirement that any candidate receives the permanent role.

### 2. The Capability Model

We see five distinct capabilities that DDG needs to develop:

| Capability | What it means |
|------------|---------------|
| Data foundation | Architecture, identity, permissions, quality, lineage, real-time data and a trusted knowledge layer. |
| AI & agent engineering | Models, orchestration, tools, evals, memory, observability, security and integrations. |
| Enterprise & workflow design | Redesigning how information, decisions and work move between humans and agents. |
| Organisational change | Changing roles, incentives, management practices and skills across the workforce. |
| AI product & commercialisation | Using proprietary data and intelligence to create products, pricing advantages and competitive value. |

Finding one person who is genuinely world-class across all five is unlikely. The leadership structure should therefore separate agentic transformation, implementation/engineering and data leadership, while making them work as one system.

### 3. Foundational Decision Protocol

A core principle of the transformation should be a consistent decision-science workflow embedded into important AI agents and management processes:

**FRAME → CHALLENGE → EVIDENCE → ANALYSE → CONCLUDE → ACT → LEARN**

The system should explicitly distinguish facts, assumptions, hypotheses and unknowns; preserve provenance and confidence; challenge the initial framing; separate conclusions from actions; and compare predictions and decisions against actual outcomes. The objective is not simply to automate work, but to create an organisation that continuously improves its decision quality.

### 4. Hire #1 - Founding AI & Agentic Transformation Lead

- Initial structure: 8-12 week high-level consulting engagement
- Potential destination: Group Chief AI Officer / Chief Agentic Transformation Officer
- Reports to: Owner / Group CEO

#### Mission

Redesign DDG as an AI-native, agentic, continuously learning enterprise. The mandate is not to roll out AI tools or build a collection of chatbots. It is to determine how the Group would operate if it were designed today around proprietary data, machine intelligence, AI agents and a smaller number of exceptionally capable humans.

#### Core responsibilities

- Map the organisation as flows of information → decision → action → outcome → learning.
- For major workflows, determine what should disappear, remain human, become AI-assisted, become agent-executed, or require human approval.
- Design the human + agent operating architecture across trading, finance, management, BD, sales, legal, compliance, product, engineering, operations and customer functions.
- Embed the Group decision protocol: Frame → Challenge → Evidence → Analyse → Conclude → Act → Learn.
- Design how knowledge moves between people, agents, email/documents, transactional systems, proprietary datasets, external information and models.
- Challenge existing organisational design. The brief includes eliminating unnecessary reports, meetings, approvals, processes and roles - not merely automating them.
- Design AI governance covering permissions, auditability, evals, model selection, security, IP protection, human approval thresholds and agent autonomy.
- Create measurable AI/agentic transformation KPIs: decision quality, productivity, automation, model performance, revenue impact, cost reduction, time-to-decision and organisational learning.
- Work closely with senior management while being willing to challenge management's assumptions, including the owner's.

#### Candidate profile

An unusual intersection of AI systems, agents, product thinking, data understanding, organisational design, commercial judgement and hands-on execution. Relevant backgrounds may include leading AI labs, frontier technology companies, elite quantitative/systematic organisations or genuinely AI-native startups. Brand-name employers are useful signals, not selection criteria.

#### Explicitly not looking for

- A conventional CIO or generic digital-transformation executive.
- A pure data scientist or pure ML researcher.
- A Copilot/enterprise-software rollout specialist.
- A management consultant who stops at strategy decks.
- A prompt engineer or AI evangelist without systems-level execution.

### 5. Hire #2 - Founding AI Implementation / Agent Engineering Lead

#### Mission

Turn the agentic architecture into working production systems at speed.

#### Core responsibilities

- Take workflows from idea → specification → prototype → evaluation → production → monitoring → iteration.
- Build reusable agent infrastructure: tool interfaces, orchestration, permissions, memory, retrieval, evals, logging, observability and human-approval mechanisms.
- Build management, research, reporting, meeting/action, finance, customer intelligence, sports-pricing research, document/knowledge and monitoring agents.
- Integrate the Microsoft environment, internal databases/APIs, company systems and appropriate model providers.
- Work with business owners to translate messy operational requirements into reliable automated workflows.
- Document systems so DDG does not replace dependence on one legacy developer with dependence on one AI engineer.
- Measure adoption, reliability, time saved, decision improvement and commercial impact.

#### Candidate profile

Considerably more technical than Hire #1: strong software/Python engineering, LLM APIs, agent systems, RAG/knowledge systems, tool calling, evals, data pipelines, cloud/security and production engineering. Must also have strong product instincts and be able to understand the business problem rather than simply implement a specification.

### 6. Hire #3 - Group Data Leader

Possible titles: Chief Data Officer / Group Head of Data / Data Platform Director

#### Mission

Turn DDG's fragmented data estate into a trusted, governed, machine-readable proprietary asset that humans, models and agents can safely use.

#### Core responsibilities

- Own group data architecture, catalogue, taxonomy, common definitions and entity resolution.
- Own ingestion, quality, cleaning, lineage/provenance, permissions, access controls and retention.
- Design real-time and batch data architecture, APIs/data products, ML-ready datasets and feature infrastructure where appropriate.
- Bring together useful sports, pricing, transactional, exchange, customer and trading data across the Group without creating an uncontrolled data swamp.
- Make the Evidence layer of the Group decision protocol reliable, traceable and accessible.
- Support ML, customer profiling, pricing, risk, fraud, forecasting and other intelligence products with high-quality data foundations.

#### Candidate profile

A genuinely excellent data architecture / data platform leader, potentially from a leading technology, fintech, quantitative or data-intensive organisation. We should not assume this individual is also the right person to lead enterprise-wide agentic transformation.

### 7. How We Should Recruit the Chief

Rather than appointing a Chief AI Officer from interviews, run a paid consulting/audition process with approximately 3-5 exceptional candidates.

#### Proposed format

- Engage candidates individually for approximately 6-10 weeks.
- Provide controlled access to selected people, systems and anonymised data.
- Give each candidate several real DDG problems rather than hypothetical case studies.
- Require both architectural thinking and working implementation.
- Assess how quickly they understand the business, challenge assumptions, prioritise and produce measurable outcomes.
- Appoint a permanent Chief only if someone genuinely earns the role.

#### Example audition problems

- Management: redesign weekly management reporting, commitments and accountability using AI.
- Trading: redesign the football pricing workflow from research and models through market comparison, risk, execution, backtesting and learning.
- Data: show how fragmented sports/customer/transactional data becomes usable institutional intelligence.
- Organisation: identify at least one existing process or function that should be radically redesigned rather than merely automated.
- Agent architecture: build at least one production-quality workflow.
- Governance: demonstrate how proprietary IP remains protected while agents receive enough access to be genuinely useful.

### 8. Chief Candidate Scorecard

| Capability | Weight |
|------------|--------|
| Agentic enterprise / systems design | 20% |
| AI technical depth | 15% |
| Execution / building ability | 15% |
| Decision science / analytical thinking | 15% |
| Organisational & workflow redesign | 10% |
| Commercial judgement | 10% |
| Ability to challenge owner / management | 5% |
| Data understanding | 5% |
| Governance / security / IP | 5% |

The eventual Chief does not need to be the best programmer or the best data architect. The Chief's differentiating capability should be putting the whole system together: technology, data, workflows, decisions, people, governance and commercial outcomes.

### 9. Possible Later Hire - Decision Science / Applied AI Lead

A later specialist role may sit across sports pricing, customer profiling, risk, fraud, rights valuation, forecasting and capital allocation. The ideal background could be operations research, quantitative finance, applied statistics, econometrics or elite applied data science. The role would focus on rigorous hypothesis → evidence → modelling → validation → decision → outcome → learning loops. We should not recruit this role until the first hires have assessed the capabilities already present inside DDG.

### 10. Recommended Head-Hunting Sequence

- Now: identify 3-5 exceptional Founding AI & Agentic Transformation candidates for paid consulting/audition engagements.
- Now: recruit the Founding AI Implementation / Agent Engineering Lead in parallel.
- In parallel: assess DDG's current data estate, seven development teams and existing technical/data capability.
- Then: determine the appropriate seniority and title for the Group Data Leader.
- After approximately 8-12 weeks: appoint a Group Chief AI / Agentic Transformation leader only if a candidate has demonstrated the capability in practice.

### 11. The Core Headhunter Brief

**We are not looking for a conventional Chief AI Officer.**

We are looking for someone capable of helping redesign a profitable, data-rich operating group into an AI-native enterprise: one in which proprietary data, AI agents, human judgement and continuous learning combine to make better decisions and execute faster. The successful person must be able to challenge existing processes and management assumptions, understand the technology deeply, build or direct real systems, protect valuable IP and translate AI into measurable commercial outcomes.

The consulting process itself must create value even if no permanent appointment is made: DDG should emerge with better architecture, working systems, clearer organisational findings and a stronger understanding of the capabilities required for the next stage.
