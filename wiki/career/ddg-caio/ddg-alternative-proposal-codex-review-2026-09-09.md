# DDG Alternative Proposal - Codex Adversarial Review - 2026-09-09

This is the adversarial review of [[ddg-alternative-proposal-draft]] v1, run before Julian's sign-off per the adversarial-coverage rule. It was created because the proposal is outbound, deadline-bound (24h) and decision-feeding. Julian reads it alongside his own read of the draft and decides which findings to apply; corrections are made to the draft in a separate step, never by the reviewer. Reviewer was instructed to hunt confirmation-amplification in both directions (overreach in the proposal's favour AND self-undermining hedges).

**Verdict: RESTRUCTURE** (reviewer's call; findings 1-3 are the load-bearing ones). Dash check: PASS.

## Findings (ranked by severity, transcribed verbatim from Codex output)

1. **Critical: the workload calculation is wrong.**
   **Quote:** "Run the numbers on your own plan: 30 to 50 consultant-weeks of spend, five people pulling in five directions..."
   **Problem:** DDG specifies 3 to 5 candidates for 6 to 10 weeks each. The mathematical range is 18 to 50 candidate-weeks, not 30 to 50. Five candidates is the maximum, not the plan in every scenario.
   **Why it matters:** "Run the numbers on your own plan" followed by incorrect arithmetic gives a sceptical owner an immediate reason to dismiss the whole proposal.
   **Fix:** "On the ranges in your brief, the process could consume 18 to 50 candidate-weeks in aggregate, across up to five separately managed engagements."

2. **Critical: the proposal strawmans DDG's audition.**
   **Quote:** "At the end you hold opinions and prototypes built to win a competition, not to run your company. Then the real work starts."
   **Problem:** DDG explicitly requires real problems, working implementation, measurable outcomes and value even if nobody is appointed. Saying the process produces only opinions and competition prototypes directly contradicts the brief. The follow-up claim, "the thing you actually want... has not moved," has the same problem.
   **Why it matters:** The author may recognise their own safeguards and conclude that you either did not read the brief or are attacking it dishonestly.
   **Fix:** "Your model rightly tests candidates through real work. Its risk is fragmented accountability: repeated onboarding and access, separate solutions, and no single owner integrating transformation, engineering and data. We keep the real-work test, but put it under one accountable engagement."

3. **Critical: it is unclear what part of DDG's plan this replaces.**
   **Quote:** "Engage one accountable company on outcomes."
   **Problem:** DDG's plan separates the transformation lead, implementation lead and data leader. The proposal appears to replace the entire capability model with two people, although its plausible target is only the multi-candidate audition for the transformation lead.
   **Why it matters:** A sceptical owner can reject it as under-resourced, while an ex-Accenture reader will notice that the operating model has not been reconciled with the brief.
   **Fix:** "Replace the parallel audition for the transformation lead with one accountable pilot. We work with your seven development teams and whichever implementation and data capabilities you retain or recruit."

4. **High: the commercial claim contradicts the fee structure.**
   **Quote:** "a commercial structure where we only fully win when your constraint is gone."
   **Problem:** Stage 1 is fixed fee, most of Stage 2 appears fixed, and Stage 3 is a retainer. Only an undefined portion of Stage 2 is contingent. The team therefore wins financially before the constraint is gone.
   **Why it matters:** Commercially experienced readers will treat this as sales language that the table disproves.
   **Fix:** "We earn fixed fees for defined work delivered, while a specified part of Stage 2 is payable only when the agreed result is achieved." Describe the offer as "outcome-led," not wholly outcome-based.

5. **High: Stage 2 promises an undefined and potentially impossible result.**
   **Quote:** "Then we remove it..." and "The constraint measurably gone, at least one production-quality AI workflow live, your team using it."
   **Problem:** The "biggest constraint" could involve data quality, organisation design, regulation or people. It may not be removable in 4 to 6 weeks or require an AI workflow. "Gone" is an absolute promise before scope, dependencies or baseline exist.
   **Why it matters:** An owner can test this with one difficult constraint and expose the commitment as implausible.
   **Fix:** "Stage 1 agrees and baselines one bounded, high-value constraint. Stage 2 delivers a measurable improvement against the agreed target, including a production workflow where that is the appropriate intervention."

6. **High: speculation is presented as established client fact.**
   **Quote:** "Around that edge sits an operational layer... that you suspect costs more than it returns..." and "Most of what it does can be redesigned around AI agents and a smaller number of capable generalists."
   **Problem:** The Prompt Zero explicitly says much of this is speculation for Matt to test. "Most" is unsupported, and grouping seven development teams into questionable overhead may be especially provocative. The proposal also prescribes role removal before discovery.
   **Why it matters:** The acting COO may feel that outsiders have judged his organisation without evidence.
   **Fix:** "Your brief asks what should disappear, remain human, become AI-assisted or become agent-executed across a profitable, data-rich group with seven development teams. Our first hypothesis is that reporting, management cadence and routine process contain avoidable work. Stage 1 tests that before any organisational decision."

7. **High: "The honest question" currently supplies the rejection case.**
   **Quote:** "'Where have you done exactly this before?' Nobody has..."
   **Problem:** "Nobody has" is an indefensible universal claim and unnecessarily advertises the team's missing reference case. "Anyone claiming a long track record... is selling you something" also attacks other credible candidates. The remaining credentials are generic assertions rather than proof.
   **Why it matters:** Buyers tolerate novel delivery more readily than unsupported exceptionalism. This paragraph invites them to ask for evidence the proposal does not yet contain.
   **Fix:** Rename the section "How you control the risk": "We have not run this exact programme for a sports-data group. That is why Stage 1 is short, fixed-scope and separately gated. Dean brings [specific shipped result]; Julian brings [specific relevant result]. You judge the work before committing further."

8. **Medium: the cumulative tone risks insulting the brief's author.**
   **Quotes:** "The format is the problem." "Run the numbers on your own plan." "Not a beauty parade." "which is how entrepreneurial businesses actually change." "something the audition process was never going to give you."
   **Problem:** Any one line might feel punchy. Together they sound superior and dismissive, particularly because the proposal misdescribes the process.
   **Why it matters:** The first reader is friendly, but may have helped create the brief. The final owners are entrepreneurial, not necessarily receptive to being told how entrepreneurial companies "actually" work.
   **Fix:** Preserve the disruptive headline, but frame the argument as a trade-off: "The idea is sound. The risk is fragmented accountability." Remove "beauty parade," the universal claim about entrepreneurial change, and "never going to give you."

9. **Medium: HK-visible accountability remains asserted rather than designed.**
   **Quote:** "On the ground, in your office, the person you hold accountable." and "working UK hours that overlap your HK afternoon and evening."
   **Problem:** Dean's delivery authority, availability and onsite cadence are unspecified. Julian's sentence foregrounds distance and suggests evening work for DDG without explaining how responsibilities divide.
   **Why it matters:** Local accountability is a central buying requirement and the obvious objection to Julian's involvement.
   **Fix:** "Dean is the Hong Kong engagement lead, accountable for delivery and present onsite at an agreed cadence. Julian leads architecture and operating-model design from the UK, with daily overlap during Hong Kong afternoons." State the actual cadence before sending.

10. **Medium: the IP, security and independence promises are too absolute.**
    **Quote:** "Your IP stays yours, access is controlled and auditable from day one, and everything we build is documented so you are never dependent on us."
    **Problem:** IP treatment requires contractual distinctions between DDG data, pre-existing methods, third-party components and new work product. Auditability depends partly on DDG's systems. "Everything" and "never dependent" are not credible absolutes.
    **Why it matters:** These claims invite legal and technical objections that a two-page proposal cannot presently answer.
    **Fix:** "Before access begins, we agree IP ownership, access controls, audit requirements and handover. DDG retains its data and pre-existing IP; agreed deliverables are documented for operation by DDG or another supplier."

11. **Low: the call to action promises knowledge the team cannot yet have.**
    **Quote:** "Bring the constraint that annoys you most; we'll bring what we'd do about it in the first fortnight."
    **Problem:** The proposal says discovery is needed, but the closing implies a ready-made fortnight plan for an unknown constraint. "Annoys you most" may also select an irritation rather than the highest-value bounded problem.
    **Why it matters:** It weakens the otherwise sensible discovery logic.
    **Fix:** "Bring one candidate constraint. In 30 minutes, we will test whether it is valuable and bounded enough for Stage 1, then show how we would approach the first fortnight."

## Prompt Zero fit (reviewer's assessment)

The proposal is succinct, disruptive, skimmable and recognisably DDG-specific, so it broadly meets Q3. It only partly satisfies Q4: sports data, pricing and seven development teams provide specificity, but the treatment of DDG's three-part capability model and decision protocol is too shallow, while several supposedly current-state facts come from acknowledged speculation.

## Verdict justification (verbatim)

**RESTRUCTURE.** The staged engagement, bottleneck-led approach and two-person HK/UK model are strong foundations. However, the central argument currently relies on incorrect arithmetic and a material misrepresentation of DDG's own process. Recast the proposal as preserving DDG's real-work test while replacing fragmented accountability, define exactly which recruitment element it supersedes, and make the commercial and delivery promises precise. The section order can remain, but the argument running through it needs rebuilding before even a friendly resonance test.
