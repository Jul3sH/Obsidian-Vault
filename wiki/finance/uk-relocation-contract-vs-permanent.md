# UK Relocation: Contract versus Permanent Employment

## Purpose

This is the place for one question: if TTI offers Julian a role in Hong Kong, is he better off on a fixed-term contract than in permanent employment? It exists because the answer touches four things at once: Sophia's university fee status, Julian's UK tax residence, job security and renewal terms, and what to ask for in the negotiation.

## Why it matters

Julian, 30 Sep 2026: "there is potential for me to go to Hong Kong on a 2-year contract and then potentially renew another 2 years and keep the home status for university fees. This should be part of my negotiation with TTI, and I should avoid permanent employment."

Two routes to home fees on a renewal exist, and only one of them depends on the form of employment:

| Route | Depends on | Contract or permanent? |
|---|---|---|
| The gap year route | Sophia living in the UK from 1 September 2030, with a gap year before university ([[uk-home-fee-status]]) | Makes no difference |
| The temporary employment provision | Persuading a university that Hong Kong was a temporary absence from a UK home | Only a fixed-term contract could support it |

So the renewal path keeps home fees without a contract. The contract question is a possible extra benefit, and the long shot.

## What is already known

- **The provision exists.** UKCISA: "If you can demonstrate that you have not been ordinarily resident in the relevant residence area only because you, or a family member, were temporarily working outside the relevant residence area, you will be treated as though you have been ordinarily resident there." Sources and the tests assessors apply are in the parked section of [[uk-home-fee-status]].
- **Julian's read, 30 Sep 2026:** "It sounds like it's a bit of a long shot, and it's based on a different set of rules to the residency rules." "I think it's going to be quite difficult to convince the university that it was only temporary work in Hong Kong, that I only had a brief visit back to the UK, and that I have avoided being regarded as a UK resident."
- **The tension.** The facts that keep Julian non-resident for UK tax (few UK days, Cecil Road let, a home in Hong Kong) are the facts a fee assessor reads as having left the UK. The two tests are separate in law, and a case in the sources found a family ordinarily resident in two places at once, but the evidence for one position weakens the other.
- **A renewal counts against it.** Student Finance England's tests ask whether the contract "is renewable, has it been renewed or is it one of a succession of contracts abroad", and how long the employee has already been resident abroad.
