# UK Home Fee Status: Opus Review, 30 Sep 2026

## Purpose

This is an adversarial review of [[uk-home-fee-status]], checking its rule claims against its own sources, its date correction, and its fit with Julian's actual return plans. It exists so the document can be corrected before it is relied on for the GBP 120,000 university reserve in the Hong Kong return decision.

Map: Key Takeaways; Findings (ranked, most severe first); Checked and correct; Sources fetched; Verdict.

> As of 30 Sep 2026: review complete. No edits made to the source document. Not legal advice.

## Key Takeaways

- The rules the document states are correct, and the date shift is clean and complete.
- The main gaps are omissions, not errors: the temporary-employment provision, one cited university's "maintaining links" route, and Julian's primary plan (return by summer 2028) are all missing.
- Those omissions cut toward Hong Kong looking cheaper, so they are questions to test, not savings to bank.
- Key Takeaway 3 in the document states a home-fee outcome as fact where the body says "strong prospective case, subject to assessment".
- Four sources could not be verified today (Commons Library, Cardiff, Kingston, Oxford: HTTP 403), and the three Reddit threads were also blocked.

## Findings

No FATAL findings.

### 1. SERIOUS: the temporary-employment provision is omitted, though three of the document's own sources state it

**Text at issue.** The document lists the settled-person conditions and discusses temporary absence only as holidays, gap years and travel. It never mentions that a person is treated as ordinarily resident if they would have been but for a parent's temporary employment abroad.

**Evidence.**
- S3 (UKCISA, Definitions in HE England): "If you can demonstrate that you have not been ordinarily resident in the relevant residence area only because you, or a family member, were temporarily working outside the relevant residence area, you will be treated as though you have been ordinarily resident there."
- S6 (Cambridge glossary): temporary absence "also covers periods of education, fixed-term employment or assignments (by the student or immediate family member) in a different country", with "copies of all employment/assignment contracts (including extension letters) covering the three-year period" and "evidence of where the family/offer holder would reside at the end of this absence".
- S5 (Manchester): for temporary absence due to employment "we normally require employment contracts (yours or your parents') that: Cover the entire period spent abroad; Clearly state start and end dates; Explicitly confirm the role is temporary".
- S12 (SFE guidance, section 2.6) lists the tests: the contract's fixed period, whether it "is renewable, has it been renewed or is it one of a succession of contracts abroad", "how long the employee has already been resident abroad", and a right of return. Section 2.4 adds that "Absence from the UK because of emigration should generally not be considered a temporary absence".

**Why it matters.** A fixed-term 24-month Hong Kong contract with a planned return is the kind of case these tests were written for. If it qualified, the Hong Kong years would count as UK residence, which bears on the fallback (stay to summer 2030) and possibly removes the need for the gap year. It could also go the other way: a renewed contract, or a family history of living in Hong Kong, reads as emigration (compare Huddleston, 13 years in Hong Kong with a father's work, refused, in S2). Julian concluded on 28 Sep that the provision does not apply to him ([[HK-Return-Beliefs]] claim 13), but no reason is recorded there or here.

**Fix.** Add a short section "Temporary employment abroad" quoting S3, listing the S12 section 2.6 tests, and recording Julian's 28 Sep conclusion with his reason and date. Do not treat it as a saving in the savings model unless a university fee team confirms it in writing on the actual contract.

### 2. SERIOUS: Julian's primary plan (return by summer 2028) is not addressed, and the findings are not mapped to the GBP 120,000

**Text at issue.** Scenario 1 is a return "for sixth form" by 1 September 2030; Scenario 2 is a return only for university. The Purpose says the document exists because of the GBP 120,000 reserve, but no section says which scenario that figure applies to.

**Evidence.** [[uk-relocation-project]] Status (30 Sep) and [[HK-Return-Beliefs]] claim 16: the plan is a return before GCSEs in summer 2028, with the post-GCSE return in summer 2030 as the fallback. [[uk-relocation-savings-comparison]] prices the GBP 120,000 as "If Sophia spends the four years before university overseas in HK", which is the document's Scenario 2 only.

**Why it matters.** By the rule the document states, a return by summer 2028 clears the three-year window for straight-through entry in autumn 2032 (window from 1 September 2029) by a year, and the joint-move argument applies a fortiori. A return during Year 10, before 1 September 2029, also clears it. Neither is said. So the reader cannot see from this document that the GBP 120,000 is incurred only if Sophia stays in Hong Kong through Year 13, and is avoided in the primary plan and (if the gap year route holds) in the fallback.

**Fix.** Add a three-row table at the top: return by summer 2028 (window clear, no gap year needed); return by 1 September 2030 (Scenario 1, gap year needed); no return before university (Scenario 2, the GBP 120,000 case).

### 3. SERIOUS: one cited university describes a "maintaining links" route that the document omits and that qualifies the Scenario 2 conclusion

**Text at issue.** "Scenario 2 would normally result in Overseas status for the undergraduate degree".

**Evidence.** S5 (Manchester): "If you hold British or Irish citizenship but have lived outside the UK or Overseas Territories during all or part of the three years before your course starts, you may still be considered ordinarily resident if you've maintained strong and regular links to the UK ... you should have made at least one visit every two years in that ten-year period." S6 (Cambridge) also allows "Dual residence" for "equally longstanding and sustained links with two countries".

**Why it matters.** At least one institution applies a more lenient test than the document's general statement. It is institution-specific and discretionary ("may"), and Cambridge's line that "Most undergraduates will be deemed ordinarily resident in the same country as their parents" points the other way. It does not justify removing the reserve, but "normally Overseas" is not true everywhere.

**Fix.** Add one sentence under Scenario 2: some universities (Manchester, S5) consider British citizens abroad with regular return visits as possibly ordinarily resident; this is institution-specific and not a basis for budgeting. Keep the "Budget for Overseas undergraduate fees" recommendation.

### 4. SERIOUS: Key Takeaway 3 states the outcome as fact, which the body does not support

**Text at issue.** Key Takeaway 3: "then a gap year, with university from autumn 2033 on home fees (deferred entry applied for in Year 13)."

**Evidence.** The body says "a strong prospective Home-fee case", "Strong, subject to assessment", and "There is no published dataset quantifying success for the exact 'two A-level years plus gap year' route". S1 (UKCISA): "Each educational institution uses the regulations to make its own decision about your fee status." The same unhedged wording is carried into [[uk-relocation-project]] (Status: "gives university entry in autumn 2033 on home fees"; status log: "the gap year route ... is confirmed with conditions").

**Why it matters.** This is the confirmation-amplification pattern: the filing summary is firmer than the research. The takeaway was written by the filing agent, not Julian.

**Fix.** Reword to: "a joint return by 1 September 2030 plus a gap year gives a strong prospective case for home fees from autumn 2033; each university decides, and there is no published success data for this route." Apply the same hedge to the project Status paragraph and log row.

### 5. MINOR: the education-purpose condition is stated loosely, and its exception is missing

**Text at issue.** "Not have been resident during that period wholly or mainly to receive full-time education."

**Evidence.** Education (Fees and Awards) (England) Regulations 2007, Schedule 1 paragraph 2(1)(b): residence that "has not during any part of the period ... been wholly or mainly for the purpose of receiving full-time education"; paragraph 2(2): this "does not apply to a person who is treated as being ordinarily resident ... in accordance with regulation 2(4)" (the temporary-employment rule). S12 section 2.5 confirms the exception for children in the UK whose parents work abroad temporarily.

**Why it matters.** "Any part of" makes the gap year and sixth form each individually testable. The exception is exactly what a boarding variant (Sophia in the UK, Julian in Hong Kong) would rely on. This also resolves the "Not verified" note under [[HK-Return-Beliefs]] claim 16: the rule exists.

**Fix.** Quote paragraph 2(1)(b) and 2(2) and name the regulations.

### 6. MINOR: the deferred-entry claims rest on two unverifiable sources, one Welsh; a verified English source is available

**Text at issue.** "Published Cardiff guidance expressly identifies a deferred-entry applicant who later meets the residence requirement as a case suitable for reassessment; Kingston also states ...".

**Evidence.** S8 (Cardiff) and S11 (Kingston) both returned HTTP 403 today, so neither claim could be checked. Cardiff is in Wales, where the Welsh fee regulations apply, so it is weak evidence of English practice. S10 (KCL), verified: "Your fee status is assessed at the time of application ... and takes into consideration a specific start date. If you defer your course start date, be aware that this could affect your fee status ... The Admissions Office reserves the right to re-assess your status for your deferred start date."

**Fix.** Cite KCL for the mechanism, note Cardiff applies Welsh rules, and mark the Cardiff and Kingston quotes as unverified (30 Sep 2026).

### 7. MINOR: the SFE gap-year condition is only partly stated

**Text at issue.** "Student Finance guidance treats a gap year as compatible with continuing ordinary residence where a UK home is maintained."

**Evidence.** S12 (Gap years): SFE must be satisfied the student "has maintained a residence in the UK ... and will return to England ... other than solely for the purpose of completing the relevant course", and a student abroad on 1 September "must be able to evidence that they will return to the UK prior to the first day of the course". S12 is the AY 2024/25 edition.

**Fix.** Add the "other than solely for the course" condition and the return-before-course evidence to the gap-year evidence list; note the guidance edition and recheck the current one in 2032.

### 8. MINOR: citation mismatches

- S1 is cited for the settled-person conditions and for 1 September, but the page states neither. The 1 September rule is in S3 and S7 (Sheffield: "Your fee status is assessed as at the first day (1 September) of the first academic year"). S7 is listed in the register but cited nowhere in the body.
- S1 does support the Years 2 to 3 point ("The three years are fixed in time and don't move as you progress through your course"), which currently cites only the unverifiable S14.
- S4, S8, S11, S14 (403) and S15 to S17 (Reddit, blocked) could not be verified.

**Fix.** Recite as above and mark the unverified sources with the date.

### 9. MINOR: the Scenario 2 master's case leans on forums where an official source exists, and omits parental residence

**Evidence.** S12 section 2.5: "A person who has come to the UK to study or be schooled may initially be ordinarily resident here primarily for educational purposes, but the purpose of residence may subsequently change." This is a firmer basis for "possible on some reported facts" than the three unverifiable Reddit threads. S6: "Most undergraduates will be deemed ordinarily resident in the same country as their parents", which the Scenario 2 evidence list does not mention (where Julian lives during her degree).

**Fix.** Anchor the conclusion on S12 section 2.5; add "where her father lives" to the evidence list. The forum section is already labelled anecdotal and is not over-weighted.

### 10. MINOR: Key Takeaway 2 overstates the body

**Text at issue.** "the father's joint move is what shows Sophia would live in the UK even without sixth form." The body says it "help[s] show". **Fix.** "helps show".

## Verdict

The document can be relied on for what it states: the rules are correctly described, the date correction is clean, and the budgeting advice (assume Overseas unless confirmed in writing) is prudent. It is not yet complete enough to plan on, for three reasons. It does not address Julian's primary plan (return by summer 2028), so it does not show that the GBP 120,000 applies only if Sophia stays through Year 13. It omits the temporary-employment provision and one university's "maintaining links" route, both from its own sources, which cut toward Hong Kong looking cheaper and must be tested rather than assumed. And its Key Takeaway 3, echoed in the project file, states an assessed outcome as fact. Fix findings 1 to 4 before the document feeds the savings model or the decision.
