# TTI board risk: Fable Council

As of 2 October 2026: council verdict delivered, adversarially checked, ready for Julian's read. Review advice, not an acceptance or relocation decision. Reporting line, budget, performance assessment, term and termination protection remain unagreed.

## Key Takeaways

- **Astra is the review to keep.** Every public claim it made checked out against primary sources, and it is the only one that tested the brief's premises instead of absorbing them. DeepSeek is the most decisive on the job and worth keeping for that; Gemini answered a different, literal question with no sources and should not be relied on for any figure.
- **The council agrees with Astra and DeepSeek on the shape of the risk:** a hostile displacement of both Pudwills within six years is unlikely; ordinary succession is likely; the live risk is the role itself, which can be hollowed or ended inside two years with no board drama at all.
- **The brief's own premises need correcting before any of this is reused.** TTI has no Group COO. The CFO in the "CEO, CFO, COO" trio is Frank Chan, Horst's 34-year Hong Kong man, not a US ally. Horst is 81 now and 87 at the end of the window, not 84 to 90. Nothing in the Articles entrenches him: one share, one vote, chairman appointed and removable by the board.
- **The deciding question is not "will the board flip" but "who signs Julian's performance review".** None of the three said it that plainly. Stephan deferring to Ty on 25 September is the warning sign, and it has already fired.

## Council Verdict

### Where the council agrees

- **Hostile takeover is the wrong frame.** Astra and DeepSeek both reject it; the reviewers and fact-check support them. Richman's leadership already runs the business, results are strong (H1 2026 revenue +5.9%, net profit +17.5%), and Horst is 81. Nobody needs a confrontation. Waiting wins.
- **Succession, not coup, is how family control ends.** Horst retires or dies, Frank Chan retires, Stephan stays a wealthy minority holder, professional management carries on. Astra calls this "material"; DeepSeek puts it at 60-75% over six years. The council sees no reason to disagree with either.
- **The role is the exposure.** Astra and DeepSeek both rank "role blocked, reduced or terminated without any board event" as the most likely bad outcome, and all three reviewer lenses concur. It rests on a verbal mandate from one 81-year-old against a written institutional no, and Stephan's sponsorship has already failed once against the same chain.

### Where the council clashes

- **Stephan's sponsorship: DeepSeek says "token role, little power to lose"; Astra says his influence is unproven either way.** The evidence auditor marks DeepSeek's line as inference dressed as fact. The chairman sides with Astra on the evidence, and with DeepSeek on the practical point: against Ty, Stephan's sponsorship has so far been worth nothing.
- **Routing via Ty: Astra's safeguards (named operating sponsor, Steve and Ty acknowledging the remit) go back through the chain that said no.** The decision-logic reviewer calls this the 20 August move repeated. DeepSeek's alternative, a reporting line that does not route through the people who said no with board or Remuneration Committee approval, is the only recommendation that differs from the precedent, but DeepSeek does not say who would hold that line. Neither review resolves this; see the recommendation below.
- **Staged entry.** The decision-logic reviewer argues all three treated relocation as binary and missed a consult-first, family-stays-in-the-UK option. The chairman flags this as a genuine clash with Julian's own pre-committed rule in [[horst-meeting-key-asks]] ("do not accept a consulting-bridge reframe"). It is raised here so he can reject it knowingly, not so the council can overturn a rule he set for good reasons.

### Blind spots the council caught

- **Nobody priced the one number that is actually pricable: losing the sponsor inside two years.** For a man of 81, two-year mortality alone is roughly one in ten (the chairman's estimate from published male life tables for Hong Kong and Germany, about 5% a year at that age); add incapacity and retirement and one in six is a defensible planning figure. That is a rough actuarial number, not a calibrated forecast, but it is better founded than any takeover percentage in the three reviews.
- **Who controls the Sunning Inc. and Cordless shares after Horst** (trust, estate, spouse) is the decisive six-year ownership question. Astra notes it as unestablished; nobody looked, and public sources do not say. This is the variable that would turn succession into something sharper.
- **A quiet, backdated contract is a job that exists because one man said so.** DeepSeek calls it a warning; nobody asked how a hire made by the chairman's say-so, with no owner inside the organisation, fares at its first performance review, or what it signals about how Horst governs and therefore how the operating chain will treat his appointees once he is gone.
- **Richman is 66.** His own succession sits inside the window. Only Astra mentions it, in one clause.
- **The mandate rests on Julian's own recollection.** The only record of what Horst said on 13 and 14 September is Julian's memory of it, with the backdated-contract detail added from memory on 1 October; nothing is in writing from Horst or Stephan, and Julian is the person with most at stake in how it is remembered. That does not make it wrong, but it is weaker evidence than a written offer, and none of the three reviews discounted for it.
- **The boring explanation was never stated in full.** One reading the council finds at least as plausible as the threat theory, and no better evidenced: Ty was handed an unbudgeted senior hire via the chairman's son in the week he was cutting headcount, and could not justify it to the people he was laying off. Horst heard that someone he had decided to help was refused and overrode it, as founders do. Stephan, knowing an override without Ty will not stick, slowed it down. No threat, no coalition. Astra came closest; DeepSeek installed Julian's "turkeys don't vote for Christmas" theory as the established cause instead, which the standing assumption does not license any more than it licenses Ty's stated reason.

### Root-cause and precedent check

- **Does the leading recommendation resolve the core uncertainty?** The core uncertainty is whether the organisation will support the role once Horst's attention moves on. Written terms alone do not resolve it; they only cap the financial loss. The recommendation below therefore has two parts: contractual floor and legitimised reporting line. Both are needed.
- **Enacted and failed, against the 20 August precedent.** The 20 August no was produced by the operating chain under a Stephan-to-Ty route. Any safeguard that again asks Ty to endorse the role ends the same way, with a second no or a hollow yes. The recommendation differs from that precedent only if the papering and reporting line are settled by Horst through the board's own machinery (HR, Remuneration Committee, a reporting line into the chairman's office), not by Ty's assessment. A reporting line into Stephan does not qualify: his sponsorship has already deferred to Ty once. If Horst will not do it that way, the mandate is not what it sounded like at lunch, and that is the answer.

### The recommendation

Do not unwind the UK move on the current mandate. Treat the job as real only when three things exist together: (1) a written multi-year contract with termination protection, approved through TTI's own machinery rather than slipped in quietly; (2) a reporting line and performance assessor that do not sit inside the chain that said no; (3) a named operating sponsor and a funded first assignment, so the role has work on day one. If any one is refused, the refusal is the information: the role would be hollowed within two years, with or without any board event. The takeover probability is low and does not drive this decision. The sponsor-loss probability is not low, and does.

### The one thing to do first

One written message to Stephan, because Horst left execution with him, asking two questions and nothing else: who papers the contract, and who Julian reports to. Deadline named. The message is diagnostic, not a request for endorsement, which is what makes it differ from the 20 August route. His answer (HR via Horst's office, or "let me check with Ty") tells Julian which world he is in before any schooling, housing or flight decision is touched.

## Per-model notes

**Astra (keep).** Separates losing office, seat and ownership; cites the Articles correctly; labels its 10-25% as a subjective judgement and refuses to inflate it. Weaknesses: no two-year versus six-year split on the job, and its safeguards route back through Ty. Did not state the boring explanation outright.

**DeepSeek (keep for the job verdict, discard the arithmetic).** The clearest line on the role: "below even money" that it is created on written terms and survives two years intact, and the quiet backdated contract read as a warning. Its probability chain is internally inconsistent: 10-20% attempt and 5-10% overall imply success given an attempt of at least 25%, and about 50% on matched bounds, which contradicts its own "low". "Horst's control rests on share ownership, which no director can take away by a board vote" conflates ownership with executive office: Articles 120, 121 and 128 let the board remove both the chairmanship and executive office without touching a single share. Used the wiki, and it shows: it absorbed Justin-sourced anecdotes and Julian's own threat theory as established fact.

**Gemini (discard for figures, keep one idea).** Answered the brief literally with no vault context, so never reached the job. Builds a faction table from nationality and appointment timing after the input it was given said loyalty is unknown, then weaponises the brief's premises ("flight risk as a weapon", "expected willingness to cash out"). Three unsourced figures, one wrong fact. Its one contribution Astra and DeepSeek should have kept: a list of mechanisms (board coup, management buyout, executive walk-out). Its buyout route is a family sale, not a displacement; it would mean a mandatory general offer at 30% of voting rights, which it did not mention, and the family's 21.9% alone does not reach that threshold.

- **Verification:** three reviewer subagents (evidence, decision logic, blind spots) and one fact-check subagent feed the chairman synthesis; an adversarial subagent checks the synthesis before handback; Julian is the verifier of private-source observations and of the verdict itself.
