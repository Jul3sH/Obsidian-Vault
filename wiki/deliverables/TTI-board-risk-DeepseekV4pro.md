## Purpose

An independent review of TTI's board, internal power dynamics and succession risk, answering how likely US leadership is to displace Horst and Stephan Pudwill from effective control over the next six years, and what that means for Julian's employment risk in the Horst-mandated role.

# TTI Board Risk - Independent Review (DeepSeek V4 Pro)

## Conclusion

A hostile, board-led displacement of both Pudwills within six years is unlikely. Horst's control rests on share ownership (~21.86%), which no director or the CEO can take away by a board vote; directors serve at shareholders' pleasure, and no takeover is in evidence. What is likely is ordinary succession: Horst is 81 and already operationally hands-off, Stephan holds a token Vice-Chairman role with little power to lose, and the US/Milwaukee professional leadership (Richman, CEO since 2024) already runs the business. "Effective control" is not being seized; it is already migrating through normal generational succession. The risk to Julian is different and higher: the role rests on an 81-year-old's verbal mandate, against a written institutional "no" from the very leaders he would sit alongside, with nothing in writing. That role can be hollowed or ended inside two years, with or without any board drama.

## Verified vs assumed

Verified (public): 13 directors, five executive (Horst, Stephan, Richman, Frank Chan, Camille Jojo) and eight independent, confirmed on TTI's corporate page; Richman CEO since May 2024, board Aug 2024; Horst co-founded TTI in 1985 and is 81 (Forbes), net worth ~$7.4B, German citizen, HK resident.

Assumed ([[TTI-board-current]], not independently re-derived): the ~21.86% shareholding, Frank Chan's age (72) and retirement timing, Ty Staviski's exact title ("Deputy CFO"), and the absence of any formal succession plan. None of these are decisive to the conclusion.

## The three scenarios

**1. Deliberate hostile displacement of both Pudwills.** Attempt probability: low, roughly 10-20% over six years. There is real US-vs-HK tension, but it is about headcount, IT control and "dirty washing", not about removing the founder and his son; no motion, proxy fight or bid is evidenced. Success conditional on an attempt: low, because a ~21.86% holder with family allies is not dislodged by a board vote. Overall: roughly 5-10%. I would need evidence of an actual move (a Nomination Committee challenge, a share sale, a takeover approach) to raise this.

**2. Ordinary succession, family retaining ownership.** This is the base case, roughly 60-75% that within six years Horst is no longer executive chairman (retirement or death), Frank Chan retires, and the family becomes passive, wealthy shareholders while professional management runs the company. Julian's own hypothesis is well-supported: Stephan neither wants nor would secure the chairmanship and would offer little resistance. This is not a risk to the Pudwills; it is how founder-led Hong Kong manufacturers normally end.

**3. Julian's role blocked, reduced or terminated without either event.** Highest probability, and partly independent of 1 and 2. The role's only foundation is Horst's verbal "just make it happen" and a verbally accepted number. The operating chain that would have to fund, staff and accept it wrote Julian a considered no on 20 Aug. Reporting line, budget, performance assessment, guaranteed term and termination protection are all unagreed. The threat dynamic Julian named ("turkeys don't vote for Christmas") explains both the rejection and what happens if he is forced in over their heads: they will contain or remove him. The role is not yet created, and the odds it is created on written, protected terms and survives two years intact are below even money.

## Employment risk

First two years: high. Without a written term and termination protection Julian is exposed to the exact people who declined him, and his sponsor is disengaged and 81. The "quiet, backdated contract" Horst floated is a warning, not a comfort: it signals the role is being slipped in around the operating leadership rather than legitimised with them, which makes it easier to slip out later.

Full six years: the role almost certainly does not survive six years on its own sponsorship. By year six Horst is 87 or gone. It survives only if re-anchored inside the professional leadership or the board, not the family.

What would change my mind: a written contract with a guaranteed multi-year term and termination protection, approved at board or Remuneration level; a reporting line that does not route through the people who said no; named budget authority. Warning signs: Stephan still failing to get HR to draft the contract; Ty's "assessment" producing no funded work or operating access; Horst's attendance or engagement slipping; any takeover or share-sale chatter.

Questions that could change the conclusion: (1) Is there any board or Nomination Committee resolution creating the role, or only Horst's instruction? (2) What is Ty's actual title and reporting line, and does his no carry board backing? (3) Who succeeds Frank Chan, and from which side of the US-vs-HK divide? (4) Is there any actual evidence a US director has sought to remove either Pudwill, or is it all inference from headcount politics?
