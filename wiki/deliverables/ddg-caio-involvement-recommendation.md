## Prompt Zero

*Written 9 Sep 2026 by Julian, in his own words. Read this in full before working on this deliverable.*

1. **Trying to accomplish:** An outcome-based SOW-type proposal for DDG Group as an alternative to them going to market for talent.

2. **Why it matters / stakes:** They want HK-based AI leaders and Julian has just decided to relocate to the UK. A good opportunity he could do from the UK if he can get them to buy in. 24 hours to come back to the contact.

3. **Done looks like:** A succinct, high-level, disruptive proposal, 1-2 pages, for low-attention entrepreneurial salespeople (one ex-Accenture founder-type can tolerate structure). It captures the root need and cuts through the waffle: "what you're trying to do is this, and this is the better way. Skip 8-12 weeks of candidates going in different directions on assessments; remove your biggest bottleneck now, deliver value immediately, test, repeat, change organically. Sod the job structures and over-engineered job search." Sendable to Matt (friend, acting COO / pseudo-CIO) as a resonance test.

4. **Wrong looks like:** Generic. If it can't relate to the current state of their business and where they need to go, we lose them. It must capture where they're at right now, present the problem as a common one we understand and know how to solve, and be laser-focused on that.

5. **What I know that isn't written down:** Gaming / sports-data industry, cricket-centric, providing data and odds to sportsbooks. Oli Slipper: successful exit from a sportsbook data company, chairman of Surrey Cricket, front-man CEO entrepreneur, not interested in operations. Yan (Chinese, background finding high-net-worth individuals and helping them gamble) runs it and is about the gambling and making money. Ops is a necessary evil to both. Oli sees the local team as inefficient overhead and would ultimately like to remove roles one by one and run with fewer generalists plus AI. Two-pronged AI opportunity: (a) running the operational side of the business, (b) leveraging AI practically in the core value proposition (sports data, statistics, odds). Much of this is speculation - the Matt resonance test is how it gets checked. Leadership glazes over at process; Matt isn't really interested either.

6. **The pieces:** (a) The vehicle: outsourced to a company ("ACME Company Limited" placeholder), not an in-house hire - Dean (HK-based, own company, real pragmatist, would be impressive) visible and accountable on the ground, Julian in the UK, work split between them; HK presence offered as fractional and outcome-based. (b) Dean's buy-in - the same proposal goes to him. (c) The 2-page proposal itself, then Matt for resonance, then tune. (d) The commercial shape: outcome-based - to brainstorm with Claude, unresolved. (e) The bottleneck is theirs to name: offer example ideas of what dynamic companies like this want, but they tell us; if they don't know, that IS the discovery. Framing: effectively a pilot - "instead of loads of candidates, let us have a go" - and even if nothing else they come out with a real discovery for going to market or to tender with.

7. **The hard part:** Julian's confidence: out of the market, doing AI but not yet delivered it; no time and no appetite to pitch them himself. Dean has been doing it and would carry the room, which is why the partnership matters: get Dean in front of Oli and it might be game on. Second: Dean's not local, Yan is Chinese, and locals don't always like Westerners telling them what to do, so this is really about Oli. Third: stopping them going to tender/headhunters when they ask "where have you done this before?" - neither has done this explicitly in big companies; the ask is "stop the fucking about, let us come in and just do it." And the money.

*Amendment 9 Sep 2026: piece (d) resolved - commercial shape is the staged model (fixed-fee discovery sprint, gates an outcome pilot with a contingent slice, gates a fractional monthly retainer). Fee levels still Julian's to set on the draft.*

*Amendment 9 Sep 2026 (2), Julian's ruling on Codex finding 4: NO contingent payment element anywhere. A contingent slice lets difficult employees or unrealistic expectations turn into a definition-of-done dispute where they decide not to pay, and Dean would not sign up to that. All fees are fixed against a very clearly defined SOW with outcomes explicitly agreed by both sides; the client's outcome protection is the stage gate (stop buying if not delivered), never the fee.*
