## What the evidence actually supports

### 1. The rejection is real; its motive is unresolved

**Working assumption, instructed by Julian on 2 October:** the explanation in Ty's 20 August rejection is a formal narrative that cannot be trusted as the real reason. This is the basis of the analysis, not an independently established finding that Ty lied.

**Observable outcome:** Ty communicated that employment would not proceed despite Stephan's sponsorship. The message in [[tti-comms-log]] contains claims about leadership consultation, organisational change and familiarisation time; those claims do not establish the actual decision process or motive. Its silence about geography or power is not evidence against either motive. Steve's individual position is not established by the message.

The same log's **15-16 July record** reports an HK-based architecture need and the **10 August call recollection** records encouraging comments. These show what was said, not a commitment to fund or support the appointment. They could reflect genuine initial interest, diplomacy or different preferences about remit and reporting. **I withdraw the earlier claim that they materially rebut the geographic explanation.**

At the **14 September lunch**, Stephan reportedly acknowledged that the role might have been perceived as threatening and warned that bypassing consultation would leave Julian without support and information. The warning is relevant to role viability. His agreement followed Julian's proposed explanation, so it is not independent confirmation of the motive. Source: [[engagement-history]], 14 September entry, including the 1 October addition.

**My inference under this assumption:** protecting authority, controlling information or resisting a family-sponsored appointment are credible explanations. Other undisclosed commercial or organisational objections remain possible. The official rationale earns no evidential weight in choosing between them. What matters for the job is that the earlier sponsorship did not secure acceptance, and practical support for a chairman override is still unestablished. This raises concern about cooperation without establishing a coordinated attempt to remove the Pudwills.

### 2. Current board power does not map cleanly onto nationality

TTI currently lists **13 directors: five executives and eight independents**. [TTI corporate information](https://www.ttigroup.com/investors/shareholder-services/corporate-information)

| People | Formal position | What can safely be inferred |
|---|---|---|

| Robert Getz, Peter Sullivan, Hans-Gerd Hesse, Virginia Wilmerding, Caroline Kracht, Andrew Roberts, Karen Ng and Stephen Wong | Eight independent directors | Potentially decisive on contested governance questions. Their biographies do not establish voting allegiance. |

Frank remains the publicly listed Group CFO; TTI's 2025 results presentation identifies **Ty as Group Deputy CFO**. Ty is absent from the current board list. His influence is operational, not a separate director vote. [Board biographies](https://www.ttigroup.com/company/our-board), [results presentation, slide 22](https://www.ttigroup.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/AR25_Analyst_Presentation.pdf)

### 3. Horst has substantial influence, not unilateral control

The June 2026 interim report discloses **21.90% aggregate interests for Horst**, covering personal, spouse, corporate and derivative interests; Stephan has 0.62%. These are disclosure figures, **not an audited count of immediately exercisable family votes**. Unvested awards and derivatives matter; simply adding percentages is unsafe. The holding is substantial but below a majority. Patrick Chan retired from the board on 31 May 2026 while continuing management duties. [Interim report, printed pp. 27-28 and 39](https://www.ttigroup.com/documents/interim-results-2026/files/TTI-2026IR-Full_Report-en.pdf)

The attachment's assertion that chairing nominations means Horst controls appointments is too strong. The current committee comprises Horst and six independents and recommends candidates to the board and shareholders. [Nomination committee](https://www.ttigroup.com/cg/committees)

The May 2026 articles distinguish three powers: the board appoints its chairman (Article 128), executive appointments can be terminated by the board (120-121), and shareholders can remove directors by ordinary resolution with special notice (113). Board decisions generally use majority votes, with a chairman's casting vote on a tie (131). **Losing an executive office, losing a board seat and losing ownership are different events.** These provisions make a board-led challenge feasible; they do not demonstrate one is being organised. [TTI articles](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0508/2026050800892.pdf)

### 4. Succession is the stronger structural concern

- **Business weight supports US management influence.** Milwaukee represented 70% of 2025 sales. Richman ran it for over 17 years. This supports his commercial standing, not a claim about hostile intent. [Results presentation, slide 18](https://www.ttigroup.com/sites/default/files/2026-03/AR25_Analyst_Presentation.pdf), [board biographies](https://www.ttigroup.com/company/our-board)
- **Performance cuts both ways.** H1 2026 revenue grew 5.9% and net profit 17.5%. Success may strengthen management's influence while reducing the incentive to provoke a destabilising confrontation. The inference that leadership feels under imminent replacement threat is not established by these public results. [H1 results](https://www.ttigroup.com/documents/interim-results-2026/en/)
- **Do not freeze today's people for six years.** TTI's published biographies list Horst at 81, Frank at 72 and Richman at 66. They do not substantiate the brief's 84-to-90 age range. Exact current ages, health and retirement timing require confirmation; age alone does not establish disengagement. The CEO's own succession also belongs in the scenario. [Board biographies](https://www.ttigroup.com/company/our-board)
- **Board renewal affects both presumed camps.** HKEX's transition ends with no independent director serving nine years or more by the first AGM on or after 1 July 2031. Today's independent lineup therefore cannot be extrapolated unchanged. This is renewal of independent status, not a requirement that every affected individual leave the board altogether. [HKEX guidance](https://www.hkex.com.hk/Listing/Sustainability/INED-Corner/Role-of-INEDs?sc_lang=en)

The reviewed evidence does not establish a Frank retirement date, a designated successor to Horst, Stephan's willingness to resist removal, or how family voting authority would operate after Horst steps back. A wealthy Stephan could disengage; he could also defend the family's investment through other directors. Neither should be assumed.

## What would make the job safer

These are proposed tests, not conditions Julian has adopted. As of 2 October, none is confirmed agreed.

| Cause -> event -> effect | Response | Evidence needed before relying on it |
|---|---|---|
| Chairman-backed entry without management support -> access/work is withheld -> you cannot deliver and your reputation suffers | **Reduce probability:** agree a named operating sponsor, first assignment, information access and escalation route | Steve/Ty and the affected IT owner acknowledge the actual remit and responsibilities; cooperation starts in practice, not merely a polite endorsement |
| Dependence on Horst -> sponsor withdraws or changes -> role is reduced or ends | **Reduce impact:** negotiate a company-backed financial floor covering early termination and material reduction of duties | Executed contract; employer and authorised signatory identified; HK employment counsel checks probation, notice, exclusions and enforceability, including sponsor-change scenarios |
| Unclear authority -> incompatible instructions or unfair performance tests -> blamed for outcomes you cannot control | **Reduce probability:** settle reporting line, budget owner, performance assessor and achievable measures | One agreed role charter, communicated to the people whose cooperation delivery requires |

All three effects are material to the job decision. Contract protection can reduce financial loss; it cannot guarantee useful work or prevent family disruption. Residual risk cannot be rated credibly until the actual terms and operating commitments exist.

**The practical test:** what has changed since the rejection, and who will own this role's success? Judge the answer through funded work, named responsibilities, actual information access and agreed protection. Another supportive explanation alone is not evidence that the problem has been resolved.

Reassess upward on a contested succession, changes in family voting control, a demonstrated director coalition, repeated refusal of access, or a role that remains private from its operating counterparts. Reassess downward on an openly supported remit, funded work, delivered access and contractual protection that does not depend on Horst personally.
