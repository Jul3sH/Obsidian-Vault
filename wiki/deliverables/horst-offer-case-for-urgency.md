# Horst Offer — The Case for Urgency

> **The argument for pressing TTI to decide, and which parts of it survive scrutiny.** Sourced from the tax analysis in [[uktax-srt-fy26-27|UK Tax - Statutory Residence Test FY26/27]], which holds the full workings, the statutory references and the peer-review record. **This file is the negotiation surface; that one is the evidence.**
>
> **Expect this to be checked.** Stephan is likely to pass any tax claim to TTI's own adviser, so every argument below is annotated with how to state it accurately. An overstatement that gets rebutted costs credibility at the worst possible moment.

*Written for the conversation with Stephan. The compliance deadlines in this document (9 Dec, 28 Jan, 9 Feb) are **useless in a negotiation** — an adviser would correctly answer "you have until the new year." The arguments below bite this autumn, and two of them are not tax arguments at all.*

> ### ⚠️ Keep the numbers out of the room
> **Do not quote any date or day count — 182, 120, 90, 46, December, February.** Every number invites *"so you have until X."* Make the case on **structure**: the closing exit window, the unlettable flat, and Sophia's schooling.

## 1. The decision point is late October — and delay is destroying one of only two options

**This is the real urgency, and it is six weeks away, not four months.**

There are two clean ways for the family to leave, and TTI's delay is closing one of them:

| | **Leave by late October** | **Leave at the end of the autumn term** |
|---|---|---|
| Sophia's schooling | ❌ **Mid-term withdrawal** — the damaging option | ✅ **Clean break at a natural boundary** |
| Flexibility in 2027/28 and 2028/29 | ✅ **Preserved in full** | ❌ **Cut by a third, for two years, irreversibly** |
| This tax year | ✅ Safe | ✅ Safe |

**Both work for this year. Neither works for everything.** And the choice between them cannot be made until TTI decides, because there is no reason to pull Sophia out mid-term for a job that may not exist.

> **So the cost of delay is not abstract: every week without a decision forces the family toward the option that costs two years of flexibility, because the other one becomes unconscionable for a 12-year-old.** Past late October the choice is gone altogether — the flexibility cannot be recovered afterwards.

**The sentence:** *"I need an answer this month, because after October my only clean exit is one that costs me two years of flexibility I can't get back. Before that, I have a choice. After it, I don't."*

## 2. The Hong Kong flat is sitting empty and cannot safely be let — a loss running now

**The most concrete cost of the delay, and it is accruing every month.**

The flat is the only thing preventing automatic UK residence this year. Letting it on a normal tenancy would destroy that, because a property let with exclusive possession stops being his home. So while the decision is unresolved:

- **The flat stays empty and unlet**
- **Every month is forgone rent**, and it is not recoverable
- *(Precisely: the constraint runs to roughly early March, after which a letting can no longer affect this tax year)*
- **It cannot be worked around while Sophia is here.** Even a replacement home for Julian does not fix her position — the flat is her home too, and a Hong Kong place she has never lived in is not hers

> **State it precisely or it will be rebutted:**
> - Say **"cannot *safely* be let"**, not "cannot be let". A letting that genuinely retains his right to occupy — a lodger, a part-let, a licence rather than a tenancy — can preserve home status, though it carries real risk.
> - It is **bounded**: from **early March** a letting can no longer affect this tax year. So the loss runs to roughly then, not indefinitely.
> - The honest formulation: *"I can't safely let it before March, and not at all while Sophia and I are both here."*

## 3. Sophia's schooling — the disruption worsens every week

**Not a tax argument, and possibly the most persuasive one.**

Sophia is settled in a UK school. Returning to Hong Kong means withdrawing her and placing her in a Hong Kong school:

- **The damage grows the longer she settles.** One term in is far easier than three
- **Hong Kong admissions have their own timetable**, which does not wait for TTI
- **Natural break points are passing.** Each term boundary that goes by without a decision forces a worse option
- **She returns to the UK for boarding in roughly two years anyway** — so this is a move out and back; the only question is how well it is handled

> **This is what makes argument 1 bite.** The two exit options are only a genuine choice while a mid-term departure is still thinkable. Every week of delay makes it less so.

## 4. The job start date — a January deadline, on their own timeline

> ### ⚠️ This has NOTHING to do with 2026/27 — be clear about that
> **The full-time-work-abroad test is assessed over a single tax year**, not a rolling period: HMRC's wording is *"the relevant year means the whole of the tax year that is being considered."*
>
> **For 2026/27 it is already dead and cannot be revived.** No overseas work was done from 6 April to late September — roughly 170 days — which is a significant break *inside* this tax year, and the test requires none across the whole year. **Even a job starting tomorrow would not rescue 2026/27.**
>
> **So there is no need for the job to start before 5 April 2027.** Starting earlier does nothing for this year. The only date that matters is **6 April 2027**, and it is purely about 2027/28. (Starting slightly before 6 April is marginally better, giving margin on the break rule, but is not required.)
>
> **The one way a job could still affect 2026/27 is negatively:** Hong Kong-employer work performed physically in the UK creates UK workdays. Forty days of more than 3 hours gives a **work tie** — a second tie, dropping the ceiling from 182 days to 120 — and sustained full-time UK work would trigger automatic residence outright.

**Secondary, but it closes off "we'll get to it."**

If the Hong Kong role is to give Julian the clean route to non-residence in 2027/28 — the one that does not depend on ties, or on how HMRC characterises his Hong Kong home — it must **start at the beginning of the UK tax year in April**. *(Strictly the outside edge is early May — a start after that leaves a 31-day gap and fails. Lead with April; if an adviser points to May, concede it rather than defend April, and note a May start also needs ~38 working hours a week for the rest of the year against ~35 from April.)* Working back through visa, contract, relocation and a Hong Kong school place, **that requires a signed offer by around January.**

> **State this accurately.** It is a hard deadline **for the clean route, not for non-residence itself**. Missing it does not make him UK resident; it drops him onto the day-count route for a year. An accountant will spot the difference, so say: *"April is the deadline for the clean route — the one that doesn't depend on counting days or on how HMRC views my Hong Kong home. Miss it and I spend the whole year on the fragile one."*
>
> ⚠️ **It is not a route free of day counting.** The test carries its own limits — fewer than 91 UK days and fewer than 31 UK workdays. What it avoids is the *ties* analysis and the dependence on the Hong Kong flat's characterisation, not day counting as such. An adviser will correct the looser version.
>
> A late start also raises the bar independently: the idle weeks still count in the hours calculation, so a May start needs roughly 38 working hours a week for the rest of the year, and a July start roughly 45.

**Why it matters here:** even on TTI's own preferred timeline, a decision is needed by January. **So the question is not whether to decide soon — it is whether to decide in time to avoid the October losses as well.**

## What NOT to raise

- **The December and February compliance lines.** They are the genuine deadlines but they sound comfortable, and raising them invites *"so you have until February."*
- **The Hong Kong flat as a technical residence risk.** Raise the empty-and-unlettable cost, not the exposure — the latter is within Julian's control and creates no pressure on anyone else.
- **Tax cost figures.** Unquantified (§9.8), and Hong Kong taxes the income either way with UK credit, so the number is smaller than it sounds.
- **"A hard deadline that costs the whole tax year"** without *"for the clean route"* (point 4).
- **"I cannot let the flat"** — say *"cannot safely let it before March"* (point 2).
- **Any specific date or day count.** See the warning at the top.

## Rebuttals to have ready

**1. *"Just stay under 16 nights at your mother's and you get the flexibility back."***
True in law — the accommodation tie needs 16 or more nights. **Answer:** it is a personal choice already made; staying under 16 nights means hotels for most of a visit whose purpose is seeing Sophia. Concede the mechanism, decline the trade.

**2. *"If we say yes, you don't need the flexibility. So our timing only matters if the answer is no — why is that our problem?"***
**This is the sharpest rebuttal and the case should state the answer before it is asked.** The cost is not the *answer*, it is *when* the answer comes. **A "no" in October costs far less than a "no" in December** — by December the clean-exit option has gone, and the relocation and schooling costs are higher too — because by then the option to leave cleanly has gone. So the delay is only free if the answer turns out to be yes, and nobody knows that yet. **TTI is asking Julian to carry the cost of their optionality.**

**3. *"There is a third option — stay through the tax year."***
There is, and it costs the same two years of flexibility. **Do not raise it**, and note the trap in it: it is **not** enough for Julian to leave before Sophia becomes resident. Ties are assessed across the whole tax year, so if she becomes resident at any point and he has exceeded 120 days, **his earlier departure does not save him**. Staying later is therefore riskier than it sounds, not merely a delayed decision.

**4. *"What flexibility, exactly?"***
The number will come out if pressed. **Have the one-liner rather than the table:** *"the UK days I can spend in each of the next two years drop by about a third."* Do not offer 182 or 120.

## The case in one paragraph

> *"I need an answer this month. Until you decide I can't pull Sophia out of school, and after October the only clean exit left is one that costs me two years of flexibility I can't recover — so the delay itself is making the decision for me. Meanwhile my Hong Kong flat sits empty because I can't safely let it while we're both here, and that's money gone every month. And for the role to work properly I need to be in post at the start of the tax year in April, which on a practical view of visa, contract and schooling timelines means terms agreed around January."*
