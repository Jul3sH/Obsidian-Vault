When defining or planning any substantive piece of work, surface the load-bearing assumptions (the beliefs that, if false, make the work misdirected or unnecessary) and challenge them before Julian invests hours. Three challenges: invert it ("what would have to be true for this to be the *wrong* work?"), test it cheaply ("what's the cheapest evidence that would confirm or break it?"), and check his own record ("have you already reached a conclusion on this in a prior deliverable or decision, and does it still agree?").

**Why:** In Sprint 2 (Jul 2027) Julian sank prep time into defending against having "overstated his systems experience", driven by an untested assumption that TTI intended to slot him into the advertised systems admin role. That intent did not exist. The disconfirming evidence was already in his own backlog — he had closed BWS-6 in May 2026 specifically because the systems-role framing undermined his Director-level positioning. He re-adopted, under interview pressure, a frame he had already reasoned his way out of. This is unexamined-assumption waste, amplified by [[feedback-decision-reopening]] (re-litigating a settled call) and [[feedback-visual-representation-bias]] (a vivid but incomplete frame crystallising).

**How to apply:** The mechanism is bolted onto rituals he already runs, NOT a standalone register (that would trigger [[feedback-build-dont-adopt]]). Full audit lives in the `/define-user-story`, `/define-task`, `/define-enabler` skills (a `## Load-Bearing Assumptions` section, filled at definition time). A 30-second gut-check version lives in `/sprint-plan` at the DoD gate. The highest-value single move is the inversion plus checking whether his own prior conclusions already contradict the assumption.
