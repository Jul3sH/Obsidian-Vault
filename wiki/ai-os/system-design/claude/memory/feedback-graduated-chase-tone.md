# Graduated tone when chasing a stalled contact

**The pattern:** When re-engaging a contact after a missed deadline or stretch of
silence, Julian's default is NOT to lead with directness or a dated ask. The first
follow-up stays warm and light - a soft check-in, not a forcing question. Directness
is reserved as an escalation, deployed only if the deadline keeps slipping. He does
not want to burn the "polite chase" card by opening firm.

**Corrected instance:** TTI - Kari missed his own self-set decision deadline (end of
week of 28 Jul). Claude proposed a dated, semi-forcing first follow-up ("You'd hoped
to have a decision by end of last week - where has it landed, and is there a date I
can work to?"). Julian overruled it: keep the first touch soft ("Any line of sight
yet on where things have landed?"), and only turn direct if the week passes with
nothing back.

**Second half of the correction - don't pre-plan follow-ups before a response
lands.** Julian also does not want proposed next actions (a call, a document, a
specific ask) baked into a message that's still waiting on a reply. The right
follow-up depends on what actually comes back, so it should be assessed reactively
once the response is in hand, not pre-committed.

**Why it matters:** This sits alongside, but is distinct from,
[[feedback-commitment-forcing]] (push Julian himself to commit, impose deadlines).
Chasing a *third party* runs on different logic: force too hard, too early, and a
risk-averse contact reads it as pressure and disengages. The forcing-function
instinct is right for Julian's own decisions; it is wrong applied wholesale to
someone else's inbox.

**How to apply:**
- First re-engagement after a missed deadline or silence: warm, light, no dated ask,
  no proposed next step. Just re-open the line.
- Escalate to direct/dated language only once a further, meaningful stretch has
  passed with still no answer - not on the first touch.
- Do not propose the follow-up action in a message that's still waiting on a reply.
  Hold that decision until the actual response lands, then assess what it warrants.
- Applies to chasing external stakeholders generally (job leads, favours, decisions
  owed by someone else), not to decisions Julian himself needs to make - that case is
  [[feedback-commitment-forcing]], the reverse pattern.
