# MM: Confirmation Bias

This is the Confirmation Bias mental model: the recurring pattern of building a case
for a position already held, rather than genuinely testing whether it's wrong. It
exists because this pattern surfaced repeatedly across the lessons-learnt archive,
personal decisions, technical troubleshooting, and post-feedback self-sabotage, and
was never captured as its own model despite being named explicitly by Julian himself
multiple times. Read it whenever a position feels settled and the next move is to
build support for it rather than test it.

**One-liner:** You will find what you're looking for, so look for the disconfirming
case.

**Reach for it when:** you notice you're gathering evidence or arguments for a
position you've already reached, rather than genuinely testing whether it's wrong.

## Key Takeaways

- Building a persuasion case for your own position is not the same as testing
  whether you're right.
- A preferred theory changes what you notice; the disconfirming detail is often
  available, just not being looked for.
- Confirmation bias shows up in personal decisions, technical troubleshooting, and
  self-sabotage after negative feedback, it is not just a decision-making bias.
- After critical feedback, the instinct to build a case for quitting or disengaging
  is confirmation bias directed at yourself.

## Principles

- **Test, don't build a case.** If the next action is persuasion rather than
  evaluation, the decision is probably already made and being justified, not
  examined.
- **A preferred theory filters what you see.** Once a theory is preferred, the
  evidence that fits it gets noticed and the evidence that doesn't gets skipped.
- **Ask for the simpler explanation.** In technical or factual troubleshooting, an
  elaborate wrong theory usually means a simpler, disconfirming explanation was
  passed over because it didn't fit the story already being built.
- **Negative feedback is a trigger, not just an event.** A confirmation-bias search
  for reasons to quit or disengage after criticism is a distinct, predictable
  pattern, watch for it specifically at that moment.

## Guidelines

- Before finalising a position, deliberately construct the strongest case for the
  other side with the same creativity spent on your own.
- When troubleshooting and the explanation is getting complicated, stop and ask
  whether a simpler explanation was dismissed early because it didn't fit the
  working theory.
- After receiving critical feedback, treat any sudden case for quitting or
  disengaging as a signal to check for confirmation bias, not as a fresh, objective
  conclusion.

## Limitations

This model names the pattern and the trigger points where it recurs; it doesn't
provide a structured debiasing technique (e.g. a formal pre-mortem or red-team
process), that would be the next layer if the pattern keeps recurring. It also
doesn't distinguish confirmation bias from legitimate conviction, holding a view
firmly after genuinely testing it is not the failure mode this model targets.
