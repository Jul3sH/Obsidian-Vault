# MM: Stories Aren't Evidence

This is the Stories Aren't Evidence mental model: the habit of filling an information
gap (a silence, an unexplained decision, someone's motives) with an invented
narrative, and then treating that narrative as a read of the situation. It was
created from the TTI engagement, which Julian's own review found was characterised
by it: "I told myself a lot of stories that were largely speculation." Read it
whenever a gap in the facts is being explained by a story about what other people
are thinking or doing.

**One-liner:** The story that fills a silence is speculation, not a read - label it
before it hardens into fact.

**Reach for it when:** you catch yourself explaining a silence, a delay, or someone
else's motives with a narrative nobody has actually confirmed.

## Key Takeaways

- A gap in the facts gets filled automatically; the filling is a story, and stories
  arrive feeling like reads.
- Price the boring explanation first: busy, on holiday, mid-process. It is usually
  right and never the one the anxious story picks.
- A story built on unchecked details steers real decisions until the details are
  checked; check them before the story steers anything.
- Stories can still be useful: tell them if they help, but stamp SPECULATION on
  them in the record so they inform without hardening into fact.

## Principles

- **Silence is data-free.** It supports every story equally, which means it
  supports none. The longer the silence, the more elaborate the invented
  explanation tends to get; elaborateness is a property of the story, not of the
  evidence.
- **The mundane explanation goes first.** Before reading motive, threat, or
  rejection into a gap, ask what the dullest structural cause would be (busy,
  travelling, a process running on its own clock) and what it would look like -
  usually identical to what is being observed.
- **Check the load-bearing details.** A narrative that steers positioning or
  strategy earns a fact-check of the specific claims it rests on; if they fail,
  withdraw it formally rather than letting it fade.
- **Label speculation at the moment of telling.** The working practice that
  functioned by the end of TTI: state the story, say "this is speculation, we will
  never know", and log it that way. Labelled stories inform judgement; unlabelled
  ones impersonate facts.

## Guidelines

- When a silence stretches, write down the boring explanation next to the dramatic
  one before acting on either.
- Before letting a narrative shape a message or a decision, name the checkable
  details it rests on and check them.
- In any log or record, mark story-content as speculation explicitly, so a later
  reading (by you or an agent) cannot mistake it for an established fact.
- Notice narrative escalation under anxiety: "no reply yet" becoming "it's game
  over" is the pattern firing, not new information arriving.

## Limitations

This model covers inventing the story that fills a gap. Its siblings cover the
neighbouring failures: [[mm-confirmation-bias]] (defending a story already held by
gathering only supporting evidence), [[mm-visual-representation-bias]] (the vivid
mental image standing in for analysis), and the relational-paranoia rows in
[[mm-never-react]] (the emotional cost of living inside an unchecked story). It
also does not say stories are worthless - hypotheses drive good investigation;
the failure mode is the unlabelled hypothesis promoted to fact.
