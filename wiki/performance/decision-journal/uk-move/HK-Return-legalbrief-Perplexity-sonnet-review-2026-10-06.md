## Supported points worth keeping

- Permission is needed from everyone with parental responsibility, or a court, before taking a child abroad (fn 4, 7).
- A prohibited steps order, all-ports alert and passport measures are available to a parent fearing removal (fn 1, 3).
- Permanent relocation from Hong Kong needs a court application; temporary removal under the order needs written consent plus a general undertaking (fn 14, 15).
- MIAM exemption for urgency includes risk of unlawful removal (fn 17, 30).
- Hong Kong maintenance enforcement routes and the 12-month leave rule (fn 27).

## Key Takeaways

- About half the claims are only partly supported, and the two worst gaps sit on the points that matter most: the criminal offence (outdated statute text) and jurisdiction (2015 sources).
- One cited case (AR v RN) undercuts the "temporary move" argument Reunite relied on.
- Nothing here says the report's conclusions are wrong; it says several are less well sourced than they look.
