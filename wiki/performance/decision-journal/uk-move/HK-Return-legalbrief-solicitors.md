# HK Return: Solicitor Consultations

## Purpose

The questions Julian asks each family solicitor about taking Sophia from England back to Hong Kong, and each solicitor's answers. It exists to compare their experience, advice, timeline and costs before instructing one.

Sections: questions; responses by solicitor (VWV first).

> As of 9 Oct 2026: two consultations held. VWV not proceeding; Weightmans (Dipika Mistry) is the lead so far, not yet instructed. Send [[HK-Return-legalbrief-Fable]] to each solicitor beforehand; supporting evidence in [[HK-Return-whatsapp-evidence]].

## Consultation Questions

### 1. Experience

1. How many international relocation cases have you run in the last two years? Any involving Hong Kong, or Hague Convention return claims?
2. Who would actually do the work, how experienced are they, and what is their hourly rate?

### 2. Provisional view on my position

3. Can I leave without Clodagh's consent? Is it a criminal offence under s1 Child Abduction Act 1984 whatever Sophia's habitual residence?
4. If I make a full proposal and she refuses, is the "unreasonably refused" defence (s1(5)(c)) realistic?
5. With consent: what form must it take (written, made into a consent order)? Does the 2020 Hong Kong order also need varying in Hong Kong?
6. Is Sophia habitually resident in England now? Will she be by Christmas?
7. What is your provisional view of the likely outcome if it went to court?

### 3. If she refuses consent

8. What are the stages of a specific issue application (MIAM, first hearing, Cafcass report, final hearing)?
9. How long to a final hearing? Can it be expedited for a 4 January 2027 school start?
10. What are the costs per stage and the total range? Is there a fixed-fee option?
11. Could Clodagh get legal aid?
12. Can she get an order stopping Sophia leaving while the case runs?

### 4. Scope and fees if I instruct you

13. What is your fixed fee and turnaround to draft a consent proposal letter to Clodagh and a draft consent order?
14. What do you need from me?

Not proceeding with VWV; I will probably call the specialists she named.

**Three issues she summarised, which Julian agreed with:**
1. Habitual residence: his later stated intention to stay (above) may be enough to make Sophia habitually resident in the UK.
2. Getting Clodagh's consent.
3. Protection if he leaves: Clodagh might try to take Sophia from Hong Kong. This needs the existing Hong Kong order amended and a new order drafted. Julian chose to keep this out for now: he wants the minimum asked of Clodagh, focused only on Sophia returning to Hong Kong without a block. Dipika's caution: if Clodagh rejects the consent order, all three may have to be dealt with together. Julian's position on 9 Oct: separate for now.

**Julian's read (9 Oct 2026):** The most comfortable conversation so far. She made common-sense suggestions without being prompted, starting with a letter to Clodagh on the benefits to her. The spiel on why a decent lawyer matters felt a little like a pitch, but she is a partner and he agrees it is win or lose, so getting it right first time matters; he thinks he can manage the cost if he engages her. Best feeling of the solicitors so far. Compared with VWV and Stowe Family Law (his note here was unclear: see below).

**Open (9 Oct 2026):** Julian's note ended "VMV and Stow Family Law" after the rate. Unclear whether that means Stowe Family Law is another solicitor to consult or a comparison. Not yet confirmed.
