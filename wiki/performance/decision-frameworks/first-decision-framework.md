# FIRST Decision Framework

## Purpose

FIRST is Julian's five-step process for small decisions that need an answer now: Facts, Issue, Repercussions, Scenarios, Then. It exists because it is the first framework to reach for on any decision, and the one that shows when a decision has grown too big for it and needs [[brain-brand-framework|BRAIND]].

> As of 1 Oct 2026: built by Julian in session, from Sharran Srivatsaa's four steps with a 10-10-10 check added between the risk step and the next steps. One worked example. Not a published method.

## Key Takeaways

- Start every decision here. FIRST validates an action in minutes; BRAIND evaluates one option in depth and locks it. Move to BRAIND when getting it wrong is expensive or hard to undo, or when the issue will not fit in a sentence.
- Step 3 names what breaks for certain the moment you act. Step 4 asks what could go wrong later. In the vocabulary of the risk framework, step 3 is issues and step 4 is risks.
- The step that most often changes the outcome is step 2. If the issue is already stated in one sentence, the rest confirms and takes ten minutes.
- Every step is answered in Julian's words, one line each.

## The five steps

| Step | Question | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **F**acts | What do we know, and where is the shared information? | The context, laid out as facts with their sources |
| 2. **I**ssue | What am I actually solving? | The problem, not the symptom |
| 3. **R**epercussions | If I do this, what breaks the moment I act? | Issues: certain consequences, accepted with eyes open |
| 4. **S**cenarios | How will what breaks look in ten minutes, ten months and ten years, and what else could go wrong by then? | Risks: uncertain consequences, judged at three distances |
| 5. **T**hen | What are the next steps, in order, with dates? | The commitment |

## When to use it, and when to move on

**The plain test (Julian, 1 Oct 2026, corrected the same evening).** Both frameworks look at one thing. FIRST validates an action about to be taken: am I doing the right thing, what breaks now, what breaks later? BRAIND evaluates one option in depth: what it gives, what it costs, what the gut is relying on, and whether to commit, with the outcome locked. Its Alternatives section names the other options, any of which may become a BRAIND of its own. The difference is depth, stakes and the lock, not the shape of the question. Small and undoable: FIRST. Expensive or hard to undo if wrong: BRAIND.

| Signal | Framework |
|---|---|
| The issue fits in a sentence, the choice can be undone or is modest, an answer is needed today | FIRST |
| The issue will not fit in a sentence after step 2 | Stay at step 2 until it does, or move to BRAIND |
| Options need widening, or the choice cannot be undone, or it earns a journal entry | [[brain-brand-framework|BRAIND]] |
| Hours or days are available and the stakes are high | [[seven-step-decision-process|Seven-step process]] |

## Worked example: the message to Stephan, 1 Oct 2026

| Step | Julian's answer |
|---|---|
| Facts | Horst said on 13 and 14 Sep the job would happen and accepted the number; at the lunch he spoke of a contract put in place quietly and backdated to September, and told Stephan not to consult Ty. Stephan has since sought Ty's buy-in; Ty's chain said no on 20 Aug. Three asks of 26 Sep unanswered. A DBIS start on 20 Oct needs terms by about 9 Oct; terms by 27 Oct or waiting stops; the flat offer may be lost this week. Direct, dated asks have got Stephan's fastest replies |
| Issue | "Stephan's procrastination. He needs to pull the trigger and get HR to draft a contract. Instead he's looking for buy-in from Ty." |
| Repercussions | Option chosen: message Stephan and Horst the same night. "The only thing that would break is that Stephan could get agitated that I've gone round his back." Added from the risk sheet: a contract forced through by his father may mean a boss who was made to take him |
| Scenarios | To be filled by Julian after the messages are sent (first use of the step) |
| Then | Stephan's message first, with the 9 Oct date for Sophia's start and a line that Horst will be updated; Horst's message after it; read Justin's report first if it lands; act on the Friday date as set |

What the run changed: the message's target (Stephan not doing what his father told him, rather than whether TTI will hire him) and one missing fact recovered for the record. It did not change the option Julian had already chosen.

## Limits

- It does not widen options. Bring the options to it.
- Step 4 checks a framed choice; it cannot repair a misframed issue. If the ten-month view shows a different problem, go back to step 2.
- On a decision already clear in the head, it confirms rather than changes, and costs about ten minutes.
