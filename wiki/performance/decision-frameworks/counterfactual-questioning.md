# Counterfactual Questioning

## Purpose

Counterfactual questioning is a technique for finding out which factor is driving a gut feeling, by removing one factor at a time and asking what I would choose. It exists because my intuition often mixes several motives into one feeling, and I cannot weigh them until they are separated.

> As of 29 Sep 2026: built in session by the model and agreed with Julian. One use so far. It is not a published method. The name borrows from counterfactual thinking, which in psychology usually means past events; here the questions are hypotheticals about a present choice.

**Map:** Key Takeaways · When to reach for it · How to run it · Forms of the question · Limits · Links

## Key Takeaways

- It answers one question: which factor is causing this feeling? It does not say whether that factor is well founded.
- If the feeling stays when a factor is removed, something else is enough to cause it. The removed factor may still contribute.
- Test two factors together only where one depends on the other.
- Write the list of runs before starting, then add no more. An open-ended list is scenario-churn ([[decision-maker-profile]]).
- It hands over to [[belief-assumption-testing]]: this tool finds the driver, that tool tests it.

## When to reach for it

**The trigger:** I have a strong feeling about a choice, I can name two or more possible reasons for it, and I cannot tell which one is doing the work.

| Setting | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Inside a BRAIND | In the Intuition step, when the gut pulls more than one way | "My gut instinct is telling me not to do it", with guilt, fear of a mistake and a real read of the risk all possible causes ([[HK-Return-Counterfactuals]]) |
| Standalone, small decision | The choice is too small for a BRAIND but the motive is unclear | Accepting an invitation, taking on a piece of work, a purchase |
| Standalone, avoidance | I am reluctant to do something and have several explanations | "If the call were certain to go well, would I still put it off?" |
| Standalone, motive check | I want something and suspect the reason is not the stated one | The silence test in [[payoff-vs-prestige-bias]] removes the audience |

**Skip it when** the gut is clear, or only one factor is in play.

## How to run it

1. Write the feeling in one line.
2. List the factors that could be causing it.
3. Write the full list of runs now. One run per factor, plus one run for each pair of factors that depend on each other.
4. For each run, ask one question that changes that factor and holds everything else as it is.
5. Answer in one line, with the first reaction. Record it in my own words.
6. Read the result.

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| The feeling stays | Something else is enough to cause it on its own. This factor may still contribute |
| The feeling goes | That factor is the cause, or part of it |
| The feeling shrinks | It contributes, and something else does too |

7. Stop when the list is done. Take the driver to [[belief-assumption-testing]].

## Forms of the question

| Form | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Remove | Takes the factor away | "With nothing to feel guilty about, would I sign?" |
| Equalise | Makes the factor the same in every option | "Same job and pay, based in Malvern. Relief or disappointment?" |
| Guarantee | Makes an uncertain factor certain | "If six years were guaranteed in writing, would I go?" |

## Limits

- **Two causes that are each enough.** Removing either one leaves the feeling, so a "stays" result never clears a factor.
- **Changing one thing often changes others.** Moving a job to another country also moves the risks and the people affected. Say what else changed before reading the answer.
- **Linked factors.** Removing one factor at a time misses factors that work together, such as money that only arrives if the job lasts. Test those as a pair.
- **An imagined answer is not a lived one.** The first reaction to a hypothetical is evidence about the feeling. It is not a forecast of how I would act.
- **It does not test truth.** A driver can be real and still rest on a false belief.
- **It can become a way of not deciding.** Four factors give fifteen possible combinations to remove. The written, closed list is the defence.
