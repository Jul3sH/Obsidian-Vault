# Choosing a Decision Framework

There are many decision frameworks. The risk with ADHD is analysis paralysis - spending more time choosing a framework than making the decision. This article solves that with a two-question filter.

## The Default (Julian, 4 Sep 2026)

**BRAIND is the default framework for any decision that gets a journal entry** ([[braind-framework]]: Benefits, Risks, Alternatives, Intuition, Need time, Decision - the union of BRAIN and BRAND, first used on [[dec-uk-move|the UK-move decision]], where it was spelled "BRAINED"; BRAIND is the standard spelling from 4 Sep 2026). The filter below still applies to quick, easily-reversible, in-the-moment calls that never reach the journal - that is where the Sharran default holds. A journalled decision using anything other than BRAIND records why in its entry.

## The First Pass (Julian, 1 Oct 2026)

**Start every decision with [[first-decision-framework|FIRST]]**: Facts, Issue, Repercussions, Scenarios, Then. It takes minutes and validates an action about to be taken. Both FIRST and BRAIND look at one thing; BRAIND evaluates one option in depth, tests the beliefs under it and locks the outcome, and its Alternatives section may spawn further BRAINDs. Move to BRAIND when getting it wrong is expensive or hard to undo, or when the issue will not fit in a sentence. Sharran's four steps are superseded by it and are kept only as a source inside the FIRST page.

## The Two-Question Filter

**Q1: Do I know what problem I am actually solving?**

- **No - still fuzzy** → Run [[first-decision-framework|FIRST]] steps 1 and 2 first (Facts, Issue). Then return here.
- **Yes - clear** → Go to Q2.

**Q2: Is this decision reversible?**

- **Yes, easily reversible** → Use [[first-decision-framework|FIRST]]. Act fast, get to next steps, review later.
- **No, hard to undo or medium-high stakes** → Go to Q3.

**Q3: Do I have time to research?**

- **No (under an hour)** → Use [[braind-framework|BRAIND]]. Structured evaluation without deep research.
- **Yes (hours to days)** → Use the [[seven-step-decision-process|7-step process]]. Full deliberate process.

## Framework Comparison

| | [[first-decision-framework\|FIRST]] | [[braind-framework\|BRAIND]] | [[seven-step-decision-process\|7-step]] |
|--|--|--|--|
| **Speed** | Minutes | 15-60 min | Hours to days |
| **Best for** | Validating one action, now | One option in depth, pre-commitment, locked | Complex or irreversible |
| **Covers alternatives?** | No | Yes | Yes |
| **Covers second-order effects?** | Yes, the 10-10-10 step | Through R and I | Yes |
| **Covers review/learning?** | No | No | Yes |
| **ADHD-friendly?** | High | Medium | Low (without structure) |

## The ADHD Rule

**When in doubt, default to FIRST.** A committed next step made in 5 minutes beats a perfect decision made never. The review step (Step 7 of the 7-step process) exists precisely to course-correct - you don't need the first decision to be perfect.

Irreversible decisions are the exception. For anything that cannot be undone - career moves, financial commitments, relationship endings - slow down and use BRAND or the 7-step process regardless of time pressure.

## Key Takeaways

- Analysis paralysis is itself a decision (to do nothing). Treat it as one.
- The two-question filter takes under 30 seconds. Run it before opening any framework.
- Most decisions are more reversible than they feel in the moment. Test that assumption before escalating to a heavier framework.
- No framework covers everything. FIRST skips alternatives; BRAND skips execution; 7-step skips speed. Knowing the gaps prevents over-reliance on any one tool.
