# MM: Facts First

This card is about the order of work: gathering the facts an analysis depends on
**before** the analysis starts, rather than discovering them while it runs. Written
after the UK residence analysis of 23-24 Sept 2026, where six facts arrived mid-run
and the headline answer reversed three times, each reversal throwing away work that
was already finished and reviewed.

**One-liner:** Every fact that arrives mid-analysis invalidates the work already done.

**Reach for it when:** starting any analysis whose conclusion depends on someone's
circumstances - their history, their assets, their commitments, their intentions.

**Position:** before the chain. Admission decides ceremony and Prompt Zero fixes
scope; this fixes the *inputs*. A correctly-scoped analysis on an incomplete fact
base still has to be rebuilt.

## Key Takeaways

- A late fact does not add to an analysis, it **reopens** it - and everything built
  on the old picture has to be re-checked, including work already reviewed.
- The cost is not linear. Each reversal also invalidates the reviews of the work it
  replaces, so the waste compounds with how far you had got.
- Models will not ask for the fact base unprompted. They answer the question in front
  of them with whatever they have, and sound equally confident either way.
- The facts that break an analysis are rarely exotic. They are the ordinary ones
  nobody thought to state: where you sleep, what you own, who lives with you.
- **Volunteering facts is cheap; being asked for them one at a time is not.**

## Principles

- **Close the fact base before opening the analysis.** Spend the first pass listing
  what the answer could possibly turn on, and fill it. Analysis built on a moving
  base is rework waiting to happen.
- **Ask what would change the answer, then go and find it.** The useful question at
  the start is not "what do I know" but "what fact, if it turned out differently,
  would reverse this". Those are the ones to establish first.
- **An assumed fact is a fact until someone says otherwise.** Anything not confirmed
  is an assumption, and assumptions propagate silently into conclusions that read as
  settled. Mark them at the point of use, not in a register.
- **The obvious facts are the dangerous ones.** Exotic details get questioned;
  everyday ones are assumed. "I'm staying with my mother" changed an entire
  residence analysis and had never been asked about.
- **Rework is the real cost, not the asking.** Ten minutes of questions at the start
  is cheaper than any single reversal later, and reversals arrive in clusters.

## Guidelines

- Open with a fact-gathering pass and say that is what it is. Do not begin analysing
  until it is done, even if an answer seems obvious.
- Write the fact base down where the conclusions live, so any conclusion can be
  traced to an input. A separate note is a note nobody re-reads.
- Distinguish **established** from **assumed** in that list, visibly. An assumption
  recorded as a fact is worse than an assumption not recorded at all.
- When a fact does arrive late, say plainly what it invalidates. Silently rewriting
  the conclusion hides that the reasoning moved.
- For anything with a deadline, establish the facts that set the deadline **first**.
  Discovering them late means discovering them after a date has passed.

## Limitations

- Exploratory work legitimately discovers what matters as it goes; the model applies
  to analysis with a defined question, not to open-ended thinking.
- Some facts genuinely cannot be known upfront - a decision not yet made, a
  negotiation not yet concluded. Name those as open and model both branches, rather
  than assuming one and rebuilding later.
- Taken too far this becomes interrogation before any value is shown. Front-load the
  facts that could reverse the answer, not every fact conceivably relevant.

## Detail

Instance: the UK residence analysis in [[uktax-srt-fy26-27]]. Six facts arrived
mid-run - the UK home at his mother's, the London property being let, the 16 nights
a year, Sophia's schooling plan, the Hong Kong job, and her 2024/25 day count. The
last was the most serious: it sat in the document's own open-items register marked
"worth confirming" for a day, when it was a prerequisite capable of reversing the
answer for both people.
