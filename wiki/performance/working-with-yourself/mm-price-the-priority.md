# MM: Price the Priority

This is the Price the Priority mental model: prioritising by what a task actually
costs to skip, not by how interesting or comfortable it is. It exists because
subjective interest reliably beats objective importance unless a real cost is
attached to the comparison. Read it when a priority decision is being made on how a
task feels rather than what it costs. The full detail lives in
[[prioritisation-and-scheduling]].

**One-liner:** Prioritise by tangible cost, not by interest or comfort.

**Reach for it when:** you notice a priority decision is being driven by what feels
interesting, safe, or comfortable rather than by what it actually costs to get wrong.

## Key Takeaways

- Subjective prioritisation drifts toward comfortable work; objective prioritisation
  ties the decision to a real cost.
- Fear disguises itself as reprioritisation, the safe, technical, or familiar task
  gets chosen over the uncomfortable, high-stakes one.
- Denial about urgency is a distinct failure from misjudging priority, facing the
  daily financial cost of inaction is its own separate discipline.
- Discomfort is not a valid reason to cut a negotiation or a priority decision short;
  the forgone amount is concrete and usually larger than the discomfort.

## Principles

- **Attach a number.** "If I don't do X by Y, it costs me £Z or opportunity A" turns
  a vague priority call into a real one.
- **Interest is not a priority signal.** The work you find interesting is not more
  important for being interesting.
- **Name the fear when it's driving the choice.** Studying instead of applying,
  preparing the safe thing instead of the scary thing, are usually fear wearing the
  costume of prioritisation.
- **Denial has its own cost.** Being in denial about urgency (a job search, a
  deadline) is a separate problem from simply misjudging which task matters more.

## Guidelines

- Before choosing between two priorities, ask what each one actually costs if
  skipped, in money, time, or a missed window, not just how each one feels.
- When avoiding an uncomfortable task in favour of a comfortable one, check whether
  fear is doing the deciding.
- Don't let discomfort in the moment (a negotiation, a hard conversation) cut short
  a decision whose upside is concrete and larger than the discomfort.

## Limitations

This model prices what to work on; it doesn't guard the time once chosen; that's
[[mm-guard-the-critical-path]]. It also can't manufacture urgency where none is
felt, denial about a real cost has to be named before pricing it helps.
