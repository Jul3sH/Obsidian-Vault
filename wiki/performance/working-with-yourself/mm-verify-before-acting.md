# MM: Verify Before Acting

This is the epistemic half of the old combined "never react" pattern: checking
whether something is actually true before you act on it, announce it, or challenge
someone over it. It was split from the emotional-regulation half, which is now
[[mm-never-react]]. Read it before you respond to, challenge, or build on anything
you haven't independently checked.

**One-liner:** However convincing it looks, verify it before you act on it.

**Reach for it when:** you're about to respond, challenge someone publicly,
announce something, or make a decision based on something you haven't
independently checked.

## Key Takeaways

- Certainty is not evidence. The feeling of "obviously true" precedes some of the
  worst calls: a colleague wrongly challenged in public, a costly assumption, a
  late arrival.
- Verifying costs little; acting wrongly in public costs a lot, in credibility, in
  money, or both.
- The discipline is proactive: check agendas, attendee lists, and details before
  they matter, not after something has already gone wrong.
- Silence beats a premature announcement or update: wait until you're certain, not
  until you're keen to look good.

## Principles

- Strong evidence is not proof. Overwhelming coincidence still deserves due
  diligence before you act on it.
- Verify before you challenge anyone publicly: being right in private and wrong in
  public costs more than being slow.
- Resist the urge to announce or update before the facts are locked; premature good
  news, or premature progress, is still premature.
- Distinguish "I assume" from "I know" explicitly when a decision has real cost.
- Getting details wrong (a name, an agenda, an attendee list) reads as disorganised
  even when it was just an unchecked assumption.

## Guidelines

- Before responding to anything that feels emotionally loaded or "obviously true,"
  ask what you'd need to see to be wrong.
- Check details involving your boss especially closely; assumptions there cost the
  most.
- Choose fewer, later updates over frequent, early ones when the underlying picture
  is still moving.
- When a decision has financial or relational weight, name your source: is this a
  fact, or an assumption you haven't tested?

## Limitations

This model is about checking facts, not about managing the urge to respond; that
discipline is [[mm-never-react]]. It also doesn't cover how to verify cheaply at
scale when the output is AI-generated; that's the separate [[mm-verification]]
model. This is the one-off, human version of the same idea.
