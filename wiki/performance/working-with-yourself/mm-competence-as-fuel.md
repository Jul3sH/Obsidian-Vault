# MM: Competence as Fuel

This is the Competence as Fuel mental model: confidence is a byproduct of delivering
and building things, not a precondition you need before you're allowed to act. It
exists because the causal arrow keeps getting reversed, waiting to feel confident
before delivering, rather than delivering to generate the confidence. Read it when
confidence feels low and the instinct is to wait for it to return before acting. The
full detail lives in [[competence-as-fuel]].

**One-liner:** Confidence is downstream of delivery, not a precondition for it.

**Reach for it when:** you're waiting to feel confident, ready, or smart enough
before starting, instead of starting to generate the confidence.

## Key Takeaways

- Action and results get noticed and build identity; being smart or well-read does
  not.
- Confidence is a byproduct of delivering something of value, studying or preparing
  is not a substitute for it.
- Genuinely new, risky action combined with real results is what shifts self-image;
  minor, safe adjustments don't.
- Terminal, competence-building work is a legitimate energy source, not a distraction
  from "real" priorities.

## Principles

- **Deliver, then feel confident**, not the other way round. Waiting for confidence
  first is procrastination with a respectable name.
- **Results beat smartness.** Spotting smarter or more eloquent people and
  concluding you can't compete is a paralysis trap; action is the differentiator,
  not intelligence.
- **Risk plus results shifts identity.** Genuinely new behaviour that gets a real
  result changes self-image; safe, incremental tweaks don't.
- **Protect the fuel source.** Extended time away from competence-building work
  causes a quiet erosion of self-belief that doesn't announce itself.

## Guidelines

- When stalled by comparison to smarter or more capable people, take the next
  concrete action instead of trying to out-think the comparison.
- If a decision requires confidence you don't yet have, look for the smallest real
  delivery that would generate it, not more preparation.
- Notice when terminal or competence-building work has been absent for weeks; that
  absence is a leading indicator of self-esteem drift, not a neutral gap.

## Limitations

This model explains where confidence comes from; it doesn't determine what's worth
delivering in the first place, that's a prioritisation question, not a confidence
one. It also doesn't apply to taste or judgement calls where there's no external
result to generate the feedback loop.
