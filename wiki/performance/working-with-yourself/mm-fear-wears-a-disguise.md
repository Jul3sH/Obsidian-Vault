# MM: Fear Wears a Disguise

This is the diagnostic half of the procrastination pattern: naming fear before it
gets a chance to pass itself off as a reasonable objection. It pairs with
[[mm-start-to-start]], which covers what to do once the disguise is spotted. Read
it when a decision to avoid something starts to feel unusually well-argued.

**One-liner:** Fear presents as a reasonable objection; name it before believing
it.

**Reach for it when:** you catch yourself building an elaborate, reasonable-
sounding case against doing something that scares you.

## Key Takeaways

- Fear rarely announces itself; it shows up dressed as "not ready yet," "not a
  priority," "consideration for someone else," or a sudden change of focus.
- The self-sabotage tell is "I could do it if I tried, but I'm choosing not to." It
  avoids ever testing whether you're good enough.
- Naming the disguise is most of the intervention; once seen, the objection
  usually stops holding up.
- The pattern recurs hardest at the highest-stakes moments (job interviews, career
  decisions), where the disguise is most convincing.

## Principles

- Fear masquerades as a rational objection: a missing qualification, bad timing,
  "I don't want to let someone else down."
- Watch for stories that let you take the easy option while feeling like you made
  a considered choice.
- A sudden, convincing shift in priorities is worth questioning; it may be fear of
  the harder thing, not a genuine reprioritisation.
- "I could succeed if I tried but it's not a priority" is a comfortable hiding
  place, not a real assessment.

## Guidelines

- When a reason for not doing something sounds unusually solid, ask whether it
  would survive if fear weren't in the room.
- Isolate the actual blocking variable (a specific person, a specific outcome) and
  check whether the objection is really about that, or about fear in general.
- If you find yourself explaining an avoidance in terms of protecting someone
  else, check whether it is really about protecting yourself.

## Limitations

This model only helps once you suspect a disguise is in play; it doesn't get you
moving on its own, pair it with [[mm-start-to-start]] once the fear is named. It's
also distinct from ordinary caution: not every hesitation is disguised fear, and
treating genuine risk assessment as "just fear" is its own mistake.
