# MM: Commitment-Stalling Bias

**One-liner:** Analysis complete is not decided. The commit point recedes until something external forces it.

**Reach for it when:** the analysis is done but the choice isn't written down, or a finished draft is gathering "one more" review before sending.

## Key Takeaways

- The weak point is committing, not analysing. More analysis doesn't fix it.
- The stall wears working clothes: extra review gates, model upgrades before sending, micro-revision loops, waiting for a third party who has not committed to reply.
- The likely driver is protecting your options. An open decision keeps every option alive, and closing it kills all but one ("it's about not wanting to give up the feeling of options and of abundance", 3 Jul 2026).
- A booked, external forcing function (an exam date, a stated deadline) turns an open intention into something that has to be met.
- Drifting into a quit is worse than deciding either way explicitly.
- Outside validation can be the trigger that flips a stalled decision into action.

## Principles

- **No deadline, no decision.** If nothing forces closure, closure won't happen, so create the deadline on purpose.
- **A decision is made when it's written and dated,** not when the analysis supports it.
- **Waiting on an outside signal is a decision to stall** unless that signal has a date.
- **Commit, then let execution follow.** Searching for the perfect answer is procrastination in disguise. The next step shows you what more thinking can't.
- **Quit consciously or not at all.** An explicit decision to stop is clean; drifting out counts as failure.
- **A schedule beats a list.** Commitment plus a scheduled slot with a deadline beats any amount of intending.

## Guidelines

| Situation | Fix |
|-----------|-----|
| **Decision stalled** | Create an outside forcing function: a booked date, a stated deadline, a choice written down, or work scheduled with another person. |
| **At a send point** | Name what the next review could change. If nothing, send. |
| **Waiting on a person** | Set the date waiting ends and what happens then. Do it while calm, so bad news only pulls a trigger. |
| **Unsure whether to continue** | Make the cost-benefit evaluation explicit instead of letting it fail by neglect. |

## Limitations

- A pending fact with a known arrival date is a legitimate reason to schedule a decision, not stall it.
- It gets the decision made. It doesn't say whether the choice was right.
- Analysis that keeps growing before it's enough is a different bias: [[mm-analysis-paralysis]].
- Once a decision is locked, this card hands over to [[mm-decision-reopening-bias]] and the `commitment-guard` skill. Stalling is before commitment, reopening is after.
