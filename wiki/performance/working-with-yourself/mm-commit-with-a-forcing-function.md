# MM: Commit With a Forcing Function

This is the Commit With a Forcing Function mental model: how analysis paralysis and
open-ended options actually get closed. It exists because more analysis never
resolves indecision, only a deadline, a booking, or a stated choice does. Read it
when a decision has enough information but no closure. The full detail lives in
[[commitment-avoidance]].

**One-liner:** Analysis ends when a deadline, booking, or written choice forces it.

**Reach for it when:** you notice yourself gathering more information on a decision
that already has enough, or drifting without a deadline attached.

## Key Takeaways

- The weak link is commitment, not analysis; building more analysis doesn't fix it.
- A booked, external forcing function (an exam date, a stated deadline) converts an
  open intention into something that has to be met.
- Quitting or committing unconsciously, by drift, is worse than deciding either way
  explicitly.
- External validation can be the trigger that flips a stalled decision into action;
  it doesn't have to come from inside.

## Principles

- **No deadline, no decision.** If nothing forces closure, closure won't happen;
  manufacture the deadline deliberately.
- **Commit, then let execution follow.** Endless speculation for the perfect answer
  is procrastination in disguise; the next step reveals what more thinking can't.
- **Quit consciously or not at all.** An unconscious, drifted-into quit still counts
  as failure; an explicit cost-benefit decision to stop is clean.
- **A schedule beats a list.** Commitment plus a scheduled slot with a deadline
  beats any amount of intending or listing.

## Guidelines

- When a decision has stalled, ask what external forcing function would close it,
  a booking, a date, a stated commitment, and create it deliberately.
- If genuinely undecided whether to continue something, make the cost-benefit
  evaluation explicit rather than letting it fail by neglect.
- Treat a booked date (an exam, a deadline, a stated public commitment) as the
  mechanism, not a nice-to-have on top of intention.

## Limitations

This model gets a decision or a piece of work started and closed; it says nothing
about whether the decision itself was the right one. It also doesn't address the
post-commitment U-turn (reopening a settled decision under emotional pressure),
that is a separate failure mode covered in [[commitment-avoidance]] and defended by
the `commitment-guard` skill.
