# MM: Sunk-Cost Bias

This card names the bias of anchoring decisions on what was paid rather than on what the asset is worth now. Documented as bias #8 in the [[commitment-lock-protocol]] register: the HK flat, down ~HK$3M, held to "wait for recovery" - a strategy anchored on the HK$8M paid, not on where HK$6M is best deployed.

**One-liner:** The question is where the money is best deployed now, never how to get back what was paid.

**Reach for it when:** holding, continuing, or doubling down on anything - an asset, a project, a strategy - and the reasoning references what has already been spent.

## Key Takeaways

- The purchase price is not information about the future. The market does not know what you paid.
- "Wait for recovery" is a decision to invest today's value in the same asset, every day, silently.
- The bias extends beyond money: time already spent on a project or plan argues for continuing it with exactly the same false logic.

## Principles

- **Sunk costs are spent under every option, so they cannot differentiate options.**
- **Today's value is the only stake on the table.**
- **Getting back to even is a story about the past, not a strategy.**

## Guidelines

- Reframe every hold decision: "is [today's value] best deployed here?" - never "will it recover to [what I paid]?" ([[commitment-lock-protocol]]).
- Set a dated review trigger for held assets so the decision is re-made on schedule, not deferred indefinitely.
- For time sunk into projects: size the remaining work against the remaining value, ignoring effort already spent.

## Limitations

- Exit costs (fees, taxes, disruption) are real forward-looking numbers and legitimately enter the decision - they are not sunk.
