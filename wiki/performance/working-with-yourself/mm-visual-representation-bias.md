# MM: Visual Representation Bias

This is the Visual Representation Bias mental model: a compact six-slot
distillation of the existing pattern-grain article [[visual-representation-bias]],
which already carries the full pattern, reference case, and mechanism. This card
exists so the pattern sits alongside the other working-with-yourself models in one
format; the article remains the authoritative source. Read it before a decision
option starts to feel good rather than being weighed.

**One-liner:** A vivid picture of the good day is not a decision; ask for the
ordinary bad day too.

**Reach for it when:** evaluating a life, career, or relationship option and
noticing a preference forming from how it feels rather than from the numbers or the
full picture.

## Key Takeaways

- The pleasant image crystallises fast and functions like evidence, even though it
  is only half the picture.
- What gets left out is the lived-experience detail: the commute, the crash-pad
  nights, the financial vigilance, not just the appealing moments.
- Fatigue amplifies the effect: a tired brain pulls toward whichever option feels
  easiest to imagine.
- The fix is not to distrust the pleasant image, but to deliberately build the
  matching ordinary-bad-day image before weighing the option.

## Principles

- **Ask for the ordinary bad day, not the disaster case.** A regular Tuesday when
  things are fine but not great is the comparison the pleasant image is missing.
- **If both images are present and the option still wins, that's a genuine
  preference.** The check isn't there to reverse decisions, only to make sure both
  images existed before the preference formed.
- **Surface it in the subject's own words and log it**, so the completed image is
  checkable later rather than dissolving back into feeling.
- **This fires before commitment.** Once locked in, a different model
  ([[ai-os/skills/commitment-guard/SKILL|commitment-guard]]) defends the decision;
  this one exists to get the decision right before that point.

## Guidelines

- When a preference starts to form from how an option feels, stop and ask: what does
  the ordinary bad day under this option look like?
- Do this earlier under fatigue or time pressure, not later; that's exactly when the
  incomplete image has the most pull.
- Compare the completed image against the numbers, not just against the other
  option's incomplete image.

## Limitations

Does not apply to options with no meaningful emotional pull; the bias needs a vivid
image to distort. Does not tell you which option to choose, only ensures the choice
is made from a complete picture rather than a partial one.
