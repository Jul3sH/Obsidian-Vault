# MM: Loss-Aversion Bias

This card names the bias of letting fear of loss vote on a decision when no scenario actually threatens the thing being protected. Documented as bias #7 in the [[commitment-lock-protocol]] register: the whole Malvern lean existed to "protect the bottom line" - a floor no modelled scenario breached.

**One-liner:** A fear with no breachable floor gets no vote.

**Reach for it when:** a safe-looking option is pulling ahead and the pull is about protecting something - money, security, a fallback.

## Key Takeaways

- The bias converts "this loss would be terrible" into "this loss is likely" without ever checking the second claim.
- It is strongest exactly when the stakes sound big (running out of cash, losing the flat), because the size of the imagined loss substitutes for its probability.
- The two-question test kills it fast: state the floor; state the scenario that breaches it. If no scenario breaches it, the fear is real but irrelevant to the choice.

## Principles

- **Fear votes only on scenarios that exist in the model.**
- **The severity of a loss and its probability are separate numbers; the bias multiplies by the wrong one.**
- **Protection has a price - name what the safe option costs before paying it.**

## Guidelines

- Ask first, always: "state the floor. State the scenario that breaches it. If none, this fear gets no vote" ([[commitment-lock-protocol]]).
- Pair with [[mm-visual-representation-bias]]: the feared loss usually arrives as a vivid image, not a probability.
- If a real breaching scenario exists, size it properly - then it earns its vote through the model, not the feeling.

## Limitations

- Genuine tail risks with catastrophic, irreversible outcomes justify protection beyond expected-value maths - but they must still be named scenarios, not free-floating dread.
