# MM: Payoff vs Prestige

This is the Payoff vs Prestige mental model: a compact six-slot distillation of the
existing pattern-grain article [[payoff-vs-prestige-bias]], which already carries
the full test and rationale. This card exists so the pattern sits alongside the
other working-with-yourself models in one format; the article remains the
authoritative source. Read it before starting a piece of learning that has no named
buyer yet.

**One-liner:** Learn for what it produces, not for how impressive you'll sound
explaining it.

**Reach for it when:** deciding whether a piece of learning or research is worth
starting, especially anything that feels exciting to explain to people whose opinion
matters.

## Key Takeaways

- The prestige motivation (picturing yourself impressing an audience) is genuine
  fuel and should be redirected, not suppressed.
- Redirect it by swapping the imagined room: from peers who are impressed, to one
  buyer asking what it costs.
- The sharpest single check is the silence test: would you still do it if you could
  never tell anyone you knew it?
- Depth needs a stopping line stated before starting, observable by someone other
  than you, or "impressive" has no natural end.

## Principles

- **Two legitimate payoff routes**: money a named buyer would pay, or a named
  deliverable made faster/cheaper/better, reusable across two or more deliverables
  with the saving stated.
- **The silence test isolates the prestige payoff directly**: if you'd only do it to
  be able to tell someone, the payoff answer has to stand on its own without that
  crutch.
- **An unbounded stopping condition is how learning becomes academic.** Write down
  what "good enough" looks like before starting, in terms someone else could check.
- **Scoped to Career and Performance work.** A speculative answer is allowed, but
  capped and labelled, so the gate has a legitimate way to pass rather than being
  routed around.

## Guidelines

- Before starting, answer: who pays or what deliverable does this make faster, and
  by how much?
- Apply the silence test as a gut-check, then require the payoff answer to stand
  without it.
- Write the "good enough when" line before starting, not once already deep into it.

## Limitations

Does not apply outside Career/Performance work (Wellbeing, Relationships, Personal,
Finance are exempt). Does not forbid speculative learning with no named buyer yet,
provided it is labelled and capped.
