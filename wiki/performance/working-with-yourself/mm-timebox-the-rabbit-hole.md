# MM: Timebox the Rabbit Hole

This is the Timebox mental model: curiosity and flow state are genuine ADHD assets,
but unbounded they turn into scope creep with no deliverable attached. It exists
because Julian's own logs show the same failure mode across learning, phone
distraction, and presentation prep: something that starts small expands to fill
whatever time is available, unless a limit is set before it starts. Read it before
starting anything framed as "just a bit of learning" or "just polishing this."

**One-liner:** Curiosity without a timebox is scope creep wearing a productive
disguise.

**Reach for it when:** you notice you are "just learning a bit more" about something
that has no deliverable attached, or reluctant to interrupt a flow state to get
feedback.

## Key Takeaways

- A rabbit hole rarely announces itself as one; it starts as "learning a bit about
  X" and ends 3-4 days later having built something nobody asked for.
- The urge to check the phone after a short burst of focus is real (ADHD-linked),
  and fighting it head-on is harder than pre-agreeing when the distraction is
  allowed.
- Flow state protecting itself from interruption is not always good; sometimes flow
  is avoiding the discomfort of finding out you're on the wrong track, and the
  checkpoint that would reveal that is exactly what gets skipped.
- The fix in all three cases is the same: set the limit before starting, not
  partway through once the pull is already active.

## Principles

- **Set the timebox before the activity starts, not once it feels like it's running
  long.** By the time it feels long, the sunk-cost pull is already active and
  self-limiting stops working.
- **Learning is the biggest culprit.** Framing an activity as "learning" makes it
  feel automatically justified, which is precisely what removes the urgency to
  timebox it.
- **Pre-agree the distraction, don't just resist it.** Resisting an ADHD urge cold is
  expensive; giving it a scheduled outlet (a Pomodoro break) is cheap and holds.
- **Force a checkpoint before flow state, not after.** A checkpoint placed after flow
  has taken hold arrives too late; it has to be a rule set going in, so it survives
  the exact state (perfectionist absorption) that makes people skip checkpoints.

## Guidelines

- Before opening a "quick bit of research," state the timebox and what you'll do at
  the end of it out loud or in writing. If there's no natural end condition, that's
  the signal it's a rabbit hole, not a task.
- Keep the phone physically out of sight during focused work, and pre-commit to a
  fixed interval (e.g. every 30 minutes) where checking it is allowed. The urge is
  real; a scheduled release valve beats pure willpower.
- Before entering flow-state polish on a presentation or interview prep, schedule
  the checkpoint (a feedback session, a dry run) in advance, at a time before the
  deliverable is due. Do not let "I'm not ready for feedback yet" be the reason it
  gets skipped.

## Limitations

This model is about bounding time already spent on a legitimate activity; it does
not decide whether the activity should be started at all (see
[[mm-payoff-vs-prestige]] for whether the topic itself is worth learning). It also
does not apply to genuinely open-ended exploration deliberately scoped as
speculative; the timebox there is set at the deliverable level, not mid-flow.
