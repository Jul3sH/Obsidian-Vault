# MM: Setbacks Are Glitches

This is the recovery model: how to get back to normal fast after a knockback, a
mistake, or a bad mood, before it bleeds into the rest of the day. Read it when a
setback is threatening to become bigger than the setback itself.

**One-liner:** Recover fast: a setback is a data point, not a verdict.

**Reach for it when:** a knockback, mistake, or bad mood threatens to bleed into
the rest of your day or week.

## Key Takeaways

- Setbacks almost always turn out to be glitches in hindsight; the real cost is
  the time spent demotivated while finding that out.
- Sulky or martyred behaviour is self-indulgent: it achieves nothing, contaminates
  the people around you, and is easy to snap out of by conscious choice.
- One trigger doesn't have to cascade into a second, unrelated blow-up.
- Letting a perceived slight fester and consume mental energy is its own separate
  cost, distinct from the original setback.

## Principles

- Recovery is a deliberate choice, not something that happens to you; snap out of
  it on purpose.
- A setback that feels catastrophic in the moment is rarely a verdict on your
  competence; treat it as a data point and move on.
- Don't let a reaction to one thing bleed into an unrelated blow-up (a boiler
  email triggering a rant about a different boiler).
- Address a nagging worry directly and quickly rather than letting it run as
  background paranoia for days.

## Guidelines

- Notice sulking or martyred behaviour in real time and make the conscious choice
  to shift state.
- Ask "will this matter in a week?" as a fast gut-check before letting a setback
  define the rest of the day.
- If a concern keeps recurring in your head unaddressed, that's the signal to
  raise it directly, not to keep sitting on it.

## Limitations

This model handles emotional recovery after the fact. It doesn't cover the moment
of the trigger itself, that's [[mm-never-react]], or whether the underlying fact
pattern needs checking, that's [[mm-verify-before-acting]].
