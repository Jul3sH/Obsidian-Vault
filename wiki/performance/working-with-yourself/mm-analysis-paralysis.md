## Purpose

The Analysis Paralysis mental model: the bias of analysing past what a decision needs, and how to stop it. It replaces `mm-overanalysis-bias` under a more familiar name.

# MM: Analysis Paralysis

**One-liner:** The decision sets how deep to go, not the material available.

**Reach for it when:** you keep adding to an analysis: another source, another cross-check, another review round, a model upgrade before sending.

## Key Takeaways

- Past a point, more analysis doesn't move a decision forward. It only feels like progress.
- It looks like work: extra research passes, re-reviews, version churn, frameworks for a single decision. That's why it survives.
- It costs twice: the time and tokens, and outputs too detailed to hold in your head ("so detailed I can feel my head exploding", 5 Aug 2026).
- Going down a rabbit hole is the same bias spread over time: depth in one tangent instead of enough depth everywhere.
- The tell is asking "what else could we add?" instead of "what would change the decision?"

## Principles

- **The decision sets the depth.** If no decision is named, no depth is earned.
- **More material is not a reason to use it.**
- **A review round must name what it could change.** A re-review that couldn't change the outcome is churn.
- **The check has to run before you start.** Spotting it in the retro is too late.

## Guidelines

| Stage | Fix |
|-------|-----|
| **Before starting** | Ask "what decision does this feed, and what's the smallest output that decides it?" Answer the `/prompt-zero` "depth warranted" question honestly. Push detail downstream: say where it will live instead of including it. |
| **Mid-task** | If you drift into a tangent, apply [[mm-timebox-the-rabbit-hole]]. |
| **Before another review** | Name what it could change. If nothing, stop. |

## Limitations

- Lock-level decisions earn full adversarial ceremony because of their stakes. That isn't paralysis.
- Assurance work required by convention (adversarial review, verification) isn't overanalysis, even when it feels heavy.
- Once the analysis is enough but the choice still isn't made, that's a different bias: [[mm-commitment-stalling-bias]].
