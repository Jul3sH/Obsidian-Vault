# MM: Eggs In One Basket

This is the Eggs In One Basket mental model: the failure of letting conviction
about one pending outcome shut down every alternative while the outcome is still
someone else's decision. It was created from the TTI engagement, where months of
believing the job was coming - built from internal narrative, wishful thinking,
confirmation bias, and an ally's speculative optimism - meant other opportunities
were ignored, so the eventual no landed on an empty pipeline. Read it whenever one
opportunity starts consuming all the effort and the others start feeling
unnecessary.

**One-liner:** Conviction is not an offer - keep the other baskets live until the
contract is signed.

**Reach for it when:** one pending opportunity feels so likely that working the
alternatives has started to feel like wasted effort.

## Key Takeaways

- The feeling of certainty about someone else's decision is built from stories,
  hope, and filtered signals - it is not an offer, and it prices nothing.
- The cost of a dropped pipeline is paid exactly when you are least able to pay
  it: on the day the favourite falls through.
- Allies close to the opportunity are the most convincing source of false
  certainty; their optimism is loyalty, not information.
- Working the alternatives is not disloyalty to the favourite; it is what makes a
  no survivable and a negotiation honest.

## Principles

- **A belief is not an offer.** Until something is signed, the favourite is a
  probability, and probabilities do not warrant exclusivity. Ask what odds you
  would honestly put on it, then ask whether those odds justify an empty pipeline.
- **Watch the belief machinery, not just the belief.** The certainty is
  manufactured by [[mm-stories-arent-evidence]] (the internal narrative) and
  [[mm-confirmation-bias]] (reading every signal as support), amplified by
  invested allies. When those are the sources, the confidence level is
  information about you, not about the outcome.
- **The pipeline is the hedge, and it also improves the favourite.** Live
  alternatives change how you negotiate, how desperate you read, and how a
  deadline lands. An empty pipeline leaks into the favourite as neediness.
- **Exclusivity is a cost paid silently.** Nobody records the day the other
  opportunities stopped being worked; it only becomes visible when the favourite
  dies. Make the trade-off explicit and dated if you choose to focus.

## Guidelines

- While any single opportunity is pending, keep a minimum heartbeat on the
  alternatives: applications moving, conversations warm, the
  [[job-search-pipeline]] (or its equivalent) actually ticking.
- When an ally close to the deal says it's certain, log it as morale, not signal.
- Notice the tell: mentally spending the outcome (negotiating the offer's size in
  your head, planning around it) before anything is signed.
- If you deliberately choose all-in focus, write the decision down with its date
  and its review trigger, so the exclusivity is a priced choice rather than a
  drift.

## Limitations

This model is NOT the opposite instruction to [[commitment-avoidance]] and
[[mm-commit-with-a-forcing-function]], and must not be read as licence to hoard
options. The distinction: those cover **decisions that are yours to close** -
close them fast, with forcing functions. This covers **outcomes someone else
decides** - never let their pending decision close your pipeline for them. Close
your own decisions; hedge everyone else's.
