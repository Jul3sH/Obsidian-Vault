# MM: Impaired-State Rules

This is the Impaired-State mental model: tiredness, drink, and strong emotion all
degrade judgement in the same direction (skipped checks, unfiltered speech, false
confidence), so the rule has to be set in advance and applied automatically, because
the impaired state is exactly the state in which you can't trust yourself to judge
whether you're impaired. Read it before an important conversation or a decision when
you are tired, have been drinking, or are running hot.

**One-liner:** Tired, drinking, or emotional: the rule decides, not you, because the
state itself is what makes you a bad judge of the state.

**Reach for it when:** you're about to speak to someone senior, sign off on
something material, or make a call, while tired, drinking, or running on emotion.

## Key Takeaways

- Tiredness and alcohol produce the same failure signature: things said land badly
  without you noticing, and diligence gets skipped without you noticing it's been
  skipped.
- The cost is often invisible at the time and shows up later, financially (a
  skipped due-diligence check), reputationally (a comment that costs an invitation),
  or as a state (paranoia that then suppresses risk-taking).
- Because judgement is what's impaired, the rule has to be pre-set and mechanical
  ("run it through your head first," "no material decisions while impaired"), not
  a fresh judgement call made in the moment.
- This is a decision-rule model: it says apply a pre-set rule when impaired. The
  physical and wellbeing cost of alcohol and sleep loss themselves are covered
  separately; see Detail.

## Principles

- **Think before you speak when tired or drinking.** The other person doesn't know
  you're tired or a few drinks in; what lands for them is only the words, not the
  excuse in your head.
- **Never skip due diligence because you're impaired.** Impairment makes corner-
  cutting feel normal and reasonable in the moment; it isn't, and the cost surfaces
  later, at full price.
- **Poor sleep and heavy drinking both drive paranoia**, and paranoia suppresses
  risk-taking, so a string of bad nights or drinks is a reason to discount your own
  read on a risky situation, not a reason to trust caution more.
- **Control in public is binary, not gradient.** Once someone has seen you lose
  control, they question your judgement generally, not just in that moment; the
  correct move is to make the impression and then leave, not to keep going.

## Guidelines

- Before an important conversation, name your state honestly (tired / a few drinks
  in / running hot) and apply the pre-set rule for that state rather than trusting
  your in-the-moment read of how well you're managing it.
- If a material decision (financial, contractual, employment) needs signing off and
  you are impaired, default to delaying it, not deciding it. The £8k lost-fortune
  lesson below is the exact shape of what impaired due diligence costs.
- In social settings with people who matter professionally, treat "make an
  impression, then move on" as the whole plan. Elaborating further under drink is
  where the risk lives, not in the first impression itself.

## Limitations

This model is a decision-rule for behaviour while impaired; it does not cover the
physical health, sleep hygiene, or drinking-reduction work itself, which is a
wellbeing matter, not a self-management one. It also doesn't decide when you're
impaired enough to trigger the rule; that judgement call has to be made earlier,
before the state sets in (e.g. deciding a drink limit going into an event).
