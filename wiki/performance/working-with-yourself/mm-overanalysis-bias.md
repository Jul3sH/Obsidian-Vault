# MM: Overanalysis Bias

This card names the bias of building analytical depth beyond what the decision at stake warrants. It was confirmed with quoted evidence across 12 time-slices of Julian's session history (May-Sep 2026) during [[bias-history-review]], promoting what was previously a memory-only correction (`feedback-overanalysis-check`) to a card. Read it when scoping any analysis, register, framework, or research task.

**One-liner:** Depth is earned by stakes, not by available material - analysis past the decision's needs is cost, not rigour.

**Reach for it when:** scoping a piece of analysis, or noticing it growing: another source, another cross-check, another review round, a model upgrade before sending.

## Key Takeaways

- The bias shows as extra research passes, re-reviews after model upgrades, version churn, and multi-layer frameworks for single decisions.
- Its cost is double: the tokens and time spent, and outputs too detailed to hold ("a lot of these outputs are so detailed my head explodes, and ultimately I'm going to have to make the decision").
- Rabbit-holing is the same bias in the time dimension: depth in one tangent instead of depth everywhere.
- The tell is asking "what else could we add?" instead of "what would change the decision?"

## Principles

- **The decision being fed sets the depth ceiling.** No decision named, no depth earned.
- **More available material is not a reason to use it.**
- **A review round must name what it could change.** A re-review that could not alter the outcome is churn.
- **Awareness does not fix it at scoping time.** The check must run before work starts, not in the retro.

## Guidelines

- Before starting: ask "what decision does this feed, and what is the smallest output that decides it?" (this is the standing `feedback-overanalysis-check` question).
- The `/prompt-zero` interview's "depth warranted" question is the formal gate - answer it honestly, not aspirationally.
- Mid-task drift into a tangent: apply [[mm-timebox-the-rabbit-hole]].
- Push detail downstream: name where detail will live rather than including it.

## Limitations

- Does not apply to lock-level decisions, which earn full adversarial ceremony by their stakes.
- Assurance work (adversarial review, verification) mandated by convention is not overanalysis, even when it feels heavy.
