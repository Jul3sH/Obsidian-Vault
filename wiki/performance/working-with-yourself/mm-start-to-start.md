# MM: Start to Start

This is the action half of the procrastination pattern: what actually gets you
moving once you're stalled. It was split out so the mechanics of starting stand on
their own, separate from the fear-naming half in [[mm-fear-wears-a-disguise]]. Read
it when you're stuck, not when you're trying to work out why.

**One-liner:** Fear and stress shrink the moment you start; they never shrink while
you wait.

**Reach for it when:** you're stalled, over-researching, or waiting to feel ready
before opening the file, making the call, or committing.

## Key Takeaways

- The block dissolves the moment you start; anticipation, not the task itself, is
  what's stressful.
- Barriers built in your head before starting are rarely the real obstacles; the
  real ones only appear once you're in it.
- A decision, once made, shifts the brain into solving mode. Deciding is the hard
  part, not the doing.
- Minimal research then start beats extensive research then start; real feedback
  comes from doing, not speculating.

## Principles

- Open the file, make the call, book the thing. Direction becomes obvious once
  you're inside the task, not before.
- Confront a source of anxiety with a concrete task rather than trying to think or
  meditate your way out of it.
- Commit first, let execution follow: once a stake is in the ground, the brain
  starts solving for it.
- A high say-do ratio, doing something with limited scope, beats months of
  paralysis waiting for the perfect goal.
- Do minimal research, act, then research the roadblocks that actually show up, not
  the ones you imagined.

## Guidelines

- When stalled, pick the smallest next concrete action and do it today.
- If a decision keeps getting deferred, treat "decide" itself as the task, and put
  a deadline or booking against it.
- Notice when planning has quietly become procrastination with better branding.

## Limitations

This model covers the mechanics of getting moving; it doesn't diagnose why you're
stuck. If the stall is really a rationalised fear, use
[[mm-fear-wears-a-disguise]] to name it first, then start.
