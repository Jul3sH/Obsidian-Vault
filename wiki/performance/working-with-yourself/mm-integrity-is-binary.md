# MM: Integrity Is Binary

This is the Integrity mental model: whether a commitment made to yourself is being
kept, not whether it is convenient today. It exists because Julian's own audit found
he practises integrity selectively, with authority figures and not with himself, and
that selectivity is the root cause of habits never sticking. Read it before deciding
whether a self-commitment can flex "just this once."

**One-liner:** A commitment to yourself is on or off; there is no version of it that
bends for a good reason.

**Reach for it when:** you are about to talk yourself out of a commitment you made to
yourself, using a reason that sounds legitimate.

## Key Takeaways

- Integrity with yourself is the foundation habits are built on; break it once with a
  good reason and the next "good reason" is already licensed.
- Integrity is not selective by audience. Practising it with higher authority and not
  with yourself, or not with lower authority, is not integrity, it is compliance.
- The reason for breaking the commitment is not the problem. Believing the reason
  matters is the problem: the commitment was supposed to be immune to reasons.
- If a commitment is not going to be kept with full integrity, the fix is not making
  it in the first place, not making it and hoping.

## Principles

- **Integrity is all-or-nothing.** It has to be practised with lower authority and
  with yourself, exactly as much as with higher authority, or it is not integrity.
- **Every self-commitment, however small, gets treated like your life depends on it.**
  Size is not a variable; a small broken commitment erodes the same way a large one
  does.
- **Do not make a commitment you do not intend to keep at that standard.** An
  intention held loosely is not a commitment, and labelling it one just delays the
  erosion.
- **The reason offered for breaking a commitment is rationalisation, not evidence.**
  "This might be my last chance to see Dad" and "I just fancied watching rugby more"
  can both be true; only the second one is honest, and honesty is the test.

## Guidelines

- Before making a self-commitment, ask whether it will be kept if a better-sounding
  reason to skip it turns up. If the honest answer is no, do not make it as a
  commitment, make it as a preference instead.
- When a reason to break a commitment appears, name it in one sentence and ask if it
  would survive being said to someone else's face. "I fancied the rugby more" rarely
  survives; "there was a genuine emergency" does.
- Selective integrity is a tell, not a one-off lapse. If a pattern of keeping
  commitments to bosses but not to yourself shows up, treat it as the root cause, not
  a coincidence.

## Limitations

This model governs commitments already made; it says nothing about which
commitments are worth making, or when a considered, evaluated decision to stop
should override a stale one (see the "quit consciously, not by unconscious failure"
material in [[eq-integrity-and-ease-of-grace]]). Integrity is not a reason to keep a
commitment that has genuinely stopped serving its purpose; it is a reason to make
that call explicitly rather than by unconscious drift.
