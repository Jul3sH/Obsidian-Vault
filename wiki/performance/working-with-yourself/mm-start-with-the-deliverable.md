# MM: Start With the Deliverable

This is the Start With the Deliverable mental model: opening the actual output first
and letting the work organise itself around it, instead of researching and planning
bottom-up until a deadline forces a scramble. It exists because the bottom-up drift
pattern is chronic and the fix, manufactured urgency, top-down execution, works every
time it's actually used. Read it at the start of anything that could drift into
research before production. The full detail lives in
[[top-down-execution-start-with-the-deliverable]].

**One-liner:** Open the deliverable first and let the work organise itself around it.

**Reach for it when:** you're about to start something with research or planning
rather than opening the actual output file.

## Key Takeaways

- Starting inside the deliverable creates clarity that research and planning don't.
- Deadline urgency produces the focus chemicals; manufacture that urgency instead of
  waiting for a real deadline to force it.
- Grasp the overall objective before diving into detail, and brainstorm options
  before detailing any single one.
- Minimal research, then start, most anticipated roadblocks aren't the real ones.

## Principles

- **Open the file, not the search bar.** Direction comes from doing, not from
  planning; indecision usually dissolves the moment you're inside the work.
- **Manufacture urgency deliberately.** A tight deadline reliably produces effective
  top-down execution; the same task with no deadline drifts into tangents.
- **Grasp the objective before the detail.** Read for the overall goal first;
  grouping tasks or analysing detail before that wastes the analysis.
- **Establish your own structure before reusing content.** Adapting existing
  material for speed usually costs more time than building the right structure
  first and then pulling material in.
- **Validate before you build.** Check whether an approach is even needed before
  investing detailed work in it.

## Guidelines

- When stuck on direction, open the actual deliverable and start working in it
  rather than researching further.
- Set a firm limit on research time before beginning; the roadblocks that appear
  once you start are rarely the ones you anticipated.
- Brainstorm and review the live options before spending detailed effort on any one
  of them.
- Read a document or a set of requirements through fully before taking notes or
  starting to build.

## Limitations

This model is about how to start, not what to prioritise between competing
deliverables, that's [[mm-guard-the-critical-path]]. It also doesn't replace
genuine research where the deliverable actually depends on information you don't
have; the point is to timebox that research, not skip it.
