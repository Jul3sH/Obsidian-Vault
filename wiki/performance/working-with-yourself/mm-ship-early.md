# MM: Ship Early

This is the model for the trade-off between polish and delivery: when perfectionism
is quietly procrastination with better PR. Read it before you spend another day
refining something, or before you overhaul a system that already works.

**One-liner:** Early and done beats late and polished.

**Reach for it when:** you're tempted to keep refining something past the point
anyone will notice, or to overhaul something that already works.

## Key Takeaways

- Perfectionism is procrastination with better PR: over-committing, over-
  engineering, and refusing to ship until "ideal" all avoid the same discomfort.
- Delivering ahead of schedule, imperfect, consistently lands better than
  delivering late and polished.
- Never overhaul a working system in one go; make one small change, test it in
  real conditions, then make the next.
- Regularly ask "is this still working as intended?" and change course early
  rather than grinding against something broken.

## Principles

- Speed of delivery usually matters more than a polish nobody will notice; ship
  it, get the feedback, iterate.
- Test new processes and systems early and often; paper refinement without
  testing is often perfectionism (or hyperfocus) in disguise.
- Don't drop a working system for something new and shiny without testing whether
  the new one is actually better.
- Time spent on politics or email polish is not delivery, however necessary it
  feels in the moment.
- Understand what a technology or system achieves before diving into how it
  works; depth for its own sake is a perfectionism trap for a technical leader.

## Guidelines

- Before a big rewrite, ask: can this ship as a small, tested change instead of a
  full overhaul?
- Set a checkpoint early in any new process or format and test it against reality
  before refining further.
- If you're spending more time organising or polishing than delivering, that's
  the tell.
- Never change systems, tools, or processes right when you're new to an
  environment; wait until you're settled.

## Limitations

This model is about the trade-off between polish and delivery once you've already
started. It doesn't cover getting started in the first place, that's
[[mm-start-to-start]], or the separate discipline of not over-committing to what
you'll ship in the first place.
