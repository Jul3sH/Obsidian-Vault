# MM: Recency Bias

This card names the bias of conviction re-anchoring to the most recent emotionally warm contact rather than to the evidence. Documented as bias #2 in the [[commitment-lock-protocol]] register: all four lean-changes in the UK-move decision timestamped to a human contact, never to a document (the register's examples: Joanne leading to the reopen, Sophia to the soft-landing, the Clodagh call to "100%").

**One-liner:** Conviction re-anchors to the last warm voice, not the evidence.

**Reach for it when:** a lean or conviction has shifted and the last 48 hours contained an emotionally significant conversation.

## Key Takeaways

- The tell is timing: check what the new conviction follows. A person, not a document, is the signature.
- Warmth is the carrier - the effect is strongest with people who matter (partner, child, close friends), which is exactly when it feels least like bias.
- The contact does not need to argue a position; two good days with someone can move a decision their way unprompted.

## Principles

- **A lean that moved after a conversation was moved by the conversation.**
- **The order evidence arrives in is not information about its weight.**
- **Warmth is not data.**

## Guidelines

- 48-hour stand-down: no decision state change within 48 hours of the contact that prompted the feeling ([[commitment-lock-protocol]]).
- Run the 60-second wobble drill: name the trigger first - "who did I just talk to?"
- Re-read the anchor document before acting on the new lean.

## Limitations

- A conversation that delivers a genuinely new material fact is evidence; the stand-down delays acting on it but the fact then goes through the reopen test on its merits.
