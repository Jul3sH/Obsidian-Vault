# MM: Optimism-Accounting Bias

This card names the bias of booking unrealised assets as if they had landed. Documented as bias #6 in the [[commitment-lock-protocol]] register from the UK-move decision: TTI counted as income before any offer, a decade-dormant network counted as "warm", hoped-for outcomes driving "100%" conviction, MPF and rebates counted before they landed.

**One-liner:** Hoped-for money is narrative; only banked money is runway.

**Reach for it when:** building any plan, model, or runway figure - the moment a number enters that depends on something that has not yet happened.

## Key Takeaways

- The bias hides in classification, not arithmetic: the sums are right, but Hoped items sit in the Banked column.
- Its favourite disguises: a probable job as income, a warm-sounding network as pipeline, a legal entitlement as cash, a recovery as a plan.
- One tilted input silently tilts every downstream figure (runway, breakeven, the committed choice).

## Principles

- **Two columns, always: Banked and Hoped** - the register's counter-move, quoted in full in [[commitment-lock-protocol]]; nothing Hoped enters a committed figure.
- **A thing is Banked when it has landed, not when it is likely.**
- **Optimism belongs in the story, not the spreadsheet.**

## Guidelines

- Label every input of a financial model Banked or Hoped at entry time ([[commitment-lock-protocol]] register counter-move).
- On review, hunt the Hoped-as-Banked items specifically - they pass arithmetic checks.
- When a Hoped item lands, move it across with a date; until then it gets no vote.

## Limitations

- Scenario planning may legitimately model Hoped outcomes - as named scenarios, never blended into the base case.
