# MM: Wishful-Thinking Bias

This card names the bias of believing a hoped-for outcome will happen because I want it to, and letting that belief drive what I do or stop doing. Carded 28 Sep 2026 in Julian's own words, on review of the retired optimism-accounting card: the TTI instance was not an accounting error but wishful thinking that stopped action on the alternatives. Wishful thinking is the cause; [[mm-eggs-in-one-basket]] is one of its effects.

**One-liner:** Wanting something to happen is not evidence that it will.

**Reach for it when:** a hoped-for outcome that someone else controls (a job, a deal, a reply) starts to feel certain, and I notice I'm planning around it.

## Key Takeaways

- Wishful thinking is the cause; eggs in one basket is one effect. I can be wishful and keep working the alternatives, and I can drop the alternatives for other reasons, like procrastination.
- The belief is built from wanting it, from stories about what the other side intends, and from allies' optimism. None of that is a commitment from the person deciding.
- It has run twice on the same lead: TTI from June to August 2026, and Horst's verbal intention in September 2026.

## Principles

- **Wanting an outcome does not change its probability.**
- **Only the decider's written commitment moves the odds.** Everything else is a signal about my mood.
- **The damage is in what the belief stops me doing**, so name the effect it is driving.

## Guidelines

- Write down the odds I would give it if I did not want it.
- Ask what I have stopped doing because of this belief. If the answer is "the alternatives", go to [[mm-eggs-in-one-basket]].
- The tell is spending the outcome in my head (sizing the offer, planning the move) before anything is in writing.
- An ally's optimism is morale, not signal.

## Limitations

- Hope itself is fine and keeps me going. The bias is when the hope changes the odds I act on.
- Not the same as optimism accounting (register bias 6 in [[commitment-lock-protocol]]), retired as a personal bias on 28 Sep 2026: those instances were model-drafted figures caught by review, not a personal habit.
