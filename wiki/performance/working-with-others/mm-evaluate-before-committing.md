# MM: Evaluate Before Committing

This is the Evaluate Before Committing mental model: whether to weigh a
commitment's requirements, constraints, and risk before saying yes, or let a
can-do attitude override that evaluation. It exists because the Kroll
engagement, the reference case behind this model, shows exactly what
happens when it doesn't: a new hire absorbs a deliverable with no
requirements because saying no felt riskier than it was. Read it before
volunteering, forecasting effort, or agreeing to take something on.

**One-liner:** Can-do without evaluation is how you end up owning the
impossible.

**Reach for it when:** you're tempted to say yes, volunteer, or forecast
effort before listing requirements, constraints, and risk.

## Key Takeaways

- Kroll is the textbook case: can-do absorbed a deliverable with no
  requirements, a disengaged customer, and a skipped process gate, because
  being new made saying no feel like a risk to the relationship.
- Forecast effort only after listing requirements, constraints, and
  dependencies, never before.
- The gain from looking keen is almost always smaller than the cost of
  failing to deliver something worthless.
- Admitting an experience or capability gap early is not weakness; it's the
  input the evaluation needs.
- Pilot before full commitment. Don't fully commit to something new and
  shiny until it's been tested at small scale.

## Principles

- Ask before committing: "What am I actually hoping to achieve by taking
  this on?" If the answer isn't clear, don't take it on.
- List requirements, constraints, deliverables, effort, and dependencies
  before forecasting; flag anything missing as a risk before agreeing, not
  after.
- If you can't deliver what's being asked in the timeframe, say so clearly
  and early. Silence is worse.
- A can-do attitude is only an asset once the risk has been assessed;
  enthusiasm without evaluation is a slow-motion failure.

## Guidelines

- Weigh pros, cons, and risk before suggesting or agreeing to an approach,
  not after.
- Pilot new systems or processes at small scale before full commitment.
- When new to a role or team, forecast conservatively and say so; a culture
  that expects quality will punish an optimistic estimate harder than a
  modest one.
- If mentoring is available before a risky commitment, use it before the
  fact, not as damage control after.

## Limitations

This model governs commitments you have a genuine choice about. Once you've
committed with eyes open, execution is [[deliver-what-your-boss-wants]]
territory; re-litigating an already-made decision is
[[ai-os/skills/commitment-guard/SKILL|commitment-guard]] territory, not this
model.
