# MM: Results Are The Currency

This is the Results Are The Currency mental model: what to actually report upward,
and what to withhold until it's real. It exists because the gap between "I did
something" and "I said something" is where credibility is won or lost, quietly,
one update at a time. Read it before any status update, announcement, or moment
you're tempted to report a plan rather than a result.

**One-liner:** Results are the currency; effort and intentions don't spend.

**Reach for it when:** you're about to report status upward, or tempted to
announce a plan before it exists.

## Key Takeaways

- Bosses buy outcomes, not narrative about effort or good intentions.
- Announcing an intention invites a credibility hit if it slips; deliver first,
  narrate after.
- A high say-do ratio, only committing to what you actually deliver, is a
  reputation asset built one kept promise at a time, including the small ones
  no one would notice you'd missed.
- Exception: a public announcement can be a deliberate low-key commitment
  device, used knowingly and sparingly, never the default mode.
- Results that aren't seen aren't credited. Make delivered work visible without
  narrating the effort behind it.

## Principles

- Report what's done, not what's planned. Future tense reads as inexperience
  or excuse-making.
- Silence beats a premature update. Wait until you're certain before reporting
  progress on something still in flux.
- "Don't ask permission, ask forgiveness" - operate with the authority you
  already have and report the outcome.
- Say-do ratio: only make commitments you can keep, then keep every one.
- The exception to "don't announce intentions": a public commitment can be
  used deliberately to force follow-through. That's a chosen tactic, not a
  substitute for the habit of proving through action.

## Guidelines

- Before you speak, ask: am I reporting a result, or performing effort?
- If tempted to give a progress update, ask whether you're actually certain,
  or just want to look responsive.
- Make delivered work visible without being asked; don't wait to be noticed.

## Limitations

Doesn't cover legitimate early-warning risk communication: surfacing a blocker
before it becomes a crisis is not "talk" in the sense this model warns against
(that boundary belongs to the bring-solutions pattern in
[[managing-up-bring-solutions-communicate-success]]). This model governs the
ratio of talk to delivered result, not whether to withhold status information
people genuinely need.
