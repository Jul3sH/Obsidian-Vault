# MM: Delegate The What

This is the Delegate The What mental model: what to hand off, when, and how
much of the method to specify. It exists because the instinct to hoard a
task until it's fully understood, or to over-specify how someone else should
do it, both cost more than they save. Read it before deciding to do
something yourself that a team member could own, or before briefing
delegated work in more detail than the outcome requires.

**One-liner:** Delegate early, specify the what, never the how.

**Reach for it when:** you're about to hoard a task because it feels faster
to do it yourself, or about to over-specify how someone else should do
delegated work.

## Key Takeaways

- You're judged on whether the team delivers, not on which tasks you
  personally completed; hoarding responsibility creates a single point of
  failure.
- Delegate before you feel ready. Waiting to fully understand everyone's
  workload doesn't scale.
- Define the what and what "good" looks like; let people work out the how.
- Delegating a task doesn't delegate accountability. As the senior, you
  still own the outcome.
- Under real pressure, pull in subject-matter experts rather than trying to
  solo a problem that needs more than one head.

## Principles

- Tell people what to do; don't ask. Give more responsibility, not less,
  and let them figure out how.
- Load the team up and let them prioritise and escalate their own blockers,
  rather than trying to track every task yourself.
- Delegating the task doesn't mean deferring accountability for its outcome;
  never take a back seat because someone else is formally responsible.
- When stuck under pressure, actively pull in the right people rather than
  defaulting to solo effort.

## Guidelines

- Before delegating, spend your time on the what and the definition of
  done, not on scripting the how.
- Don't wait to feel ready or fully briefed before handing work off; brief
  what you know now and course-correct.
- If a team member's work is consistently poor, escalate to their owning
  manager rather than silently absorbing the overhead yourself, and vet new
  team members more vigorously rather than assuming high caliber.
- Under a genuine crunch, two back-to-back expert calls will often unblock
  what a day of solo effort won't.

## Limitations

This model is about the mechanics of handing work off once it's already
been taken on. It doesn't cover whether to take the work on in the first
place, that evaluation happens earlier (see [[mm-evaluate-before-committing]]).
