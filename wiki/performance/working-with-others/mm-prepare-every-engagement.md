# MM: Prepare Every Engagement

This is the Preparation mental model: every interaction that matters gets a
defined purpose, and where useful a script, before it starts. It exists because
this is one of the most repeated failure patterns in Julian's own record, and
because the detail-tier article behind it (`always-prepare-for-engagements`) had
an entirely empty evidence log until this sweep. Read it before any meeting,
call, interview, or engagement with someone senior, unfamiliar, or important
enough that walking in unprepared would cost something.

**One-liner:** If you cannot state the point of the meeting before you walk in,
you are not ready for it.

**Reach for it when:** you are about to enter a meeting, call, interview, or any
engagement with someone senior or unfamiliar, and have not yet defined what you
want out of it.

## Key Takeaways

- Preparation is not generic "know your stuff"; it is defining the specific point
  of *this* engagement before it starts.
- The habit costs the same whether the stakes are high (a CIO all-hands) or low
  (a routine call); the fix is a fixed pre-engagement routine, not judging each
  case on the day.
- Preparation includes your own state, not just content: do not turn up
  under-confident, tired, or compromised.
- Feedback and checkpoints have to be forced before flow-state prep runs away
  with itself and polishes the wrong thing.
- Risky content (anecdotes, small talk, sensitive asks) needs to be scripted in
  advance, not improvised in the room.

## Principles

- **Define the point before you engage.** "What is the point of this
  engagement" is a question to answer before walking in, not during.
- **Build a template for recurring engagement types.** An unstructured workshop
  or meeting format gets reinvented, badly, every time it recurs.
- **Prioritise the prep that is usable.** Do not burn available prep time on
  hit-or-miss technical depth when reliable, always-relevant material (company
  background, a scripted pitch) is being neglected.
- **Prepare your state, not just your material.** Confidence and composure are
  part of the engagement; do not turn up running on empty.
- **Script what is risky.** Anecdotes, small talk with senior people, and
  sensitive asks need rehearsal, not improvisation.
- **Force a checkpoint before flow-state prep takes over.** Left unchecked,
  "getting ready" becomes an avoidance loop that produces polish on the wrong
  thing.

## Guidelines

- Before any engagement, however brief, write one line: what is the point, and
  what do I want out of it.
- For recurring engagement types (workshops, sales calls, interviews), build a
  light template once rather than starting from zero each time.
- Get feedback or clarify requirements early, even mid-prep, rather than
  protecting flow state until it is too late to change course.
- If not in the right state (confidence, sobriety, energy) for an important
  engagement, treat that as information: prepare harder, delay, or make an
  excuse. Do not wing it.
- Full detail: [[always-prepare-for-engagements]].

## Limitations

Over-applied, this becomes its own procrastination: endless prep instead of
engaging. The model is about defining the point and doing the usable prep, not
maximising prep time. It also does not cover engagements where genuine
spontaneity is the point (discovery conversations, for example), where prepared
openness is the goal rather than a script.
