# MM: Surface Risk Formally

This is the Surface Risk Formally mental model: the discipline of raising a risk,
resource gap, or early warning sign through an agreed, visible channel rather
than absorbing it quietly and hoping. It exists because Julian's record shows the
same pattern repeatedly: a risk went unraised, unlogged, or unescalated, and when
it landed, he or his team wore the blame instead of the people who created the
gap. Read it when you notice a risk, a resource shortfall, or an early warning
sign and are deciding whether to flag it formally or just carry it.

**One-liner:** Raise it formally, or wear it silently. There is no third option.

**Reach for it when:** you notice a risk, resource gap, or early warning sign and
are deciding whether to escalate it or absorb it quietly.

## Key Takeaways

- An agreed escalation path, set up before you need it, turns raising a problem
  into following protocol rather than making someone look bad.
- The moment alarm bells ring is the moment to escalate, not the moment the
  crisis actually lands.
- Acting on early signals beats waiting for certainty; a comfortable story (mild
  interest, "it'll probably be fine") is not the same as good odds.
- Hedge claims you cannot fully verify yet; "based on the information we
  currently have" protects you from a confident claim that turns out wrong.
- A transparent, on-record request protects you when things go wrong later; an
  informal one leaves you exposed to being blamed for someone else's gap.

## Principles

- **Agree the escalation path upfront.** Without an agreed protocol, raising an
  issue looks like making someone look bad; with one, it is just following
  process.
- **Escalate the moment alarm bells ring, not once the crisis lands.** Waiting
  to see if it resolves itself is how you end up sharing blame for something you
  saw coming.
- **Act on early signals rather than the comfort of ambiguous good news.** A
  little positive interest is not the same as favourable conditions; do not let
  it excuse inaction.
- **State claims as provisional when the facts are not fully in.** A confident
  claim you have to walk back costs more than a hedge that turns out cautious.
- **Make requests for resource or support transparent and on the record.** It is
  the record, not the request itself, that protects you when blame gets
  redistributed.

## Guidelines

- At the start of any project or new role, agree explicitly how issues and
  resource gaps get raised, and to whom.
- Log constraints and unresolved asks in a visible place (an issues log, a
  written request) rather than relying on verbal asks that leave no trace.
- Caveat conclusions you are still validating ("based on what we know so far")
  rather than stating them as settled fact.
- Treat a genuine constraint as a design problem to work around and deliver
  within, once it is logged and flagged, not before.
- Full detail: [[proactive-risk-management]].

## Limitations

Over-escalating trivial risks burns credibility and reads as exactly the
bring-me-a-problem behaviour this model is trying to avoid; escalate what is
material. Formal process protects you administratively, but it does not
substitute for actually fixing the underlying risk.
