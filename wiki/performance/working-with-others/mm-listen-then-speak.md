# MM: Listen Then Speak

This is the Listen Then Speak mental model: whether a response is actually
answering what was asked, or answering what was assumed from the first few
words. It exists because most listening failures aren't about hearing at
all, they're about pre-empting. Read it before responding in any
conversation, meeting, or interview, especially where trust is being read
from how well you listen.

**One-liner:** Hear all of it, prove you heard it, then answer what was
asked.

**Reach for it when:** you're about to respond, especially somewhere status
or trust is being read from how you listen.

## Key Takeaways

- Most listening failures are pre-empting: deciding the answer from the
  first few words, then composing a reply while the other person is still
  talking.
- Silence after a question is thinking time to protect, not a
  misunderstanding to fill.
- Making a conversation about yourself, sharing your own concerns, latching
  onto your own experience, reads as not listening even when you technically
  heard every word.
- Asking for clarification is never a weakness; guessing and answering the
  wrong question is the actual risk.
- Listening, not talking, is what people remember and what reads as wisdom.

## Principles

- Listen to the whole question before formulating a response; answer the
  question actually asked, not the one you expected.
- Ask a clear question, then stop talking. Give people time to respond.
- Secure full attention before making a key point; don't compete with
  someone's distraction or dual-screen setup.
- If unsure what was meant, clarify, don't guess.
- Get curious about the other person; the people remembered as great are the
  ones who wanted to know about others.

## Guidelines

- Before replying, check: am I answering what they asked, or what I assumed
  they'd ask?
- If you notice yourself elaborating on your own question, stop. That's
  pre-empting their answer.
- In an important exchange, secure attention explicitly before the key point
  rather than assuming you have it.
- Once a point has landed, stop repeating it looking for more visible
  agreement; document it and move on.

## Limitations

This is about listening inside a live exchange. It doesn't cover verifying a
claim after the fact, that's [[never-react-walk-away-and-be-calculated]]'s
territory.
