# MM: Choose Winnable Battles

This is the Choose Winnable Battles mental model: whether you're right is not the
test, whether you can win is. Read it before engaging in an office-politics
fight, a public argument, or backing a manager or sponsor with your time and
credibility. Full context and evidence are in [[influencing-and-selling-ideas]].

**One-liner:** A battle you can't win costs credibility even when you're right.

**Reach for it when:** you're tempted to fight a losing battle, argue in public,
or invest in a weak sponsor.

## Key Takeaways

- Ask what you realistically expect to achieve before engaging, not whether
  you're right.
- Fighting in public, or backing a weak sponsor, loses regardless of the merits.
- Get the right people onside to carry the message instead of fighting it
  yourself.

## Principles

- **Winnability is the test, not being right.** Office politics isn't about who's
  correct; assess it tactically before engaging.
- **A public argument makes you look worse**, whatever the facts, because the
  argument itself is what gets remembered.
- **Back people with real power.** Propping up a weak manager with your time is
  wasted investment that evaporates the moment they're replaced.

## Guidelines

- Before engaging, ask: what do I realistically expect to achieve here?
- If it's a losing battle, don't start it. Calm down, review, be tactical
  instead.
- Move disputes out of public view; recruit an ally to carry the message rather
  than confronting directly.

## Limitations

Doesn't apply when the stakes require taking the loss on principle, integrity or
safety issues aren't calculable this way. This model is about managing
credibility, not a rule that every fight must be winnable to be worth having.
