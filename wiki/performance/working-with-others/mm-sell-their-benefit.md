# MM: Sell Their Benefit

This is the Sell Their Benefit mental model: the reachable rule that people act on
what's in it for them, not on how good your idea is. Read it before pitching an
idea, making an ask, or walking into an interview or a sales conversation. The
evidence and full context are in [[influencing-and-selling-ideas]].

**One-liner:** People don't buy your idea, they buy what it does for them.

**Reach for it when:** you are about to pitch, ask, or persuade someone above or
beside you.

## Key Takeaways

- Filter every pitch through their objectives, not yours.
- Establish what they actually need before offering your solution.
- Get into their world: read the situation and listen before you talk about
  yourself.

## Principles

- **Always ask: am I selling this in terms of what's in it for them?** Bosses and
  stakeholders filter everything through their own objectives.
- **Establish the need before the solution.** Ask what they're trying to achieve,
  then answer to that, don't lead with whatever you happen to be most confident
  talking about.
- **Reading the person and the situation surfaces the real lever.** Their
  constraints, their governance, their agenda, faster than your own pitch does.

## Guidelines

- Before any pitch, ask explicitly: is this framed as their benefit, not mine?
- In interviews or asks, invite the other party to state the need first, then
  target your answer to it.
- Watch for defaulting to your own comfort zone instead of their stated need,
  that's underselling, not confidence.

## Limitations

Doesn't help when there's genuinely no shared interest, or you can't see what the
other person wants. Then you're guessing at their benefit, not selling to it, and
the model gives you nothing to work with.
