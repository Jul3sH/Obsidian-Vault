# MM: Clarify Before Starting

This is the Clarify Before Starting mental model: whether to raise the
clarifying question now or absorb the ambiguity and pay for it later in
rework. It exists because the instinct to just get going, especially under
pressure or when new, consistently loses to a five-minute question asked
first. Read it before starting any deliverable, workshop, or process whose
format or objective hasn't been explicitly confirmed.

**One-liner:** The clarifying question is always cheaper than the rework.

**Reach for it when:** you're about to start building something and the
brief, format, or objective hasn't been explicitly confirmed.

## Key Takeaways

- Ambiguity at the start becomes scope creep and blame later; you own the
  output regardless of how vague the brief was.
- Get the format and objectives pre-approved before starting, not after
  producing something that has to be reworked.
- If requirements can't be established, that itself is a risk to flag and
  document, not a gap to quietly fill with assumptions.
- Escalating for guidance early is faster than fixing a mess caused by
  self-resolving ambiguity to avoid bothering a busy boss.
- When a correction to scope lands, get it confirmed in writing, then manage
  the first iteration as a prototype, not a finished deliverable.

## Principles

- Before committing to any deliverable, define: objectives, measurable
  outcomes, constraints, assumptions and dependencies.
- Don't wait for requirements to be handed to you; extract them. You own the
  process of establishing the outcome even when the customer owns the outcome
  itself.
- Insist on seeing the correct process demonstrated before proceeding, rather
  than proceeding blind to avoid seeming difficult.
- Being new is not a reason to guess. It's a reason to ask more questions,
  not fewer.

## Guidelines

- Get format and output pre-approved before starting build.
- If a correction to scope arrives, get it confirmed in writing, then
  prototype the first iteration rather than build the whole thing to the new
  spec blind.
- When you don't understand what's wanted, run a proper requirements
  workshop rather than proceed on assumption.
- Escalate ambiguity rather than resolve it silently to avoid bothering a
  busy boss.

## Limitations

Applies before commitment, not as a stalling tactic. Once objectives are
genuinely clear, further clarifying questions are procrastination dressed as
diligence (see [[beating-procrastination-face-fear-get-started]]).
