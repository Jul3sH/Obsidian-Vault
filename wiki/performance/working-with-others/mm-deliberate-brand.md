# MM: Deliberate Brand

This is the Deliberate Brand mental model: how you come across (succinct or
rambling, composed or eager, decisive or narrating) is a choice to design in
advance, not a mood that happens to you in the room. It exists because Julian's
record shows the same brand slip repeatedly costing him: over-explaining,
showboating, narrating preferences out loud, an unscripted joke. Read it before
communicating with senior or unfamiliar people, or when you notice your framing
or tone is running on autopilot rather than a deliberate choice.

**One-liner:** How you come across is a decision, not a mood.

**Reach for it when:** you notice your framing, tone, or persona is running on
autopilot, especially with senior or unfamiliar people.

## Key Takeaways

- Communicate succinctly; people extract their own "gist" and get it wrong,
  especially across cultures.
- Quiet composure reads as experience; enthusiasm and showboating read as
  inexperience.
- Announce decisions, not preferences; narrating what you are thinking about,
  rather than acting, reads as strategising rather than leading.
- A personal brand has to be stated clearly, not left implicit. If you cannot
  say your own unique combination in one line, nobody else can repeat it for you.
- Composure under a perceived slight beats defensiveness; over-explaining to
  dispel a perception usually confirms it instead.

## Principles

- **Communicate key points succinctly.** Do not rely on people extracting the
  gist correctly, especially across languages and cultures.
- **Quiet composure beats enthusiasm at any level of seniority.** Puppy-dog
  keenness reads as inexperience; people at this level want the reassurance that
  comes with experience.
- **Announce decisions, not preferences.** Talking through options out loud
  reads as strategising, not leadership; be comfortable with silence, decide,
  then act.
- **State your unique combination clearly.** If your brand is hard to
  articulate, it is hard for anyone else to grasp or repeat, and wanting to be
  respected for your work is worth naming honestly rather than leaving implicit.
- **Run sensitive framing past someone else first.** How something lands often
  is not obvious from the inside.
- **Do not say things to impress.** Talking to look good creates compromising
  positions later that a moment of composure would have avoided.
- **Respond slowly, not defensively.** A flood of explanation to dispel a
  perception reads as confirming it; calm and assured lands better than a
  rebuttal.
- **Read the room before joking.** A joke without established rapport lands as
  risk, not charm.

## Guidelines

- Before speaking in a cross-cultural or senior setting, compress the point to
  its shortest form; do not trust the listener to extract it.
- When tempted to narrate a preference or a train of thought out loud, ask
  whether the honest move is just to decide and act instead.
- Write your own one-line brand statement and test whether someone else could
  repeat it back accurately.
- When challenged on a perception ("you're all over the place"), slow down
  rather than flooding the room with explanation.
- Full detail: [[communication-style-and-personal-brand]].

## Limitations

Composure and silence can tip into opacity; people still need enough information
to do their jobs, and the model is about deliberate framing, not withholding. It
also does not cover genuine collaborative brainstorming, where thinking out loud
is the point rather than a leadership failure.
