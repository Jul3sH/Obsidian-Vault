# MM: Share The Credit

This is the Share The Credit mental model: framing a win as joint work while it
is happening, before a manager who is structurally positioned to take the credit
gets to frame it as solo. It exists because this dynamic (a manager taking credit
for Julian's resolution of an issue) was a genuine, previously uncaptured lesson
with no home in either detail-tier article. Read it when you have resolved
something, or are about to deliver a win, that is about to be reported upward by
someone above you.

**One-liner:** Frame the win as joint before your manager frames it as solo.

**Reach for it when:** you are resolving something, or about to deliver a win,
that a manager above you is positioned to report upward as his own.

## Key Takeaways

- A manager who can take credit for your work will; that is a structural
  incentive of the relationship, not necessarily malice.
- He is not going to promote you over himself, but he will happily be seen as
  leading a team that works well together.
- Framing a resolution as "we solved this together" while it is happening, not
  after, makes him more responsive, not less.
- The goal is not confrontation or credit-chasing; it is pre-empting the framing
  before someone else sets it.

## Principles

- **Assume the credit will be taken if you leave the framing open.** This is a
  structural fact of the manager relationship, not a reason for paranoia.
- **Frame the work as joint while it is happening, not after.** Reinforcing "we
  are solving this together" in the moment changes how responsive the manager
  is, and changes what story he is able to tell afterward.
- **Play to what he actually wants.** He will not promote you above himself, but
  he will take credit for leading a collaborative team; give him that framing
  and you get named inside it, rather than erased from it.

## Guidelines

- When resolving an issue that will get reported upward, use "we" language with
  your manager while it is in progress, not just after the fact.
- Do not expect to be credited solo instead of him; expect to be credited as
  part of a team he can point to, and treat that as the realistic win available.
- Watch for this pattern recurring with any manager, not just one; it is the
  incentive of the role, not the person.

## Limitations

This model is built from a single MISSED lesson with no detail-tier article
behind it yet; treat it as directional rather than exhaustively tested, and flag
it for expansion if more evidence surfaces. It also does not help when a manager
is actively hostile rather than credit-seeking: that is a different dynamic (see
[[mm-choose-winnable-battles]] or [[mm-surface-risk-formally]] for adversarial
situations).
