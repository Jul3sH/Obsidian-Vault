# MM: Proximity Is Infrastructure

This is the Proximity mental model: the discipline of building relationships and
physical/organisational nearness to the people who matter before you need
anything from them. It exists because Julian's own record shows the same failure
repeating across three employers: being physically or socially distant from the
decision maker, watching someone else fill the gap, then rebuilding from behind.
Read it when starting somewhere new, or when deciding where to sit, who to align
with, or which relationship needs investment right now rather than later.

**One-liner:** Proximity is a career asset, and the window to build it closes fast.

**Reach for it when:** you are starting a new role, team, or project and deciding
where to sit, who to align with, or which relationship needs investing in before
you need anything from it.

## Key Takeaways

- Physical proximity and regular interaction are the prerequisite for being seen
  as a valuable resource, not a side effect of already being one.
- The window to establish proximity closes fast; someone else fills it while you
  are still deciding to act.
- Cultivating the right relationship with the right person at the right time is a
  real, promotable skill, not just office politics.
- A soured relationship you have not invested in can silently block something you
  want later; a single low-investment repair is often enough to fix it.
- Choose early alliances deliberately. Who you are seen with in the first weeks
  sets a ceiling you live inside later.

## Principles

- **Sit near the decision maker.** Distance creates room for facts to get
  distorted before they reach you; presence closes that gap.
- **Build the relationship before you need it.** Waiting until there is a
  specific ask makes it transactional, and late.
- **Proximity is a prerequisite, not a reward.** You do not get pulled into the
  inner circle because you are valuable; you become visibly valuable because you
  are already in the room.
- **A soured relationship is a live risk, not a closed chapter.** Repair it before
  it quietly blocks something you want.
- **Choose early alliances deliberately.** Never form early bonds with people held
  in low regard, and never give anyone ammunition to use against you later.

## Guidelines

- On starting anywhere new, identify the two or three people who actually decide
  things, and engineer proximity to them (desk, meetings, informal time) in the
  first weeks, not once you need something from them.
- Treat a cooling relationship as a task, not a mood. A single, low-investment
  gesture (a call, genuinely hearing them out) is often enough to turn it around.
- Do not wait to be invited in. If someone else is visibly becoming the "go to"
  person in your place, that is the signal to act, not to keep observing.
- Full detail and more examples: [[internal-networking-and-relationship-building]].

## Limitations

Proximity does not substitute for delivery; it is a multiplier on visible work,
not a replacement for it. Overinvesting in relationships with people who cannot
actually help you, or who are held in low regard, burns the same time budget for
no return. Proximity has to be aimed, not scattered.
