# MM: Assume It Gets Repeated

This is the Assume It Gets Repeated mental model: the audience-awareness half of
discretion, distinct from [[mm-never-bitch]] which covers the act of complaining
itself. This model is about who's listening: assume it gets forwarded, overheard,
or repeated, and write and speak accordingly. Full context and evidence are in
[[never-bitch-whinge-or-gossip]].

**One-liner:** Say and write only what survives being forwarded.

**Reach for it when:** you're about to confide, vent, joke, or put something in
writing that would embarrass you if it reached the person it's about.

## Key Takeaways

- Assume anyone within earshot, or the person you're confiding in, will repeat
  it.
- Emails get forwarded; write only what you'd be fine having the subject read.
- An unusually attentive listener, a salesperson especially, may be listening as
  a tactic, not out of rapport.

## Principles

- **People always talk.** If you wouldn't want something repeated verbatim to the
  person it's about, don't say it.
- **Never put anything in writing you wouldn't want forwarded.** Sensitive
  content in an email is one misdirected click from the wrong inbox.
- **An attentive listener may have their own agenda.** That includes
  salespeople, whose job is literally to listen.
- **The near-miss rule**: assume someone who matters is always within earshot.

## Guidelines

- Before confiding or venting, run the forwarding test: would this survive being
  read by its subject, or forwarded to your boss?
- Keep emails to the minimum content needed; sensitive context is a liability
  the moment it leaves your control.
- Treat an unusually attentive listener as a prompt for caution, not an
  invitation to keep going.

## Limitations

This is a discretion filter, not a ban on raising real problems with people who
can act on them, see [[mm-bring-solutions]] for the distinction between
disclosure and escalation.
