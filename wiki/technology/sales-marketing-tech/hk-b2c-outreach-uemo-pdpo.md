# Hong Kong B2C Property-Investment Outreach: UEMO and PDPO Rules

> *Compliance map for a campaign targeting identifiable individuals with property-investment information or offers by email, SMS/MMS, fax, or pre-recorded telephone message, where the message has a Hong Kong link. Covers two regimes only: **UEMO** (how you send) and **PDPO** (the data).*

> [!warning] Status and provenance
> Supplied by Julian on 2026-08-05, not independently verified against the statutes. The indented quotations are presented as verbatim extracts from official OFCA or PCPD guidance and are **not** a substitute for the statutory text. Encoding artefacts in the source have been repaired; wording is otherwise unchanged. **This is regulatory research, not legal advice.**

**Used by:** [[vanguard-outreach-go-no-go]] - the 2h spike deciding whether Julian participates in Adam's campaign. Three of its load-bearing assumptions are tested against sections of this document (§4.9, the scope note, and §2.3).

## Key Takeaways

- **Two independent regimes.** UEMO governs *how* you send; PDPO governs *the data*. Satisfying UEMO does not make a campaign lawful under PDPO.
- **UEMO is opt-out for email. PDPO is opt-in for named individuals.** PDPO is therefore the binding constraint on any B2C campaign addressed to a named person.
- **A bought, scraped, enriched, or merely "public" list does not clear PDPO.** The obligation follows the data user regardless of where the data came from (§2.4).
- **Silence is not consent** (§3.2). A first unsolicited email cannot be the mechanism for obtaining consent to send it.
- **Purpose limitation bites hard** (§2.3). Contacts collected for one purpose cannot be reused to market an unrelated one.
- **Agents, contractors and officers are exposed too**, not just the principal (§4.9). This is the section bearing on Julian's personal position.
- **Scope gap:** this document explicitly excludes SFC rules, AML, Trade Descriptions, estate-agency rules, and overseas law. Assisted-living and commercial property schemes are the most likely to fall under SFC regulation.

**Important scope note (from the source).** "Property investment" is a consumer-facing marketing subject. A message addressed to a named individual is direct marketing for PDPO purposes. This document does not cover other potentially applicable regimes, for example SFC rules if the offer is a regulated investment product, anti-money-laundering rules, the Trade Descriptions Ordinance, estate-agency rules, or overseas laws where the recipient is outside Hong Kong.

## 1. Threshold: when each regime applies

### 1.1 UEMO: commercial electronic message with a Hong Kong link

**Rule.** UEMO applies where the communication is a commercial electronic message and has the required Hong Kong connection. Property-investment promotion normally satisfies the commercial-purpose test.

> "The UEMO regulates the sending of 'commercial electronic messages' with a 'Hong Kong link'."

> "'Commercial electronic messages' are defined as electronic messages with purposes, or one of the purposes of, which is, among others, to offer or supply goods, services, facilities, land, business opportunity, or advertise or promote a supplier of goods, services, facilities, land, business opportunity, etc, in the course of or in the furtherance of any business (section 2 of the UEMO)."

> "Any form of electronic messages sent over a public telecommunications service to an electronic address is regulated under the UEMO, including email, fax, SMS/MMS, pre-recorded voice/video messages."

> "In general, a commercial electronic message has a Hong Kong link if the message: (a) originates in Hong Kong; (b) is sent to Hong Kong; or (c) is sent to a Hong Kong telephone or fax number."

**Statutory anchors:** UEMO ss. 2-4; Schedule 1 (exemptions).
**Operational rule:** Treat every promotional email, SMS, fax, or automated/pre-recorded call to a Hong Kong individual as in scope unless counsel confirms a Schedule 1 exemption.

### 1.2 PDPO: personal data and direct marketing

**Rule.** The PDPO applies where data relates to a living identifiable individual and the campaign uses it for direct marketing. A named person's email, phone, residential address, name, investment profile, or lead score is generally personal data.

> "Personal Data means information which relates to a living individual and can be used to identify that individual."

> "'Direct marketing means' is further defined to mean: (a) sending information or goods, addressed to specific persons by name, by mail, fax, electronic mail or other means of communication; or (b) making telephone calls to specific persons."

> "A marketing SMS sent to the mobile phone number of a named individual is considered a form of direct marketing."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO s. 2 definitions, s. 35A, Part 6A; Schedule 1 DPPs.
**Operational rule:** Do not assume that public availability of a person's contact details permits marketing use. The PDPO direct-marketing controls apply even when the details came from a third party.

## 2. PDPO: data collection and lead-list creation

### 2.1 Lawful, fair, necessary, and non-excessive collection

**Rule.** Collect only data necessary for a lawful purpose directly related to the promoter's function or activity. Do not use deceptive collection, collect excessive profiling data, or make irrelevant data mandatory.

> "DPP 1(1) in Schedule 1 to the Ordinance provides that only necessary, adequate but not excessive personal data is to be collected by a data user for a lawful purpose directly related to its function or activity."

> "DPP 1(2) requires that personal data shall be collected by means which are lawful and fair in the circumstances of the case."

> "A data user should not use deceptive or misleading means to collect personal data for direct marketing."

> "Basically, the name and contact information of a customer should suffice for the purpose of direct marketing."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO Schedule 1, DPP1(1)-(3).
**Campaign implication:** Collecting or inferring wealth, property holdings, affordability, family circumstances, or investment appetite requires a demonstrable necessity and a clear collection notice. For ordinary outreach, it is difficult to justify much beyond basic contact data.

### 2.2 Collection notice / PICS

**Rule.** On or before collection, provide the required information about whether providing data is obligatory or voluntary, the purpose, and transferee classes. If direct marketing is intended, Part 6A's more specific notice must be given.

> "DPP 1(3) requires a data user to take all reasonably practicable steps to inform the data subject on or before the collection of his personal data whether it is voluntary or obligatory for him to supply the data, the purpose of use of the data and the classes of persons to whom the data may be transferred."

> "If a data user intends to use or provide personal data for use in direct marketing, it must inform the data subjects that it intends to so use or provide the data and that the data may not be so used or provided unless it receives the data subject's consent."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO Schedule 1, DPP1(3); ss. 35C and 35J.
**Campaign implication:** A website lead form or downloadable guide must have a clear privacy/collection statement at the point of collection. A generic statement such as "we may contact you with offers" is unsafe for property-investment marketing.

### 2.3 Purpose limitation and new-purpose use

> [!important] This section answers assumption 3 in [[vanguard-outreach-go-no-go]]
> Contacts originating in Adam's partner's whisky-investment business cannot simply be reused to market UK property.

**Rule.** Do not repurpose data for property-investment promotion where it was collected for another purpose unless the required consent is in place.

> "DPP3 prohibits the use of personal data for any new purpose which is not or is unrelated to the original purpose when collecting the data, unless with the data subject's express and voluntary consent."

> "A new purpose means a purpose other than the purpose for which the data was to be used at the time of the collection or a directly related purpose."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO Schedule 1, DPP3; s. 2(3).
**Campaign implication:** A customer list collected for property management, a webinar, mortgage administration, a survey, or a non-investment service cannot simply be reused to send investment offers.

### 2.4 Data from brokers, partners, and purchased lists

**Rule.** The campaign operator still has Part 6A obligations when data comes from someone else; a supplier's assurance is not a substitute for the operator's compliance.

> "The obligation on the data user applies irrespective of whether the personal data is collected from the data subject by the data user."

> "Hence, even if a data user has collected the personal data of a data subject from a third party, it is still required to take the specified actions and obtain the necessary consent from the data subject before using the personal data for direct marketing."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO ss. 35C-35E; where data is passed onward, ss. 35J-35K.
**Campaign implication:** Do not buy, rent, scrape, or accept an individual-contact list for a B2C property campaign unless there is documentary evidence of the exact consent/notice, exact data categories, and exact marketing-subject class that permits the intended use.

## 3. PDPO: required direct-marketing notice and consent

### 3.1 Information that must be given before own use

**Rule.** Before using personal data for direct marketing, tell the person the intended use, that consent is required, the data types, marketing-subject classes, and a free response channel.

> "The following information must be included in the notice to the data subject: (a) The data user intends to use the personal data of the data subject for direct marketing; (b) The data user may not so use the data unless the data user has received the data subject's consent to the intended use; (c) The kinds of personal data to be used; (d) The classes of marketing subjects in relation to which the data is to be used; and (e) The response channel through which the data subject may, without charge by the data user, communicate the data subject's consent to the intended use."

> "Vague and loose terms ... will fall short of meeting the requirement."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO s. 35C.
**Campaign implication:** Describe the offer narrowly and intelligibly, for example "information and invitations concerning residential property investment opportunities and associated property-investment advisory services." Do not use "products and services of us, affiliates, partners and selected third parties." The correct class will require legal review if the campaign involves securities, funds, fractional interests, property syndicates, lending, or insurance.

### 3.2 Consent standard: no silence, no implied consent

**Rule.** Do not send because the person failed to object, failed to reply, gave a business card, viewed content, or is publicly listed. Consent or an explicit indication of no objection is required after the prescribed information has been supplied.

> "Consent is defined broadly to cover 'an indication of no objection to the use or provision'."

> "Hence, consent cannot be inferred from the data subject's non-response. In other words, silence does not constitute consent."

> "No response is received from the data subject to a direct marketing solicitation by mail or electronic means." [Listed by PCPD as an example of invalid consent.]

> "Even though a data user has taken steps to inform or notify the data subject as described above, the data user should not use personal data in direct marketing unless the data subject's consent to the intended use was received."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO ss. 35A, 35C-35E.
**Campaign implication:** A first unsolicited, named property-investment email cannot itself be used as the mechanism to obtain consent for later property-investment messages if it already uses the recipient's personal data for direct marketing. Obtain consent before the marketing use, through a compliant collection/permission process.

### 3.3 Consistency with the permission granted

**Rule.** Only use the data types and marketing-subject classes for which consent was obtained.

> "The use of the personal data in direct marketing must be consistent with the data subject's consent."

> "The personal data to be used falls within the permitted kind of personal data; and ... the marketing subject in relation to which the data is used falls within the permitted class of marketing subjects."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO s. 35E(1)(c), s. 35E(2).
**Campaign implication:** Consent for "property news" is not necessarily consent to market a property-investment scheme, real-estate financing, offshore investment property, or a third party's investment service.

### 3.4 Oral consent: written confirmation within 14 days

**Rule.** Oral consent for the organisation's own use can be valid, but it needs written confirmation within 14 days, with prescribed content.

> "Where an oral consent is obtained from a data subject, a written confirmation must be sent to the data subject not later than 14 days after the oral consent is obtained."

> "The following information should be included in the written confirmation ... (a) The date of receipt of the consent; (b) The permitted kind of personal data; and (c) The permitted class of marketing subjects."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO s. 35E(1)(b).
**Campaign implication:** Record the call lawfully, retain the consent evidence, send confirmation within the deadline, and do not rely on an oral "sounds interesting" response as permission for broad ongoing campaigns.

### 3.5 Suppression / cessation of direct marketing

**Rule.** At first use, notify the person of their right to require cessation without charge. Stop when asked.

> "The data user must, when using the data subject's personal data for the first time in direct marketing, notify the data subject of his/her right to request the data user to cease to so use the data, without charge to the data subject."

> "This obligation applies irrespective of whether the personal data is obtained directly from a data subject or from other sources."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO s. 35F.
**Campaign implication:** Maintain a central, durable suppression list. Apply a request across all campaign tools, domains, CRM exports, outsourced agencies, and future lists controlled by the promoter.

### 3.6 Passing the list to another person / joint campaign

**Rule.** A separate and stricter regime applies if personal data is provided to another person for that person's direct marketing. Consent must be in writing and the prescribed notice must identify the transferee classes, marketing classes, data types, and whether the transfer is for gain.

> "Where the consent to be sought from a data subject is for the provision of his/her personal data to another person for use in direct marketing, a data user can only elect a response channel which enables the data subject's consent to be made in writing."

> "If the data user intends to transfer the data to a third party for direct marketing, he/she should inform the data subject of such intention, the classes of transferees, the classes of marketing subjects to be involved and the fact that the transfer is for a gain, etc."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO ss. 35J-35M.
**Campaign implication:** If a lead generator, broker, property developer, investment adviser, or outreach contractor is an independent marketer who may use leads for its own purposes, this is likely a "provision" issue, not merely processor outsourcing. Obtain legal advice and written consent before the transfer.

### 3.7 Data security, retention, transparency, access and correction

**Rule.** Secure the prospect data, retain it only as long as necessary, maintain transparent policies, and handle access/correction rights.

> "DPP2 requires data users to take all practicable steps to ensure that personal data is accurate and is not kept longer than is necessary for the fulfillment of the purpose for which the data is used."

> "DPP4 requires that data users take all practicable steps to protect the personal data they hold against unauthorised or accidental access, processing, erasure, loss or use."

> "DPP5 obliges data users to take all practicable steps to ensure openness of their personal data policies and practices, the kind of personal data held and the main purposes for holding it."

> "DPP6 provides data subjects with the right to request access to and correction of their own personal data."

**Statutory anchors:** PDPO Schedule 1, DPP2, DPP4-DPP6; s. 26 (erasure).
**Campaign implication:** A platform provider, agency, and campaign operator need a documented processing arrangement, role-based access, deletion/retention schedule, breach escalation plan, and a process for data-access/correction requests.

## 4. UEMO: email, SMS, fax and pre-recorded-message rules

### 4.1 Accurate sender identification

**Rule.** Every in-scope commercial electronic message needs the required sender information. For email, name, business address, contact telephone number, and contact email address are required.

> "In the case of email, the required sender information includes the sender's name, address, contact telephone number and contact email address."

> "Normally 'sender' of a commercial electronic message refers to the individual/organisation that promotes the product/service and not the individual/organisation which merely sends the message."

> "The sender information should be reasonably likely to be valid for at least 30 days after a message is sent."

**Statutory anchors:** UEMO s. 8; UEMR s. 5.
**Campaign implication:** Identify the business promoting the property-investment opportunity and, where relevant, the organisation authorising the message, not merely a generic technical mail-sending brand. Do not use a mailbox-only address or a postal box in place of the usual business address.

### 4.2 Functional, free unsubscribe facility and statement

**Rule.** Include a clear unsubscribe facility, explain it in the message, keep it available for at least 30 days, make it free to use, and make it convenient.

> "The sender of the commercial electronic message is required to provide unsubscribe facility to enable the recipient to submit unsubscribe requests."

> "Any unsubscribe facility should: (a) be capable of receiving unsubscribe requests at all times during a period of at least 30 days after the message is sent ... (b) be free of any usage charge to the recipient ... (c) be convenient to use, readily available and does not contain in itself any information promoting or advertising products, services, facilities, organisation, etc."

> "The unsubscribe facility should allow recipients to unsubscribe from all commercial electronic messages to be sent by the sender."

**Statutory anchors:** UEMO s. 9; UEMR s. 9; UEMR requirements on unsubscribe statements.
**Campaign implication:** For email, include at least one usable email address, webpage, or web address for unsubscribing. Make it visible in the email body. A login requirement, charge, sales call, or promotional landing page is unsuitable.

### 4.3 Honour unsubscribe requests: 10 working days; retain records for 3 years

**Rule.** Stop further messages to that address within 10 working days after the request was sent and retain the request record for at least three years.

> "Senders should update their database of address lists as soon as such unsubscribe requests are received in order to ensure that an unsubscribe request could take effect within 10 working days from the day on which it is sent."

> "The sender should retain a record of unsubscribe requests ... for at least 3 years after their receipt."

**Statutory anchors:** UEMO ss. 9-10.
**Campaign implication:** Suppression must operate at the sender/authorising organisation level, not merely within one sending tool or one outreach domain. Keep evidence of the time received, source, action taken, and all downstream systems suppressed.

### 4.4 Do-not-call registers: applicable channels

**Rule.** The UEMO Do-not-call registers cover fax, SMS/MMS, and pre-recorded telephone messages, not ordinary email. Do not send to registered telephone/fax numbers after the statutory period unless the registered user has consented.

> "Three DNCs have been set up by CA: DNC (Fax); DNC (Short Message) -- for Short Messaging Service (SMS) and Multimedia Messaging Service (MMS) messages; and DNC (Pre-recorded Telephone Message) -- for pre-recorded voice, sound, video and image messages."

> "The senders should not send further commercial electronic messages to the telephone / fax numbers listed on the DNCs that has been listed on the DNC for 10 working days or more, unless they obtain specific consents from the registered user of the telephone / fax number."

**Statutory anchors:** UEMO ss. 11-12 and related provisions.
**Campaign implication:** Before SMS, fax, or pre-recorded campaigns, subscribe to, download, and purge against the relevant DNC register. This does not replace PDPO consent requirements for named B2C marketing.

### 4.5 No misleading subject headings; no concealed caller ID

**Rule.** Do not use misleading email subject headings. For fax/telephone-originated messages, do not conceal caller-line identification.

> "Don't: use misleading subject heading for an e-mail message [or] conceal or withhold the caller line identification information when sending message from a fax or telephone number."

**Statutory anchors:** UEMO ss. 13-14.
**Campaign implication:** The email subject must accurately convey the commercial property-investment nature of the message. Avoid clickbait such as "Your property account update" or fake-reply formats where no prior relationship/account exists.

### 4.6 No harvesting, harvested lists, or generated addresses

**Rule.** Do not obtain or use addresses through prohibited harvesting/generation techniques without the legally required consent.

> "If the address list is obtained or leased from a third party, the sender should ensure that it is not a harvested-address list ... and refrain from using any harvested-address list."

> "Sender must not use automated means to generate an electronic address to which a commercial electronic message is sent."

> "It is an offence to ... supply, acquire or use address-harvesting software or harvested-address lists for sending commercial electronic messages without the recipients' consent."

**Statutory anchors:** UEMO ss. 15-19.
**Campaign implication:** Do not scrape personal emails or use tools/lists that collect addresses from webpages/social networks, and do not run "dictionary attack" address-generation campaigns. Independently document list provenance.

### 4.7 No false headers, false registration, open relays/proxies, or unauthorised devices

**Rule.** UEMO prohibits technical methods that disguise source or exploit infrastructure for multiple commercial messages.

> "Sender must not use open relay/proxy to relay or retransmit multiple commercial electronic messages."

> "Sender must not access a telecommunications device without authorization to send multiple commercial electronic messages."

> "Sender must not falsify header information in multiple commercial electronic messages and send such messages."

> "The UEMO also prohibits ... registration for 5 or more email addresses or 2 or more domain names using falsified information to send multiple electronic messages."

**Statutory anchors:** UEMO ss. 20, 22-26.
**Campaign implication:** Domains and mailboxes must be truthfully registered; message headers and identity must not be falsified; and the campaign must not use unauthorised relays, compromised accounts, or devices.

### 4.8 Use unsubscribe/DNC data only for compliance

**Rule.** Do not repurpose unsubscribe or DNC data for marketing or other unrelated use.

> "The information obtained from unsubscribe requests or DNCs should only be used for purposes for compliance with the statutory requirements in the UEMO and UEMR."

**Statutory anchors:** UEMO s. 58.
**Campaign implication:** Store suppression data separately and use it only to prevent messages; do not profile it, share it for promotion, or use it to target individuals through another channel.

### 4.9 Principal, contractor, employee and officer exposure

> [!important] This section answers assumption 1 in [[vanguard-outreach-go-no-go]]
> The phrase **"as well as by him"** in the employee limb is the reason "pure execution doesn't implicate me" cannot be assumed. Read this before anything else in the spike.

**Rule.** Outsourcing does not eliminate exposure. The principal may be treated as having done its contractor's acts, and the contractor/employee has potential exposure too.

> "Any act done or conduct engaged in by an agent or the outsourced service provider will be treated as done or engaged in by the principal."

> "Any act done or conduct engaged in the employee shall be treated as done or engaged in by his employer as well as by him."

> "Any director of the company, partner in the partnership, or officer in an organisation who was responsible for the internal management of the organisation shall be presumed also to have done the act unless there is evidence showing that he did not authorize the act."

**Statutory anchors:** UEMO ss. 59-60.
**Campaign implication:** The property business, campaign owner, data owner, outreach provider, platform operator, and responsible officers need written controls. The principal's statutory defence depends on proving practicable steps to prevent the breach.

## 5. Enforcement and risk summary

### 5.1 PDPO risk

For a named individual receiving property-investment promotion, PDPO Part 6A is the central risk. The first marketing contact itself will normally be prohibited if the promoter is using the recipient's personal data without first giving the prescribed notice and obtaining valid consent or no-objection. This remains true when the contact details were bought, obtained from a broker, or sourced by another party.

Official PCPD summary:

> "Failure to comply with the direct marketing requirements is an offence and can result in a fine of $500,000 and imprisonment for 3 years, or up to a fine of $1,000,000 and imprisonment for 5 years if data was provided to a third party for gain."

**Practical risk level: High.** Property-investment promotion is consumer-directed and can involve financial profiling and third-party lead sharing. These facts heighten the importance of consent records, narrow marketing-subject descriptions, and verified data provenance.

### 5.2 UEMO risk

UEMO does not generally require prior consent for an unsolicited commercial **email**; its model is sender identification, unsubscribe, honouring unsubscribes, and restrictions on abusive methods. However, this does not overcome PDPO, which independently requires consent for the use of a named individual's personal data in direct marketing.

Official OFCA summary:

> "Under an opt-out regime, a sender may send out unsolicited messages to electronic addresses if he/she follows the rules of sending of commercial electronic messages."

For Part 2 sending-rule breaches, OFCA may issue an enforcement notice. Failure to comply is an offence; OFCA's industry guide states first-conviction exposure is currently a level-6 fine (HK$100,000), later conviction up to HK$500,000, plus daily penalties for continuing offences. Prohibited harvesting, falsification, hacking, and other Part 3/4 conduct can attract materially heavier penalties.

**Practical risk level: Medium to high.** The usual failures are incomplete sender details, misleading identity/subject lines, broken unsubscribe flows, failure to suppress inside 10 working days, and use of harvested or enriched contact lists.

### 5.3 Go/no-go conclusion

**Do not launch a named B2C property-investment cold-email campaign to Hong Kong individuals from a scraped, purchased, enriched, or merely "public" list.** UEMO mechanics alone do not make it lawful. The campaign should launch only after the promoter can demonstrate, per individual:

1. lawful collection and documented source/provenance;
2. a compliant PDPO direct-marketing notice specifying the relevant property-investment marketing class;
3. valid, recorded consent or explicit indication of no objection for the intended use;
4. written consent where personal data is being passed to another independent marketer;
5. sender identity, contact details, and a working unsubscribe mechanism in every UEMO message;
6. central suppression controls, DNC screening for SMS/fax/pre-recorded channels, security, retention, and processor/agency controls.
